The new collection didn't sell: 880 sets worth 22 million TL sit in the warehouse

1,400 sets of the collection that drew praise at the trade fair were produced, and 520 have sold. The remaining 880 sets cost close to 1.1 million TL a month to hold.

Kayınbaş Mobilya Furniture · Kayseri · 160 employees · 64 dealers, 1,500 deliveries a month

The problem

In the spring, Kayınbaş Mobilya launched the Lina living room collection, made up of six models. Counting on the interest at the trade fair, management produced 1,400 sets without waiting for dealer orders. In six months, 520 sets sold; 880 sets are left in the warehouse at a cost of 25,000 TL each, or 22 million TL of goods sitting idle. Sales have dropped to 40 sets a month, and at that pace it would take 22 months to empty the warehouse.

According to Finance Manager Sibel Erdem's calculation, this inventory is being carried on credit: at 45% interest, that is 9.9 million TL a year, or about 825,000 TL a month. Because the goods don't fit in the main warehouse, an additional warehouse is being rented for 260,000 TL a month. Sales and Dealer Manager Fatma Görgülü proposes cutting the dealer price from 36,000 TL to 21,600 TL, a 40% discount, to clear the warehouse in two months.

General Manager Kerem Kayınbaş is against it. A 40% discount means selling at a loss of 3,400 TL per set, roughly 3 million TL across 880 sets. On top of that, dealers have 310 sets in their stores that were bought at the old price; if dealers ask for the price difference, that adds another 4.5 million TL. Kerem Kayınbaş also worries that the brand will become known as one where you should “wait for the discount.”

By the numbers

1,400 sets produced, 520 sold, 880 left in the warehouse
Cost of the goods in the warehouse: 22 million TL (25,000 TL per set)
Monthly carrying cost: 825,000 TL in interest and 260,000 TL in extra warehouse rent
Dealers hold 310 sets bought at the old price
What would you do?

Would you clear the warehouse with a 40% discount and take the loss, or hold the price and wait?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Don't treat the 880 sets as a single pile; break them down by model and fabric. Find out first whether the whole collection isn't selling or just a few models.
  • Call the five dealers who sell the most Lina and the five who haven't sold any today. Sales consultants know what customers say in the store before they walk away.
  • Don't announce a discount without talking to the dealers. A dealer with old-price stock who hears about the discount from someone else will first ask for the price difference.

2This week

  • If the problem is the fabric or the color, keep the frame and change the fabric. Work out the cost of reupholstering a set and compare it with the 3,400 TL per set loss from selling at a discount.
  • Look for a buyer that doesn't touch the dealer channel: bulk buyers such as hotels, student dormitories and guesthouses. A discount given here doesn't undermine the price tag in the store.
  • Announce a rule in advance for the 310 sets the dealers hold: if a discount is made, on which models, from what date, and how dealers' stock will be protected.
  • Make emptying the extra warehouse the first target; the 260,000 TL monthly rent is the quickest item to cut after the interest.

3Lasting fix

  • Put the new collection on display at 10 dealers first and limit the first batch to 300 sets. Move to serial production only once dealer pre-orders reach a set number.
  • Don't treat trade fair enthusiasm as a sales forecast. The only yardstick for a production decision should be a signed order from a dealer.
  • Leave the fabric and color decision to the very end of production: make the frame for stock and the upholstery to order.
The lesson

Applause at a trade fair is not an order; goods made without knowing they will sell are a debt that keeps accruing interest.
