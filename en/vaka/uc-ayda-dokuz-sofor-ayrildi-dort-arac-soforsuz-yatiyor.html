Nine drivers left in three months, and four trucks sit idle without drivers

Nine of 52 drivers left in three months. Finding replacements is expensive and slow. Nobody asked those who left why they went.

Vargel Lojistik Logistics · Kocaeli · 85 employees · 42 tractor units, 3 warehouses

The problem

At Vargel, nine of the 52 drivers have left in the past three months. Only five people could be found to replace them; four trucks are sitting in the garage without drivers. Each idle truck costs about 28,000 TL a day in lost revenue.

Posting the job, training and the adjustment period for a new driver cost the company about 95,000 TL. No exit interviews were held with those who left; the general view in management is that “a competitor paid more.”

Selim Vargel is thinking of giving everyone a 10% raise. Aysun Tekin says a raise made without knowing the cause won't solve the problem, only make it more expensive.

By the numbers

What would you do?

Would you give everyone a raise and move on, or call the nine who left first?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Call the nine who left. Since they have nothing left to lose, they will give the most honest answer. One question is enough: what made you leave the most?
  • The same week, talk one on one with your ten most senior drivers. Why those who stayed stayed is as valuable as why those who left left.

2This week

  • Conversations like these often turn up other things before pay: the uncertainty of the day they get home, a late-paid travel allowance, not being able to reach anyone when stranded on the road. Most of these are cheaper to fix than a raise.
  • Pay the travel allowance on the day the trip ends. A driver waiting weeks to be reimbursed for expenses he paid out of his own pocket creates a big grievance over a small amount.
  • Make the days drivers get home planned: every driver should know at least two weeks ahead which days they will be at home.
  • Compare pay with the market; if it is truly behind, fix it by seniority and performance rather than with an equal raise for everyone.

3Lasting fix

  • Give a loyalty bonus to a driver who completes a year, and a referral bonus to a driver whose recruited friend stays six months.
  • Make exit interviews mandatory for every departure and record the reasons.
  • Add the monthly turnover rate and the number of truck-days idle without a driver to management indicators. A loss that isn't measured gets explained away as “that's how the market is.”
The lesson

People often leave not because of pay but because of unpredictability and a feeling of being undervalued.
