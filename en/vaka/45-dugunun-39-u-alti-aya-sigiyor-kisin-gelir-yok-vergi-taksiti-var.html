39 of 45 weddings fit into six months: no income in winter, but a tax installment due

Last winter Tolga Erinç earned 342,000 TL in six months and spent 576,000 TL. He covered the shortfall and the March tax installment with a loan and a credit card.

Tolga Erinç Fotoğraf Self-employed · Antalya · 1 employees · 45 weddings a year

The problem

Of the 45 weddings Tolga Erinç shoots a year, 39 fall between May and October. In the six months from November to April there are only six weddings. Last winter he earned 288,000 TL from those six weddings and 54,000 TL from a few corporate events, 342,000 TL in all. The studio rent, equipment installments, insurance premiums and household living costs come to 96,000 TL a month, or 576,000 TL over six months.

On top of the 234,000 TL gap came a 74,000 TL income tax installment in March. He covered the total of 308,000 TL with a 180,000 TL personal loan and 128,000 TL of credit card debt. When the season opened he paid the debts off, but the interest alone came to 61,000 TL. When he also paid the second tax installment in July by card, he saw that this was not a one-time squeeze.

The money is actually being earned: in the summer months nearly 400,000 TL a month comes into his account. The problem is that the money that comes in during summer is spent during summer. Tolga Erinç is thinking about cutting prices in winter to take on baby, engagement and product shoots, but he worries that this work would give his summer clients the impression of a “cheap photographer.” Raising the deposit is on his mind too; he's afraid couples will go to another photographer.

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Work out the winter shortfall once and write it down as one number: a 234,000 TL gap, 74,000 TL in tax. An expense that comes back every year isn't a surprise; its place on the calendar is known.
  • Open a second bank account today and name it “winter.” Keeping the money in the same account means it looks spendable.

2This week

  • Transfer a fixed percentage of every payment received during the season to the winter account on the day it arrives. Don't try to set aside whatever is left at the end of the month; nothing is left at the end of the month.
  • In next season's contracts, raise the deposit but split it in two: part at signing, part in midwinter. The load eases for the couple and money comes into your winter.
  • Don't cut your wedding price in winter; find other work to do in winter. The hotels' meeting and conference season falls exactly in your empty months.
  • Make the tax installments the first line item of the winter account. March and July come every year.

3Lasting fix

  • Think of income annually and spend it monthly: pay yourself the same amount every month as a “salary,” and leave the rest in the business account.
  • Track winter income's share of the total. Let the line of work that doesn't depend on weddings grow a little each year.
  • Treat credit as insurance, not a plan. A loan used every winter means handing the bank nearly a fifth of your winter income.
The lesson

In seasonal work, money is set aside with the month it will be spent in in mind, not the month it is earned.
