Three trucks broke down the same morning, and 11 deliveries are up in the air

On Monday morning, three vehicles in the fleet were stranded at once. There is no spare vehicle, and four loads have an automotive customer's production line waiting.

Vargel Lojistik Logistics · Kocaeli · 85 employees · 42 tractor units, 3 warehouses

The problem

On Monday at 6:40 a.m., Fleet Lead Burak Önal's phone rang three times in a row. One truck was stranded in Bolu with a turbo failure; two others couldn't leave the garage because of injectors from the same batch, installed the same week.

Vargel's 42 trucks were already running at 93% utilization, so there are no idle vehicles. The 11 shipments due to leave that day are affected. Four of them are loads that must be delivered just in time to an automotive customer: if the parts are late, the customer's line stops, and under the contract a penalty of 180,000 TL per hour kicks in.

The workshop says the vehicles will be back in two to five days. Operations Manager Aysun Tekin has a three-hour window to decide.

By the numbers

3 of 42 trucks out of service, fleet utilization 93%
Shipments affected: 11, 4 of them line-feed loads
Line stoppage penalty: 180,000 TL per hour
Estimated repair time: 2 to 5 days
What would you do?

You have 39 vehicles, 11 loads and three hours. Which load would you give to whom, and which customer would you call first?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Rank the shipments not by order of arrival but by the cost of delay. The four line-feed loads are the undisputed first priority; they get moved onto the healthy trucks leaving today.
  • For the more flexible loads that lose their truck, talk to their owners and push them back a day. Call the customer yourself before 8:00 a.m.; a customer who hears about a delay from you and one who finds out on their own don't react the same way.
  • For the remaining loads, rent vehicles by the day from the spot market and from subcontractors you know. It will run 30 to 40 percent above the normal cost, but a single hour of line-stoppage penalty is far more expensive.

2This week

  • Pull from the maintenance records whether any other vehicles have injectors from the same batch. If there are, replace them without waiting for a failure; don't live through the same morning a second time.
  • Send the parts supplier your warranty and damages claim with documents: the rental vehicle difference, postponed trips, workshop costs.
  • Work out the real cost of the incident and present it to management on a single page. That number will be the justification for the spare capacity decision.

3Lasting fix

  • Sign a framework agreement with two subcontractors: price and call-out time set in advance, spare capacity that kicks in with a single phone call when needed.
  • Bring the utilization target down from 93% to 88 to 90 percent, or deliberately keep one vehicle in reserve. Utilization near 100% is efficient on paper and fragile in practice.
  • Keep maintenance records at the parts-batch level. Don't install critical parts from the same batch across the whole fleet in the same week.
The lesson

Spare capacity is not a waste item, it is an insurance premium.
