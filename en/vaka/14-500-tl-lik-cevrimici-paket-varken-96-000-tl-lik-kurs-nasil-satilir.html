How do you sell a 96,000 TL course when there's a 14,500 TL online package?

Parents come to enrollment meetings armed with the price of online course packages. The rate of meetings that end in enrollment fell from 38% to 26%.

Bilgi Çınarı Kursları Education · Eskişehir · 48 employees · 3 branches, 1,150 students

The problem

At Bilgi Çınarı, the annual course fee is 96,000 TL. This year, most parents who come to an enrollment meeting start with the same sentence: an online package with videos and problem solutions for every subject costs 14,500 TL, so how do you justify the difference?

According to Onur Baysal's records, 540 enrollment meetings were held in August and September, and 140 ended in enrollment: 26%. In the same period last year, the rate was 38%; at that rate there would have been 205 enrollments. The 65 students in between mean 6.2 million TL in fees.

Halil Çınar wants either to cut the price or to launch the course's own video package at 30,000 TL. Zerrin Aktaş is against this: she says you can't compete with video lessons, that the topic explanations from well-known online teachers are already good, and that what the course really sells is not lessons but follow-up.

By the numbers

Annual fee: 96,000 TL for the course, 14,500 TL for the online package
Meeting-to-enrollment rate: from 38% to 26% (540 meetings, 140 enrollments)
Enrollments lost: 65 students, about 6.2 million TL
Course program: 36 weeks, 24 hours of lessons and study sessions a week
What would you do?

Would you cut the price, launch your own online package, or explain to parents what the 81,500 TL difference buys?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Change the order of the enrollment meeting. The meeting ends on the price, and the parent sets the last number they heard next to 14,500 TL. First talk about the student's practice exam result, weak topics and weekly schedule; let the price come last.
  • Turn the price into a unit the parent understands: 96,000 TL over 36 weeks at 24 hours a week comes to about 111 TL an hour. The private tutoring rate parents know is 1,200 TL an hour.

2This week

  • Don't try to compete with video on topic explanation. Write down and make visible what the course gives that video can't: weekly practice exams, one-on-one study sessions where missed questions are worked through, attendance and homework tracking, a guidance counselor.
  • Offer undecided parents a two-week free trial period. Let the student join study sessions, take a practice exam and meet the guidance counselor; the decision comes after that.
  • Design an in-between package for families who already bought the online package: the student watches the topic lessons from video at home and comes to the course three days a week for practice exams, study sessions and guidance.
  • Rehearse with the team that runs the meetings the answer to the parent's price question. If everyone defends in their own words, parents see three different courses at three branches.

3Lasting fix

  • Make follow-up measurable and send parents a one-page report every month: how many practice exams, how many study hours, how many net points gained in which subject. A service that can't be seen gets its price questioned.
  • Break the price list out of a single line item; let lessons, study sessions, practice exams and guidance appear separately.
  • Track the meeting-to-enrollment rate every week by branch and by the person who ran the meeting.
The lesson

You can't sell what the cheap rival sells at a higher price; you make visible what it can't sell.
