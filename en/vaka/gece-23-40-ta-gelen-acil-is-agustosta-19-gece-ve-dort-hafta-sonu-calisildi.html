An “urgent” job arriving at 11:40 p.m.: 19 nights and four weekends worked in August

Clients abroad send work during their own daytime and mark everything “urgent.” Selin Karaduman turns none of it down; for 17 of the 23 urgent jobs, nobody looked at the delivery for a full day.

Selin Karaduman Çeviri Self-employed · Izmir · 1 employees · 95,000 words a month

The problem

One of Selin Karaduman's two clients abroad is in western Canada; the time difference is ten hours. Their afternoon is midnight in Izmir. In August she worked after 10 p.m. on 19 of the 31 days, and she was at her computer on all four weekends. She works from home and her desk is in the living room; every email notification on her phone is in front of her at dinner, too.

In August, 23 jobs came in marked “urgent.” Out of curiosity, Selin Karaduman looked at the records: on 17 of the 23 jobs, the client neither wrote a reply nor asked a question within 24 hours of delivery. Files she worked until two in the morning to send sat waiting until the next afternoon. She doesn't charge extra for urgent jobs, either.

She hesitates to say no. Her clients abroad are her best-paying clients, and there are other translators who would do the same work. Once she tried writing “I'll look at it tomorrow morning”; the answer was “That's fine, but we may have to send it to someone who turns things around faster next time,” and that night she worked again.

By the numbers

Nights worked after 10 p.m. in August: 19 of 31 days
Weekends worked: 4 of 4
Jobs marked “urgent”: 23; no response within 24 hours of delivery on 17
Extra fee charged for urgent jobs: none
What would you do?

Would you set working hours at the risk of losing a client, ask for an extra fee on urgent jobs, or say yes at any hour to a client who pays well?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Write down your working hours and add them to your signature: between which hours you work, and the latest you reply to an email. If the client doesn't know your hours, they go by their own.
  • Set the work email notification on your phone to turn off at a set time in the evening. You don't answer an email at night that you haven't seen.

2This week

  • Set a rate for urgent work and announce it to all clients in a single email: delivery in under 24 hours and weekend delivery carry an extra fee. You're not banning anything, you're putting a price on it.
  • Offer the client in the other time zone a fixed arrangement: work they send by the end of their day is delivered by your next afternoon. The file is on their desk when they arrive in the morning.
  • Ask one question on every “urgent” job: when do you need the file? Often the answer is a date, and that date isn't tonight.
  • Tell apart the one that is truly urgent and do it. Don't let the rule stop you from saving a client's night before a hearing.

3Lasting fix

  • Don't make the urgent fee a matter of negotiation. The day you give a discount, urgent becomes free again.
  • Move your desk out of the living room or shut down the work computer in the evening. What ends a home worker's workday isn't a door, it's a habit.
  • Count your night and weekend workdays every month. If the number is rising, it's not the rule that's stretching, it's you.
The lesson

An “urgent” request with no price on it is not urgency, it's habit.
