When it rains, on-time delivery falls from 96 percent to 81 percent

On a rainy day about a thousand deliveries run late; penalties and refunds reach 83,000 TL a day. Winter is around the corner.

Hızır Kurye Logistics · Istanbul · 152 employees · 130 couriers, 5,200 deliveries a day

The problem

In dry weather Hızır Kurye completes 96 percent of its 5,200 daily deliveries within the four-hour promise. On rainy days the rate drops to 81 percent: 988 deliveries are late. Gamze Yücel knows the reason. Self-employed couriers don't have to go out in the rain; on average 97 of the 130 couriers head out, and those who do ride more slowly.

70 percent of deliveries are under contracts with penalties. For a late delivery, the 85 TL fee is not collected and a 35 TL penalty is deducted on top: 120 TL per delivery. A rainy day's bill is about 83,000 TL for 692 deliveries. Last winter there were 34 rainy days; 2.8 million TL in total.

Gamze Yücel proposes paying an extra 20 TL on all deliveries on rainy days, but that comes to 104,000 TL a day, more than the penalty. Bahar Tunç wants a weather clause added to the contracts. Kerem Hızır doesn't believe in it: “Customers treat rain as our problem, and the competition makes the same promise in the rain too.”

By the numbers

On-time delivery: 96 percent on a dry day, 81 percent on a rainy day
Couriers going out in the rain: an average of 97 out of 130
Penalties and refunds on a rainy day: about 83,000 TL
34 rainy days last winter: 2.8 million TL in total
What would you do?

Would you pay everyone rain money, change the contract, or build a separate plan for rainy days?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Stop managing a rainy day as a bad ordinary day; define it as a separate way of working. The weather forecast is known a day ahead, and the plan can be made a day ahead.
  • Separate the two sources of loss: the 33 couriers who don't go out, and slower traffic. The first is solved with money and planning, the second only by changing the promise you make.
  • Test the idea of an extra payment for everyone against the numbers. At 104,000 TL a day, it is larger than the 83,000 TL loss you are trying to prevent.

2This week

  • Give the extra payment not to everyone but to a core team that commits in advance to going out in the rain. Build a rainy-day duty roster in exchange for a fixed daily payment.
  • On rainy days take bulky and distant deliveries off motorcycles and give them to a few enclosed vehicles rented by the day.
  • Discuss the weather clause with customers as a service definition, not as haggling over penalties: on days with an official weather warning, the delivery time goes from four hours to six, and the customer is informed in the morning.
  • On rainy days, narrow each courier's zone. A short distance reduces both time and risk.

3Lasting fix

  • Don't track the on-time delivery rate as a single number; set separate targets for dry days and rainy days.
  • Build the cost of rainy days into price quotes from the start. Something that recurs 34 days a year is not an exception, it is part of the business.
  • Support couriers with winter equipment: rain gear, gloves, suitable tires. Some of the couriers who don't go out in the rain are not staying in for money, but to avoid getting wet and falling.
The lesson

What repeats every winter is not a surprise; it is a calendar without a plan.
