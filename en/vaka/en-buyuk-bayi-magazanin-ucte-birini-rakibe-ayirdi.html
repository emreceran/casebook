The biggest dealer gave a third of its store to a rival

The Ankara dealer, which brings in 10% of revenue, has started selling a rival brand too. Raising the discount costs 3.2 million TL for this dealer and 32.4 million TL across all dealers.

Kayınbaş Mobilya Furniture · Kayseri · 160 employees · 64 dealers, 1,500 deliveries a month

The problem

The Ankara dealer, which buys 54 million TL a year from Kayınbaş Mobilya, has given 400 square meters of its 1,200-square-meter store to a rival brand. The dealer had sold only Kayınbaş for 14 years. The contract says Kayınbaş will not open another dealer in that region, but there is no clause saying the dealer must sell only one brand.

Sales and Dealer Manager Fatma Görgülü found out the reason: the rival offers a 32% discount and 120-day payment terms; at Kayınbaş, the discount is 26% and the terms are 60 days. Fatma Görgülü proposes giving this dealer six extra points of discount, at a cost of about 3.2 million TL a year.

In General Manager Kerem Kayınbaş's view, this won't stop at one dealer. The news will spread within a week, and all 64 dealers will want the same discount; on 540 million TL in revenue, six points comes to 32.4 million TL. Kerem Kayınbaş thinks the opposite: lift the territory protection and open a second dealer in Ankara. Finance Manager Sibel Erdem, for her part, asks what it would cost to burn bridges with a dealer of 14 years.

By the numbers

Dealer's annual purchases: 54 million TL (10% of revenue)
Space given to the rival: 400 of 1,200 square meters
Rival's offer is a 32% discount and 120 days; Kayınbaş's is 26% and 60 days
Six points of discount: 3.2 million TL for this dealer, 32.4 million TL across all dealers
What would you do?

Would you give the dealer the discount it wants, open a second dealer in its territory, or find another way?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Don't react by phone or in writing; go to Ankara and walk through the store. See with your own eyes which products the rival has put out and at what prices.
  • Ask the dealer one question: apart from the discount, what are we missing? The first reason given is often the easiest one to say, not the real one.

2This week

  • Break down the dealer's last two years of purchases model by model. See in numbers at which price tier and at what delivery time it is losing sales.
  • Instead of a flat discount for everyone, design a bonus that has to be earned: an extra share at year end for the dealer that hits its annual target. The growing dealer wins, and the cost grows along with the growth.
  • Give display products and store layout support to the dealer that gave you most of its floor space. Compete for the space with service, not with discounts.
  • Don't put the threat of lifting territory protection on the table. A threat you won't carry out becomes a reason for the dealer to grow its business with the rival.

3Lasting fix

  • Add a display-space clause to dealer contracts: territory protection should apply only if a certain share of the space is reserved for the brand.
  • Deliver the best-selling models from stock in a short time. A dealer often turns to a rival not for the discount but for a delivery date it can promise customers.
  • Track each dealer's share of revenue and visit the five biggest dealers twice a year at general manager level. Don't learn the news from a customer walking into the store.
The lesson

A dealer usually makes room for a rival not for the discount but for a gap you haven't filled.
