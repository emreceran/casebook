Panel prices fell 25 percent in six months, and 2.1 megawatts bought at a high price sit in the warehouse

Panels bought in March at 7.20 TL per watt are 5.40 TL today. The difference is 3.78 million TL and grows every month.

Solmira Enerji Energy · Konya · 36 employees · 30 megawatts installed per year

The problem

In March, expecting prices to rise, Solmira Enerji put 2.1 megawatts of panels into stock at 7.20 TL per watt, 15.12 million TL in total. The opposite of what was expected happened: the price fell every month and dropped to 5.40 TL in September. The panels in the warehouse are worth 11.34 million TL today; the difference is 3.78 million TL.

Sales Manager Gizem Tuna is stuck on quotes. Competitors quote at the current panel price; when Solmira calculates at the old cost, it comes out expensive. Five of the last seven quotes were lost on price. General Manager Tolga Erdin wants to wait: “A price that has fallen this far will come back. If we sell at a loss, we'll have thrown 3.78 million TL away with our own hands.”

According to Finance Manager Nesrin Yalman, the loss has already happened; the only question is when to accept it. The price is falling an average of 0.30 TL a month, which means 630,000 TL in lost value on the warehouse goods every month. Project Manager Kaan Özbey points out one more obstacle: projects with signed contracts were drawn up for panels of a different wattage, and the panels in the warehouse don't fit those drawings.

By the numbers

Panels in the warehouse: 2.1 megawatts, purchase price 7.20 TL per watt
Current price 5.40 TL; a 25% drop in six months
Purchase cost 15.12 million TL, current value 11.34 million TL
5 of the last 7 quotes were lost on price
What would you do?

Would you wait for the price to bounce back, or book the loss today and start quoting at the current price?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Switch your quote calculations to the current panel price today. What you paid for the panels is of no interest to the customer; the customer compares you with your competitor's price today.
  • Stop using the purchase price as a decision criterion. That money is spent; the question today is how to recover the most money from these panels.

2This week

  • List the projects whose contracts were signed at the old price. On these projects the panel cost was already calculated at around 7.20 TL; if the panels in the warehouse go there, no loss arises at all.
  • If the panels don't fit the drawings, change the drawings. Redrawing the layout of three projects is a few days of engineering work, and it prevents a loss of millions of lira.
  • Use the remaining panels at the current price in new quotes and book the loss this quarter. Every month you hold them costs another 0.30 TL.
  • Call the owners of the five lost quotes; take them a second quote at the new price.

3Lasting fix

  • Set a rule for stocking panels: only for jobs with signed contracts, and no more than 30 days of need.
  • Cut the price validity period on quotes to 15 days; if the panel price changes, the quote is renewed.
  • Make purchases based on price forecasts subject to a board decision. Procurement is not a place to bet on prices.
The lesson

What you paid for the goods is a matter of the past; what you can sell them for is decided by today's market.
