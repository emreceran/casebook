“Since you're here, take a look at this too”: 31 extra jobs went into a 185,000 TL shop job for free

The wiring job at a pastry shop took 19 days instead of 12. The extras the customer asked for every day came to 101,400 TL, but none was written down; the customer wants to pay only the agreed price.

Rıfat Dönmezer Elektrik Self-employed · Bursa · 2 employees · About 30 jobs a month

The problem

At the end of July, Rıfat Dönmezer agreed on 185,000 TL, materials included, for the electrical wiring of a pastry shop being renovated. The job was supposed to take 12 working days. The shop owner stopped by the shop every morning and added something each time: two more outlets behind the counter, separate lighting for the display case, a sign line, a line for a cabinet in the storeroom. Rıfat Dönmezer said “sure” to all of it and never talked about the price of any.

The job finished in 19 days. Together with his apprentice Emirhan Koçak, they counted from the photos on their phones and the material receipts: 31 extra jobs. The extra materials were 38,400 TL, the labor for the seven extra days worked was 63,000 TL; 101,400 TL in all. Meanwhile, the next two jobs slipped by a week; one of the customers didn't wait and gave a 42,000 TL job to someone else.

On the final payment day, the shop owner took out the remaining 55,000 TL and said, “We agreed on 185, the account is settled.” When Rıfat Dönmezer brought up the extras, the answer was: “If you'd told me it would cost money, I wouldn't have asked for half of it.” He doesn't have a single signed piece of paper. The shop owner is well known in the neighborhood and has sent him three more customers.

By the numbers

Agreed price: 185,000 TL, 12 working days; actual 19 working days
Number of extra jobs: 31; extra materials 38,400 TL, extra labor 63,000 TL
Total value of the extras: 101,400 TL
Work lost because of the delay: 42,000 TL
What would you do?

Would you ask for the 101,400 TL after the job is done, stay quiet so as not to lose the customer, or find a middle ground?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Make the list of extras today, while your memory is fresh and you have the photos and receipts in hand. For each line, write what was done, on which day, and the materials.
  • Take the customer the list, not the total. “I want 101,400 TL more” is a negotiating sentence; a 31-line list is an account of work done.

2This week

  • Talk about materials and labor separately. Materials have receipts and aren't up for debate. On labor, having worked without naming a price is your fault too; own your share there.
  • Read the list with the customer and separate the jobs they asked for from the ones you suggested. It's hard to bill a customer for a job you suggested yourself.
  • Offer to split the agreed amount into installments yourself. A customer who has to pay an unexpected debt in one go will take offense.
  • End this conversation as a closed account. If you're going to keep the customer, there should be no balance left in it.

3Lasting fix

  • Write the work items one by one in the quote and add this sentence under them: every job not on the list is priced separately.
  • When an extra job is asked for, put down the tool and pick up the phone: the name and price of the job go out by message, and you don't start until the “yes” comes back.
  • On long jobs, send the customer that week's extras and their total at the end of every week. The smaller the surprise, the easier it is to pay.
The lesson

An extra job done without naming a price stays a favor in the customer's eyes.
