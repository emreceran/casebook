The soft gears are inside 360 gearboxes, and nobody knows which ones

Part of a 1,840-gear batch returned from outsourced heat treatment was not hardened enough. Incoming inspection missed it; 720 gears are running in the machines of nine customers.

Wendrath Antriebstechnik Manufacturing · Esslingen, Germany · 140 employees · 21 million euros in annual revenue

The problem

At the end of June, a packaging machine maker sent back two gearboxes delivered three months earlier, complaining of premature wear. Production Manager Jochen Brettschneider had the gearboxes opened up: the surface hardness of the pinion gears was well below what it should have been. The gears came from a batch of 1,840 that had returned in March from an outside heat-treatment firm. The firm later admitted that one of its furnaces had suffered a temperature fluctuation that week.

At incoming inspection Wendrath measured five parts from each batch, and all five had come out fine. The problem lay with parts in the baskets stacked in the back rows of the furnace. Of the batch, 1,120 gears were still in the warehouse and on the semi-finished shelf. The remaining 720 gears had been fitted, two to a gearbox, into 360 gearboxes and shipped to nine customers. The gearboxes had serial numbers, but there was no record of which gearbox got gears from which basket.

Replacing a gear in a gearbox in the field cost about 950 euros including parts, labour and travel; for 360 gearboxes, 342,000 euros. Sabine Kollmer, head of finance, proposed replacing them as complaints came in, since most of the gears were probably fine. Head of Sales Miriam Adler said that an explanation made after a customer's line had stopped would cost far more. The outside firm admitted the fault, but offered only to refund its own processing fee.

By the numbers

Suspect batch: 1,840 gears; 1,120 in the warehouse, 720 with customers
Gearboxes in the field: 360, at 9 customers
Cost of replacing the gears in one gearbox: about 950 euros; 342,000 euros for all of them
Parts measured at incoming inspection: 5 from the batch
What would you do?

Would you open all 360 gearboxes, or wait for failures? When would you tell the customers, and what would you say?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Lock down the 1,120 gears in the warehouse; not a single part from the batch goes to assembly. Stop the problem from growing inside the company first.
  • Call all nine customers the same day. You don't have a rate yet, but there are three things you can tell them: which serial numbers are affected, when you will have measurement results, and what they should watch for until then.
  • Ask the outside firm for that week's furnace logs and loading plan. Which baskets sat in which positions is the only document that will show how far the problem reaches.

2This week

  • Measure all 1,120 gears in the warehouse. Don't settle for a sample; without knowing the defect rate across the batch, you can't decide about the 720 gears in the field.
  • For the gearboxes in the field, bring customers a plan, not options: which gearbox is opened which week, with whose crew, timed to the customer's planned shutdown.
  • When you discuss cost with the outside firm, put the consequences of the defect on the table, not the processing fee: replacement in the field, measurement, scrap. Ask them to bring in their liability insurance.
  • Measure the gears you remove and share the results with the customer. Showing how many gearboxes were really at risk is the only way to defend your decision afterwards.

3Lasting fix

  • At incoming inspection, take samples by position in the furnace, not at random: from every basket, especially the corner and back rows.
  • Link the heat-treatment batch and basket number to the gearbox serial number. Next time you open only the gearboxes concerned, not 360.
  • Ask the outside firm for the furnace temperature log with every batch, and write into the contract that every deviation during processing must be reported before shipment.
The lesson

If you don't know where the defective part went, you have to treat all the good ones as suspect too.
