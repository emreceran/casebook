Listeria suspected in white cheese: 3,900 kilos are already on the shelves

In routine testing, a sample from a 6,400-kilo batch came back positive. Confirmation will take three days; most of the batch has already gone out to the supermarket chain and 41 shops.

Yaylacık Süt Ürünleri Food · Balıkesir · 75 employees · 40 tons of milk a day, 186 farmers

The problem

Yaylacık takes a sample from every batch and sends it to an outside lab. The result that arrived Friday afternoon was bad: listeria bacteria were detected in a sample from a 6,400-kilo batch of white cheese. 2,500 kilos of the batch are in the warehouse. 3,900 kilos have been shipped: 2,700 kilos at the chain's stores, and 1,200 kilos at 41 delis and grocers. The batch's sales value is about 1.98 million TL.

Production and Quality Manager Erdal Akbulut favors waiting. The result is based on a single sample, the confirmation test takes three days, and a false positive is possible. In his view, the goods in the warehouse should be held, but the chain should be told only once the result is definitive: a recall could end a ten-year relationship with the chain in a single day.

Gülşen Yaylacık won't accept this. The cheese is being sold on the shelves today; within three days it will reach hundreds of tables. Listeria hits pregnant women, the elderly and people with weak immune systems hardest. In her view, having to say three days from now “we knew but we waited” is a far heavier burden than a recall today. The decision rests with Cevdet Yaylacık.

By the numbers

Batch: 6,400 kilos of white cheese, sales value about 1.98 million TL
In the warehouse: 2,500 kilos; shipped: 3,900 kilos
Shipped product is at the chain's stores and 41 shops
Confirmation test: 3 days
What would you do?

Would you wait for the confirmation result, or call the chain today and have the cheese pulled from the shelves?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Stop the shipped product today. Call the chain's quality department and the 41 shops by phone, and send the batch number in writing: stop selling, set the product aside. Destruction isn't needed until the confirmation comes in, but sales must stop.
  • Quarantine the 2,500 kilos in the warehouse and any other batches produced on the same line in the same week; send samples from all of them.
  • Notify the provincial agriculture and forestry directorate yourself. Legal obligation aside, nothing said later by someone who reported late will be believed.

2This week

  • Make an announcement to consumers: product name, batch number, expiration date, what to do, and a phone number to call. For cheese sold loose, give shops a counter notice.
  • Look for the source: brine tanks, the filling line, floor drains, gaskets. Listeria often comes not from the milk but from a wet corner of the plant.
  • Report the findings and the measures taken to the chain day by day. What will lose you the chain isn't the recall, it's their not hearing from you what happened.
  • Weigh and record the product that comes back; the difference between what was shipped and what was collected is the amount that reached consumers.

3Lasting fix

  • Monitor not just the product but the environment: take weekly samples from floor, drain and equipment surfaces. Bacteria found in the environment are the harbinger of what will be found in the product.
  • On risky products, don't ship before the test result arrives. Set aside a two-to-three-day holding area in the warehouse for this.
  • Prepare a written recall plan and rehearse it once a year: which batch went to whom, can you pull it out within four hours?
The lesson

In food, waiting when in doubt is not failing to decide; it is deciding that sales continue.
