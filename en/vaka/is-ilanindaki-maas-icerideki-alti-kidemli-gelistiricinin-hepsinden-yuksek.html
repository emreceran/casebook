The salary in the job advert is higher than that of all six senior developers already here

The advert for a new senior developer said 58,000–65,000 pounds. The six people who have been doing the same job for years average 52,500 pounds; within a week one of them resigned.

Tallowfield Software Software · Leeds, United Kingdom · 46 employees · 3,800 subscribing businesses

The problem

In early June Tallowfield advertised for a senior developer. Stating the salary range in adverts is common in the UK, and ours said 58,000–65,000 pounds. Six of the team's 14 developers were senior, and their salaries ran from 49,000 to 56,000 pounds, averaging 52,500 pounds. So the lowest figure in the advert was 2,000 pounds above the highest salary inside the company.

The advert reached everyone within a day, after an employee dropped the link into the team chat. In the same week a senior developer of six years' standing handed in his notice, and two others asked their manager about their pay. Founder Graham Pellow tried to explain: the market had moved in two years, outside hires were coming in at this price, and the people inside had been given a 3% rise in January.

Finance Director Deborah Ainscough worked out what it would cost to bring the six up to the bottom of the advertised range: 33,000 pounds a year in salaries, around 38,600 pounds with employer contributions and pension. There was no such line in the budget. On the other hand, for each senior developer who left, a recruitment agency would be paid 20% of salary, around 12,000 pounds, and the new starter would work at half efficiency for the first three or four months. Priya Raghavan, Head of Product, had a question that wasn't about money: “We knew about this gap when we wrote the advert. Why didn't we tell them ourselves?”

By the numbers

Range in the advert: 58,000–65,000 pounds
Salaries of the six senior developers: 49,000–56,000 pounds, average 52,500
Annual cost of bringing the six to the bottom of the range: 33,000 pounds in salary, about 38,600 with on-costs
Agency fee to replace a developer who leaves: about 12,000 pounds
What would you do?

Would you fix the salaries straight away, make an offer only to those who want to leave, or explain that this is how the market is?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Talk to the six the same day, one by one, and tell them how things stand: the gap is real, finding out from the advert was a mistake, and you'll come back within two weeks with a concrete offer.
  • Don't rush out a counter-offer to the person who resigned. If you give a rise only to the one who walks, you show the remaining five how to get a pay rise.

2This week

  • Set a written salary range for senior developer, and make it the same as the range in the advert. You can't tell the people inside one figure and the people outside another for the same job.
  • Bring the six into that range, and differentiate seniority and contribution within it. If you line everyone up at the bottom, you've put a six-year employee on a par with a new starter.
  • Compare the cost with the cost of a developer leaving: the agency fee, the months at half efficiency, and what the person who goes takes with them. Fixing it is usually cheaper.
  • Write down the reason for the rise plainly: here is the range, you were here, you've moved to here. A rise that comes without explanation won't stop suspicion over the next advert.

3Lasting fix

  • Write down the salary range for every role and keep it open to employees. The range is visible in the advert anyway; let it be visible inside too.
  • Before placing an advert, look at the pay of the current people in that role. If the figure in the advert is above the people inside, fix inside first, then publish the advert.
  • Assess the annual rise not only as a percentage but by position against the market range. An employee who gets 3% every year while the market has gone up 6% falls behind every year.
The lesson

The salary you advertise outside has been told to the people inside as well; if you aren't the first to tell them, the advert will be.
