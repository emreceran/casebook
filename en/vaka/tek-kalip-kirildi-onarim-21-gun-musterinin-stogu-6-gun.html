The only mold cracked: repair takes 21 days, the customer's stock lasts 6

The only mold for the detergent drawer, which is made at 4,200 a day, has cracked. There is no spare; if the customer's line stops, a penalty of 240,000 TL an hour will apply.

Kalıpsak Plastik Manufacturing · Bursa · 95 employees · 22 injection molding machines

The problem

Kalıpsak's busiest part is a washing machine detergent drawer: 4,200 a day, 11 percent of revenue. The part has a single four-cavity mold. On Tuesday night's shift the core in two of the mold's cavities cracked and the machine stopped.

The toolmaker's first answer was clear: repair takes three weeks. The customer's warehouse holds six days of stock, or 25,200 pieces. Together with the 5,000 pieces in Kalıpsak's own warehouse, the total on hand is 30,200 pieces, a little over seven days. If the customer's line stops, the contract imposes a penalty of 240,000 TL per hour.

Cengiz Ergün wants a solution found before the customer is told: “If we call without a plan, all we do is spread panic.” Müge Sarıkaya, on the other hand, says every hour reduces the customer's ability to change its plans and that the news must be given today. İlker Dinç proposes paying the toolmaker 60 percent extra and running double shifts.

By the numbers

Daily need: 4,200 pieces; the part's share of revenue 11 percent
Stock on hand: 25,200 at the customer, 5,000 at Kalıpsak (about 7 days in total)
Normal repair time: 21 days
Line stoppage penalty: 240,000 TL per hour
What would you do?

Would you call the customer before the plan is ready? How would you bridge a three-week repair with seven days of stock?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Call the customer today. You don't need a finished plan in hand; it is enough to give the stock count, the condition of the mold and when you will provide the next update. The customer needs time to change its production plan, and only you can give it that time.
  • Don't discuss a single option with the toolmaker. Ask three questions separately: can the mold be run temporarily, can the new cores be machined separately while the mold is running, and with double shifts how many days does the time drop to?
  • Write the stock calculation in pieces and days and discuss it with the customer from the same table.

2This week

  • Block off the two cracked cavities and run the mold at half capacity with the two sound ones. 2,100 pieces a day won't close the gap, but it halves the rate at which the stock melts away.
  • Have the new cores machined separately at the toolmaker on double shifts; stop the mold only for assembly and trial.
  • Close the remaining gap together with the customer: ask whether it is possible to postpone production of the model that uses this drawer for a few days and bring another model forward.
  • Every morning send the customer's planner a one-line status: stock on hand, yesterday's production, the state of the cores.

3Lasting fix

  • List the parts that run on a single mold and could stop a line. For each, decide: a spare core set, a second mold or a higher safety stock.
  • Set the safety stock for these parts according to repair time. Holding one day of stock behind a mold that takes 21 days to repair is the same as working without stock.
  • Discuss the investment in a second mold with the customer. If the risk is both parties' risk, the cost can be shared too.
The lesson

A supplier who gives bad news early leaves the customer options; one who gives it late leaves only an invoice.
