Nine plants are ready, grid approval is missing: 19.2 million TL waits at the door

The final payment on nine finished projects depends on grid connection approval. Approvals have been taking 74 days on average, and there is 6.3 million TL in the bank.

Solvera Enerji Energy · Konya · 36 employees · 30 megawatts installed per year

The problem

On nine Solvera Enerji projects worth 96 million TL in total, the panels are on the roof and the cables are run, but the plants can't generate. It is prohibited to commission them before the distribution company's acceptance committee visits and approves the connection. Approval, which normally takes 30 days, has been pending for an average of 74 days on these nine projects.

Under the contracts, 30% of the price is paid up front, 50% when materials arrive on site, and the final 20% after approval. That means 19.2 million TL depends on approval. Finance Manager Nesrin Yalman's table is tight: there is 6.3 million TL in the bank, monthly fixed costs are 4.1 million TL, and 11.5 million TL must be paid to suppliers within 45 days. Nesrin Yalman proposes covering the gap with a loan.

General Manager Tolga Erdin favors asking customers for payment: “We finished our job, the delay isn't our fault.” Project Manager Kaan Özbey, however, offers an uncomfortable detail: four of the nine files aren't waiting for an appointment, they were sent back for missing documents. The customers are impatient too; they have a plant on their roof that they paid for but that doesn't work.

By the numbers

Projects awaiting approval: 9, total value 96 million TL
Final payments dependent on approval: 19.2 million TL (20%)
Average wait 74 days; 4 of the files were returned for missing documents
6.3 million TL in the bank; 11.5 million TL in supplier debt due in 45 days
What would you do?

Would you cover the gap with a loan, ask customers for early payment, or look at your own files first?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Split the nine files in two: the four returned for missing documents are your job, the five waiting for an appointment belong to the distribution company. Finish what's in your hands first; have a clean desk before asking customers for money.
  • List the gaps in the four files today and assign each one a person and a date. A grounding measurement report or a diagram correction is a day's work, not something to wait weeks on.
  • Call all nine customers and tell them exactly where their file stands. A customer you give no information to will blame you for the entire delay.

2This week

  • Offer customers a supplementary protocol: half of the final 20% is paid against a joint record showing installation is complete, the other half on approval. In return, give a letter of guarantee for the same amount, so the customer's risk drops to zero.
  • Talk to the suppliers; deferring part of the 11.5 million TL by 30 days is cheaper than a loan.
  • Take the distribution company all nine projects in a single list and propose grouping acceptances in the same area on the same day. It suits them too for the committee to visit three rooftops in one day.
  • Prepare the loan as a last resort, but get the credit line opened now so it can be drawn in a single day if needed.

3Lasting fix

  • Prepare the application file when installation starts, not when it ends. The file should be ready to submit the day the last panel goes up.
  • In new contracts, cut the approval-dependent share from 20% to 10% and write down how payment will work if administrative delay exceeds 45 days.
  • Use a checklist for every file and make the number of files returned for missing documents one of the project team's metrics.
The lesson

If the last step your money depends on is in someone else's hands, you have to pave the road to that step yourself from the very start.
