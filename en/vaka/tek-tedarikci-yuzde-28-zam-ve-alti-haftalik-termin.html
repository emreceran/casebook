A single supplier, a 28% price hike and a six-week lead time

The indispensable alloy for ductile iron casting comes from a single source. The price hike is annoying, but the real danger is the 19 days of stock in the warehouse.

Ergitaş Döküm Manufacturing · Konya · 140 employees · 900 tons a month, two shifts

The problem

The sole supplier of the nodularizing alloy that Ergitaş uses in ductile iron casting delivered two pieces of news at once: the price is going up 28% and the lead time is stretching from two weeks to six.

Ductile iron casting is 65% of revenue. Without this alloy, ductile iron can't be cast. The warehouse holds 19 days of stock; if a new order were placed today, it would arrive in 42 days. That leaves a 23-day gap.

At the management meeting, the whole discussion revolved around the price hike. Halil Ergitaş is angry at the supplier and wants a harsh reply written. Zeynep Ergitaş Aksoy says the calendar is what needs looking at.

By the numbers

Price increase: 28%, lead time from 2 weeks to 6 weeks
Stock: 19 days, new lead time: 42 days, gap: 23 days
Alloy's share of total cost: about 4%
Ductile iron's share of revenue: 65%
What would you do?

In the meeting, would you discuss the price hike or the 23-day gap?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • First separate the two problems. Because the alloy is 4% of cost, a 28% hike raises total cost by about 1%: annoying but manageable. The 23-day stock gap, on the other hand, would halt two-thirds of production.
  • Talk to the supplier about lead times, not the hike. Ask for a partial and urgent shipment instead of a harsh reply; this is not the time to burn the bridge.
  • Prioritize the stock you have: which orders carry penalties, which customers are critical; the alloy goes to them first.

2This week

  • Ask at least two alternative suppliers for samples and start trial castings right away. The approval process takes three to four weeks; if you don't start today, you won't make it before the gap closes.
  • For the gap in between, make a bridge purchase on the spot market, even if it's expensive. One day of a stopped factory is far more than this price difference.
  • Notify automotive customers in advance about the change in material source; if they need to approve it, open the process now.

3Lasting fix

  • Draw up a critical raw material list: every item that comes from a single source and whose absence stops production. Have an approved second source found for each.
  • Set safety stock not by consumption but by the sum of lead time and the alternative source's approval time.
  • Add a raw material price index clause to long-term customer contracts; don't carry price hikes alone.
The lesson

Price is annoying, but a shortage stops the factory. Solve the shortage first.
