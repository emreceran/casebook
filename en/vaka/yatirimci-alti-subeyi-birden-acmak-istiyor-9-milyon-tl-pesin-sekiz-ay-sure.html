An investor wants to open six branches at once: 9 million TL up front, eight months

An investor from Istanbul has offered to open six branches under the brand name license. Demli Kahve struggles even to oversee its five operator branches in Ankara.

Demli Kahve Food & Drink · Ankara · 80 employees · 9 branches

The problem

An investor who owns food and beverage businesses in three shopping malls in Istanbul has made Demli Kahve an offer: six branches in eight months. An entry fee of 1.5 million TL per branch, or 9 million TL up front, followed by 6% of revenue. With expected monthly revenue of 2.4 million TL per branch, six branches would mean 864,000 TL a month in license income. The investor has one condition: all six or none.

Founder Alper Sönmez is eager: “We opened nine branches in ten years. If we turn this offer down, the same man will be knocking on a rival's door in a month.” Finance Manager Cihan Bulut likes the upfront cash, but he also shows the math: the roasting plant's capacity is 4,200 kilograms a month, and 3,400 are used today. Six branches would need another 1,800 kilograms; a new roasting machine costs 4.2 million TL.

Operations and Quality Manager Esra Kurtuluş is plainly against it. The quality team is herself and one other person; they can visit the five operator branches in Ankara only once a month. Just last month it came out that one operator was using cheap beans. Training a barista takes six weeks, and six branches means about 54 new employees, all of them 450 kilometers away.

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Don't treat the “all six or none” condition as the last word; it's the opening line of a negotiation. Before answering, find out why the investor is in a hurry: does he have locations waiting on lease agreements?
  • Work out on one page what the six branches will cost: roasting machine, regional quality lead, training team, travel. How much of the 9 million TL stays in the till?

2This week

  • Take a phased counteroffer: two branches first. If these two meet quality criteria for six months, the right to the remaining four follows automatically.
  • Write the criteria in numbers: unannounced inspection score, customer rating, the match between bean purchases and cups sold. “Quality will be good” is not a contract clause.
  • Remove the investor's real fear: commit not to license anyone else in the region for 18 months. He doesn't want to miss the region, not just the six branches.
  • Set aside part of the entry fee for a regional quality lead who will be based in Istanbul and write that into the contract.

3Lasting fix

  • Build a training program that is mandatory to complete before opening for every new operator; the trainer goes from headquarters and stays at the branch for the first month.
  • Tie oversight capacity to the number of branches: don't open new branches without one quality lead for every eight.
  • Test growth decisions with a single question: is the coffee at the eleventh branch the same as at the first?
The lesson

Hanging a sign takes a day; serving the same coffee at every branch every day takes years.
