“30 days” became “3 days” in the contract: a 380,000 TL claim over a 20,700 TL translation

The defect notification period was written wrong in a sales contract Selin Karaduman translated. Because of this, the client couldn't return a shipment and is asking her to cover the loss.

Selin Karaduman Çeviri Self-employed · Izmir · 1 employees · 95,000 words a month

The problem

In July, Selin Karaduman translated a 9,400-word sales contract from Turkish into English for a machinery importer in Izmir; her fee was 20,700 TL. In the Turkish draft, the buyer's period for notifying defective goods was 30 days. In the English text, this came out as “3 days.” The contract was signed in both languages, and it stated that in case of dispute the English text prevails.

A shipment that arrived at the end of August had defective parts. The importer reported the defect on the eleventh day. The seller abroad pointed to the contract: the period is three days. On September 3, the importer's general manager called Selin Karaduman; the shipment was worth 380,000 TL and he was asking her to cover the loss.

Selin Karaduman opened the file and saw the mistake. She had delivered the text at midnight and hadn't checked the numbers separately; nobody besides her had read the text either. She has no written contract with the client; the job was taken by email. She has no professional liability insurance. 380,000 TL is more than two months of her income.

By the numbers

What would you do?

Would you admit the mistake right away, consult a lawyer first, or say “you should have read it before signing”?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Admit the mistake in writing the same day and send the corrected text. The two texts sit side by side in the file; there's nothing to deny, but there is something to delay.
  • Set aside the question “how much will I pay” and move on to “how can the loss be made smaller.” The client's real concern isn't compensation, it's being able to return the defective shipment.

2This week

  • Talk to the client's lawyer and hand over everything you have: the Turkish draft, the text you delivered, the correspondence. Any document showing that the parties agreed on 30 days will help in talks with the seller.
  • Translate the addendum correcting the contract right away and for free. The seller may not want to wreck a long-term relationship over a typo; take them the paper to sign, ready to go.
  • Offer your own share before the client asks: a refund of the translation fee and a contribution toward the legal costs arising from the mistake.
  • Write to the client about how the mistake happened and what you'll change from now on. An apology is believable only when paired with something concrete that says it won't happen again.

3Lasting fix

  • On legal texts, do a separate reading pass for numbers: periods, amounts, dates, party names. This pass doesn't look at the meaning of the text; it only compares source with target.
  • On contracts over a certain length, work with a second reader and build the cost into your price.
  • Sign a short service agreement with every client; write the limit of liability and take out professional liability insurance.
  • Read a text delivered at night once more in the morning, then send it.
The lesson

For someone who works alone, the most expensive mistake is reading their own text with their own eyes only once.
