340 orders for out-of-stock items were sold on campaign night

Stock syncing ran three hours late, and sold-out products kept selling on the marketplaces. Canceling is easy, but the seller rating will take the hit.

Tüyo Pet E-commerce · Istanbul · 22 employees · 14,000 orders a month

The problem

The October 4 World Animal Day campaign was Tüyo Pet's busiest night of the year. After midnight, stock syncing between the warehouse and the marketplaces ran three hours behind because of the load. Products that had sold out in the warehouse kept selling on two marketplaces.

In the morning Baran Çelik pulled up the numbers: 340 orders with no stock behind them. The easiest path is to cancel them all. But marketplaces count seller-initiated cancellations against the rating; if the rating drops, products fall in search results and eligibility for campaigns is at risk.

Customers, meanwhile, think their orders are confirmed. Every hour of delay means a few more “where is my order” messages.

By the numbers

Orders without stock: 340, across two marketplaces
Sync delay: about 3 hours
70% of revenue comes from the marketplaces
Monthly orders: about 14,000
What would you do?

Would you cancel all 340 orders and close the matter, or try to save them one by one?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Resist the reflex to cancel in bulk and split the 340 orders into three groups: those the supplier can deliver within 48 hours, those where you can offer the customer a substitute product, and those that truly can't be fulfilled.
  • Write to customers before they ask: say plainly what happened and when the order will ship, or what options you are offering. Add a small make-good for those who are waiting (a discount on their next order).
  • Report the situation to the marketplaces' seller support as a technical failure. Getting the record opened today strengthens your hand in a rating appeal.

2This week

  • Find the root cause of why syncing was late. Most likely it hit a request limit under campaign load; that's a weakness that doesn't show on normal days.
  • Pin down the emergency shipment arrangement with suppliers: which products can arrive in how many hours, and at what cost.
  • Put together the balance sheet of the incident: orders saved, orders canceled, make-goods given, effect on the rating.

3Lasting fix

  • On campaign days, don't open part of your stock (say, 10%) to the marketplaces; this buffer absorbs sync delays.
  • Set up an automatic alert when sync delay passes a set threshold, and temporarily halt sales of best-selling products.
  • Apply a checklist before every major campaign: load test, buffer stock, on-call person, supplier confirmation.
The lesson

Systems break most on the day you need them most; a campaign plan has to include a failure plan.
