The top-earning dentist is leaving to open a clinic two streets away

The implant dentist who alone brings in 27 percent of revenue leaves in two months. Do the patients belong to the clinic or to the dentist?

Mirva Diş Klinikleri Healthcare · Ankara · 38 employees · 3 clinics, 11 dentists

The problem

Mirva's most experienced implant dentist told the founding partners that in two months he will leave and open his own clinic two streets away. 27 percent of the group's revenue comes from treatments performed by this dentist.

The dentist has 86 ongoing patients, most of them in the middle of the implant process. A significant share of the patients are attached to the dentist rather than the clinic. It is rumored that two assistants are also thinking of going with him.

The non-compete clause in the contract is weakly written. Dr. Kaan Uras wants to take a tough legal route; Dr. Emine Soylu worries that this would frighten both the patients and the team.

By the numbers

Dentist's revenue share: 27 percent
Ongoing treatments: 86 patients
Time until departure: 2 months
Group: 3 clinics, 11 dentists
What would you do?

Would you fight the departing dentist or make a deal? What would you tell the 86 ongoing patients?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Don't act on reaction. Hold a calm exit conversation with the dentist and put in writing together how the two months will go. A well-managed departure keeps more patients than a lawsuit won.
  • Draw up the list of the 86 ongoing treatments: at what stage, how long they will take, who can take them over.
  • Tell the team the news before gossip does. Uncertainty costs you more people than the departure itself.

2This week

  • For ongoing treatments, respect the patient's choice and say so openly: they can continue their treatment here with another dentist or continue with their dentist. A patient held by force tells everyone around them.
  • Set a written method for transferring patient records that complies with personal data regulations; settle it with a lawyer.
  • Talk one-on-one with the two assistants: what do they find lacking, what would it take for them to stay? At the same time start the search for a new implant dentist.

3Lasting fix

  • Set a ceiling on any single dentist's share of revenue (for example 20 percent) and, when it is exceeded, build up a second dentist in that field.
  • Build a structure that attaches the patient to the clinic: a patient coordinator, a single contact line, post-treatment follow-up. If the patient knows only the dentist, when the dentist goes the patient goes too.
  • Design a partnership or profit-sharing model for senior dentists. People often leave not for money but to own their own practice.
The lesson

Dependence on one person is managed before that person leaves; on the day they go, you can only limit the damage.
