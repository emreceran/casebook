HealthcareOctober 9, 2026

In four of every ten breakdowns, the engineer has to make a second visit

The first-visit fix rate is 58 percent. While the engineer waits for parts, the hospital's device is out of service for an average of 7.4 days.

Sermedon Tıbbi Cihaz Healthcare · Ankara · 28 employees · 9 service engineers, 640 installed devices

The problem

Sermedon's technical service receives an average of 210 breakdown calls a month. Barış Kılınç pulled the records for the last six months: only 122 of them, or 58 percent, are resolved on the first visit. In the remaining 88 calls the engineer sees the fault, orders a part and returns an average of six days later. The device is out of service for an average of 7.4 days in the meantime.

Each extra visit costs about 4,800 TL in travel and engineer time; 422,400 TL a month. Maintenance contracts promise that the device will be operational 95 percent of the year. In the last three months, three hospitals deducted a total of 380,000 TL in penalties because this promise was not kept.

Barış Kılınç sees the solution in the warehouse: buy 5 million TL of additional spare parts. Hülya Demirci puts the numbers on the table: the warehouse already holds 12 million TL of parts across 1,900 items, and 1,100 of them haven't been used even once in the last two years. In her view the problem isn't too few parts, but that the wrong parts are on the shelf and the right ones are abroad.

By the numbers

Monthly breakdown calls: 210; 122 resolved on the first visit (58 percent)
Average downtime of a device: 7.4 days
Cost of extra visits: 422,400 TL a month (88 visits, 4,800 TL per visit)
Warehouse: 12 million TL, 1,900 items; 1,100 items idle for two years
What would you do?

Would you buy another 5 million TL of parts for the warehouse? If not, how would you make sure the engineer goes with the right part on the first trip?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Hold off on the parts order and first count the reasons for the 88 second visits: in how many the part was missing, in how many the wrong part was brought, in how many the engineer couldn't reach the device. The fix for each of the three reasons is different.
  • For breakdowns where parts were awaited, list which parts were awaited. Most likely a few dozen items explain the bulk of the waiting.

2This week

  • Put the most frequently awaited parts not in the warehouse but in the engineer's vehicle. Every engineer should carry a fixed set of parts based on the devices they look after.
  • Have the person taking the call narrow down the fault on the first phone call: device model, error code on the screen, a photo or a short video. The engineer should know what they will face before setting out.
  • For wasted visits caused by the hospital, get appointment confirmation: will the device be free at that time, will the authorized person be there?
  • Set aside the stock that hasn't moved in two years; determine what can be returned to the manufacturer, what can be sold to other service providers and what should be scrapped.

3Lasting fix

  • Manage stock by breakdown records, not by value. Let the last 12 months of breakdown data, not purchasing, decide which parts stay on the shelf.
  • Track the first-visit fix rate by engineer and device model every month; set the target at 80 percent.
  • For the most critical devices under maintenance contracts, propose keeping a small set of parts at the hospital and include this in the contract price.
The lesson

A full warehouse doesn't mean the right part is there; stock is measured not by value but by breakdowns.
