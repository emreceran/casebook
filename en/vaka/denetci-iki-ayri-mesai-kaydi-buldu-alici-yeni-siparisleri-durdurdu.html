The auditor found two sets of overtime records, and the buyer stopped new orders

A social compliance audit found 64 employees over the legal overtime limit, and payroll and turnstile records didn't match. The Scandinavian buyer gave 60 days.

Saruhan Tekstil Textiles · Denizli · 210 employees · 40 looms, 12 million euros in annual exports

The problem

The Scandinavian chain that gives Saruhan 2.2 million euros a year in business has its suppliers audited by an independent auditing firm. The September audit turned up two findings. First: 64 of the 210 employees exceeded the legal overtime limit of 270 hours a year in the first eight months of the year. Second, and more serious: working hours in the turnstile records were higher than those in payroll. Overtime that didn't go through payroll had been paid in cash.

The buyer suspended new orders. Open orders worth 340,000 euros already in production will be completed, but if a corrective plan and a successful follow-up audit don't arrive within 60 days, the business ends. Of the 64 people over the limit, 51 are in the garment department. There, monthly overtime per person climbed to 41 hours in August; the monthly equivalent of the legal limit is 22.5 hours.

Production Manager Ferhat Bozkurt is furious: the same buyer wants delivery in four weeks, changes colors and quantities after placing the order, and then looks at the overtime. In his view, if the records are kept properly, the audit will be passed. Nesrin Tuna objects: the problem isn't on paper, it's on the shop floor; if double records turn up again in the second audit, it won't just be this buyer, but every buyer working with the same auditing firm who will leave.

By the numbers

Employees over the legal overtime limit: 64 (51 in the garment department)
Monthly overtime per person in the garment department: 41 hours (the monthly equivalent of the limit is 22.5 hours)
Buyer's annual business: 2.2 million euros
Time to fix: 60 days
What would you do?

Would you fix the records or the overtime? What would you tell the buyer who wants delivery in four weeks?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • End the cash overtime payments today. Every hour worked should show up the same way on the turnstile and in payroll. To an auditor, excess overtime is a finding you can fix; double records are a trust problem.
  • Send the buyer a short letter: we accept the findings, we have reduced the records to one set, and we will submit the plan within ten days. Don't defend yourself, give a date.

2This week

  • Work out where and why the overtime has piled up. If 51 of the 64 people are in the garment department, the problem is one department's capacity, not the factory's.
  • Set up a second shift in the garment department. If the same machines run with two crews a day, the work loaded onto overtime goes back to regular hours.
  • List the changes the buyer made after placing orders over the last six months: how many times, how many days before the deadline, how many hours of overtime each caused. This list is the evidence for the lead-time discussion with the buyer.
  • Tell the employees the situation directly. An operator who knows their take-home pay will shrink once overtime stops will leave for another factory at the first opportunity.

3Lasting fix

  • Track weekly working hours per person, and send a warning to production planning for any employee approaching the limit. See the limit during the week, not at year-end.
  • When accepting orders, calculate capacity without overtime. A lead time that can only be met with overtime is a lead time that can't be met.
  • Add a change rule to buyer contracts: a change made less than three weeks before production extends the lead time by the same amount.
The lesson

What costs you in an audit is not the overtime itself but the second ledger kept to hide it.
