One in every five appointments goes empty

The no-show rate is 22 percent. The dentist is ready, the chair is empty; about 336,000 TL a week in capacity goes unused.

Mirva Diş Klinikleri Healthcare · Ankara · 38 employees · 3 clinics, 11 dentists

The problem

About 640 appointments a week are booked across Mirva's three clinics. When Clinic Manager Sibel Aydın pulled the records for the last three months, she saw that 22 percent of patients didn't show up without notice: about 140 empty chair-hours a week.

The average value of a chair-hour is 2,400 TL. The dentist and assistant are in the clinic at that hour, costs are running, and there is no revenue. At the same time, new patients are being given appointments two weeks out.

The reminder consists of a single short text message sent only one day ahead. For free first-exam appointments, the no-show rate reaches 41 percent. Dr. Kaan Uras wants to charge no-shows a fee; Tolga Eren thinks this will drive patients away.

By the numbers

No-show rate: 22 percent (about 140 appointments a week)
Chair-hour value: 2,400 TL on average
Unused capacity: about 336,000 TL a week
No-shows at free first exams: 41 percent
What would you do?

Would you charge patients who don't show up? If not, how would you fill the empty chairs?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • First, separate out who isn't coming. Most likely the problem isn't across all patients but concentrated in two groups: free first exams and appointments booked more than three weeks ahead.
  • Design the solution around these two groups. Penalizing patients who come regularly upsets good patients without solving the problem.

2This week

  • Make the reminder a two-step process: a message three days ahead asking for confirmation, and a short reminder on the morning of the appointment. Call patients who haven't confirmed the day before.
  • Create a waiting list. Patients who want an earlier appointment go on the list; when a cancellation or unconfirmed appointment comes up, they get called first. That way the vacated chair gets filled.
  • In time slots with high no-show rates (usually the first appointments in the morning and the last in the evening), book a limited number of extra appointments.

3Lasting fix

  • Instead of a free first exam, take a small deposit that is deducted from the treatment. Not to make money, but to make people take the appointment seriously.
  • Don't give advance bookings to patients who have twice failed to show up without notice; accept them only if there is a same-day opening.
  • Track the no-show rate monthly by clinic and dentist; set the target at 10 percent.
The lesson

An empty appointment is like unsold stock, except it can't be carried over to the next day.
