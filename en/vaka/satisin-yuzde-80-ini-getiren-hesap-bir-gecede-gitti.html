The account that brought in 80% of sales vanished overnight

The social media account with 212,000 followers was hijacked, followers were scammed, and the account was shut down. The addresses for 140 pending orders were also left in that account's inbox.

Atölye Nar E-commerce · Izmir · 12 employees · 1,900 orders a month

The problem

On Friday night, Aslı Yücel received a message titled “your account will be closed for copyright infringement.” A password was entered through the link in the message; the account had no two-step verification. Whoever took over the account sent followers a fake discount announcement; 17 people paid a total of 46,000 TL. After complaints, the platform shut the account down.

80% of Atölye Nar's monthly revenue of 1.9 million TL came from this account; for every day the account stays closed, about 51,000 TL in sales is lost. Most orders were taken by message; the addresses of 140 orders waiting to be prepared were left in the inbox. All that remains is the 6,400-person email list on the website.

The platform says appeals can take up to 30 days. Founder Zeynep Narin doesn't want to wait; she favors opening a new account right away and starting from scratch. Aslı Yücel fears that a new account would weaken the appeal and that followers would take it for a fake as well. One more question is on the table: what do you tell the 17 customers who lost their money to a scammer?

By the numbers

Account: 212,000 followers, 80% of sales
Daily sales loss: about 51,000 TL
Followers scammed: 17 people, 46,000 TL in total
Pending orders whose addresses are stuck in the inbox: 140
What would you do?

Would you wait for the old account to come back or open a new one? Would you reimburse the 17 scammed customers?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Announce it right away through the one solid channel you have: tell the 6,400-person email list and the website's home page that the account has been hijacked and that nobody should send money in response to a message from that account.
  • Start the appeal today, file a criminal complaint with the prosecutor's office, and keep the application numbers. An official complaint document speeds things up in the appeal.
  • Find the 140 pending orders from payment records, not from messages. The bank statement has names and amounts; reach the customers from there.

2This week

  • Do both: while the appeal is pending, open a second account clearly labeled “temporary account” and announce it on the website, by email, and inside shipped packages.
  • Call the 17 scammed people one by one. You may not legally owe them, but they sent the money trusting you. Make good the loss with a gift voucher or product.
  • Stop taking orders by message. Have every post on the temporary account point to the website; keep addresses and payments in a place you control.
  • Turn on two-step verification on all of the company's accounts and change the passwords.

3Lasting fix

  • Make growing the website's share of revenue a goal. A business that depends on one account for 80% is at the mercy of the platform that owns that account.
  • Collect an email and phone number with every order and back up the customer list regularly. Followers are rented; the customer list is yours.
  • Train everyone with access to the account on spotting fake messages, and separate account permissions by person.
The lesson

A follower on someone else's platform is not yours; what is yours is the customer whose address you know.
