Raw milk up 20 percent, but the selling price to the supermarket chain is fixed until year-end

A liter of milk went from 19 TL to 22.80 TL. The contract with the chain that takes 45% of output has a fixed price; Yaylacık is now selling to this customer at a loss.

Yaylacık Süt Ürünleri Food · Balıkesir · 75 employees · 40 tons of milk a day, 186 farmers

The problem

The price of a liter of raw milk rose from 19 TL to 22.80 TL in early September. Yaylacık processes 40,000 liters of milk a day; 18,000 liters of that becomes cheese and yogurt going to a single supermarket chain. In the contract with the chain, the selling price is fixed until December 31.

Sales and Finance Manager Gülşen Yaylacık worked out the numbers. The extra milk cost on products going to the chain is 68,400 TL a day, about 2.05 million TL a month. Sales to this customer run at 24 million TL a month, and at a 7% margin they used to leave 1.68 million TL in profit. Now the same sales book a loss of about 370,000 TL a month. There are 104 days left in the year; the total extra cost is 7.1 million TL.

Cevdet Yaylacık wants to call the chain's bluff: if there is no price increase, shipments stop. Gülşen Yaylacık reminds him of the contract: short deliveries carry a penalty of 10% of the invoice value, and another producer will take the emptied shelf within a week. In her view, the chain should be approached not with threats but with numbers and options.

By the numbers

Raw milk: from 19 TL to 22.80 TL per liter (20%)
Milk going to the chain: 18,000 liters a day; extra cost about 2.05 million TL a month
Monthly sales to the chain: 24 million TL; previous profit: 1.68 million TL a month
Time left until the contract ends: 104 days
What would you do?

Would you threaten to halt shipments, sell at a loss for 104 days, or put a different offer on the table?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Don't halt shipments. The 10% penalty and the lost shelf are far more expensive than a loss of 370,000 TL a month. Calling a bluff is a game for someone with other buyers in hand.
  • Put the numbers on a single page: the old and new price of milk, the liters going to the chain, the monthly extra cost, the previous profit. The chain's category manager also has to take a justification to their own boss.

2This week

  • Go into the meeting with three options, not a single request. First: an 8.5% price adjustment that covers the entire extra cost.
  • Second: a smaller increase plus a payment term cut from 60 days to 30. For the chain the price tag changes little; for you the cash arrives sooner.
  • Third: change the product mix on the shelf. In place of low-margin tinned white cheese, ask for shelf space for higher-margin products like strained yogurt and sliced kashar.
  • Assume the chain's other dairy suppliers have come in with the same increase. If the increase has hit all producers, the chain swapping you for someone else doesn't solve its problem; say so in the meeting.

3Lasting fix

  • Don't sign a one-year fixed price. Add an adjustment clause, applied every three months, that ties the selling price to the published raw milk price.
  • Bring any single customer's share down from 45%. With a customer that takes half your milk you don't negotiate, you beg.
  • Calculate profitability by customer and by product once a month; see which sale is booking a loss in the week the increase arrives.
The lesson

A producer whose input price is set by someone else can't fix its selling price for a year.
