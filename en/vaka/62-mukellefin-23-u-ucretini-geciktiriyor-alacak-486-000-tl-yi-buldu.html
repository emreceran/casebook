23 of 62 clients are late paying their fees, and the money owed has reached 486,000 TL

Nurcan Balkış bills 372,000 TL in fees every month, but 23 clients aren't paying. Most of the debtors are shopkeepers she knows, and their tax returns are still filed on time.

Nurcan Balkış Mali Müşavirlik Self-employed · Konya · 2 employees · 62 clients

The problem

At the end of August, Nurcan Balkış sat down for the first time and wrote out who owed what. She bills her 62 clients a total of 372,000 TL a month in fees. Twenty-three of them have been late paying, and the accumulated receivable stands at 486,000 TL, about 1.3 times a month's revenue. Six clients haven't paid anything for four months or longer; their debt alone comes to 278,000 TL.

The office rent, the intern's salary, the software licenses and her own social security contributions come to 118,000 TL a month, and none of it can wait. Nurcan Balkış finds it hard to ask for money. Of the six clients who owe the most, four are neighboring shopkeepers or family friends; one sends sweets to the office every holiday but hasn't sent a fee in five months.

Her intern, Büşra Altınok, suggests pushing the debtors' work to the back of the queue. Nurcan Balkış won't hear of it: “If I file late, the penalty goes to the client, and they'll still hold me responsible.” Dropping a client isn't easy either; in Konya the shopkeepers all know each other, and “she dropped us because she asked for her money” spreads fast.

By the numbers

Monthly fees billed: 62 clients, 372,000 TL
Overdue receivables: 23 clients, 486,000 TL
Unpaid for four months or longer: 6 clients, 278,000 TL
Monthly office expenses: 118,000 TL
What would you do?

Would you stop work for a debtor you know, ask for the money in writing, or just wait until they pay?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Split the receivables into three groups by how long you've been waiting: one month, two to three months, and four months or more. Someone one month behind has usually just forgotten; someone four months behind is now using you like a loan. You can't say the same sentence to both.
  • Don't call, write. Send all 23 clients the same statement on the same day, in the same format: which month's fee, how much, and the total. A written statement isn't a personal reproach, it's a document, and it's easy to send to someone you know.

2This week

  • Meet the six clients with debts older than four months in person and offer two options: pay part of the debt now and the rest in written installments, or the contract ends and the books are handed over with a signed record.
  • Don't use the tax return as leverage. As long as the contract runs, the work is done in full; the relationship with a client who doesn't pay ends at termination of the contract, not on filing day.
  • Put payment plans in writing and have both sides sign. A verbal “we'll sort it out at the end of the month” is the same sentence again three months later.
  • Make an offer to the 39 clients who pay on time as well: have the fee collected on the fifth of each month by automatic bank payment order.

3Lasting fix

  • Update the receivables list on the first working day of every month. A list you look at once a year is one you discover on the day it reaches 486,000 TL.
  • Write a clear clause into new contracts: if a fee goes unpaid for two months in a row, the contract is terminated.
  • Don't do the fee reminders yourself. Have the statement go out automatically from the office address on the same day every month, so the one asking is the calendar, not you.
The lesson

Asking an acquaintance for money is hard, and every month you don't ask makes it harder to ask the next.
