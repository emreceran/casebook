A new tax-reporting rule arrived and 342 subscribers left in three months

Since April, some self-employed people have had to report digitally to the tax authority every quarter. Tallowfield had decided not to build the feature; monthly churn rose from 1.4% to 3.3%.

Tallowfield Software Software · Leeds, United Kingdom · 46 employees · 3,800 subscribing businesses

The problem

From April 2026, self-employed people in the UK with an annual income above 50,000 pounds have had to keep their income and expenses digitally and report them to the tax authority every quarter through approved software. The deadline for the first report was 7 August. We had discussed the rule two years ago and made a decision: “We're job-management software, not accounting software.” About 1,150 of our subscribers fell within the rule.

In April, May and June, 342 subscribers left; in an ordinary quarter the figure is around 160. Callum Hartigan's support team was answering the same question about 40 times a day: “Will I be able to file my tax return from your software?” Of the 205 people who filled in the exit survey, 123 gave the same reason: they were moving to software that did the filing, and they didn't want to pay for two separate packages. By Deborah Ainscough's sums, the extra 182 subscribers lost over three months meant 7,644 pounds a month in revenue.

Founder Graham Pellow reversed his decision and wanted the reporting feature built in-house: “If we send customers to someone else's product, they don't come back.” Head of Product Priya Raghavan ran the numbers: four developers, six months, around 180,000 pounds, plus the tax authority's own approval process. There was no way it would be ready for August. Her proposal was to push invoice and expense records automatically into the two accounting packages our customers use most: two developers, six weeks, around 30,000 pounds.

By the numbers

April–June cancellations: 342 subscribers; the usual quarterly figure is around 160
Subscribers covered by the rule: about 1,150 (30% of 3,800 subscribers)
Exit survey: 123 of 205 responses (60%) cited tax reporting
Building our own feature: 6 months, 180,000 pounds; export to two accounting packages: 6 weeks, 30,000 pounds
What would you do?

Would you build the feature yourself, tie customers to another piece of software, or say “that's not our job” and accept the ones who go?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Give customers a clear answer today. A tradesperson using software that says “we're looking into it” won't wait as the filing deadline approaches; they'll move to software that has an answer.
  • List the 1,150 subscribers covered. This is the problem of that group, not of all 3,800; the announcement and the fix should go to them first.

2This week

  • Look at what the customer actually wants. The line in the exit survey isn't “I want you to file my return”, it's “I don't want to pay for two packages”. What they want is not to enter the same invoice twice.
  • Decide by the calendar: the right solution that takes six months is no substitute for an adequate one that takes six weeks. The filing deadline is in August.
  • Build the export for the accounting packages your customers already use. Don't guess which ones; count them from support records and the survey.
  • Write to the 342 who left with the date of the fix. Someone who has already moved to new software and migrated their data won't come back, but someone still deciding will.

3Lasting fix

  • Track regulatory changes as a separate line in the product plan: which rule, from which date, how many customers it affects.
  • When you decide “that's not our job”, also write down where the customer will do that job. If the answer is “somewhere else, having left us”, the decision is incomplete.
  • Monitor churn separately by customer segment. A wave that starts in a group making up 30% of the base shows up in the overall average two months late.
The lesson

You can decide not to do a job yourself, but making sure the customer doesn't have to leave you to get it done is still your job.
