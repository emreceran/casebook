The album took 15 weeks and the couple gave one star: 14 more weddings are in the queue

The contract says eight weeks, but mid-season the delivery time stretched to 15 weeks. Tolga Erinç edits every wedding's photos himself and doesn't want to hand the work to anyone.

Tolga Erinç Fotoğraf Self-employed · Antalya · 1 employees · 45 weddings a year

The problem

Tolga Erinç shot 27 weddings between May and August. At the end of August he had delivered 13 of them and 14 were waiting. The delivery time in the contract is eight weeks. The weddings he shot in May he delivered in six weeks; the albums he delivered in August had waited 15 weeks.

Selecting and editing the photos from one wedding takes 22 hours. During the season, while shooting two or three weddings a week, he can set aside at most 25 hours a week for editing, which means a little more than one wedding a week. Every week he shoots, one more wedding is added to the queue.

On August 24, a couple who received their album in the 15th week wrote a one-star review on his map listing: “The photos are beautiful, but for three months the only answer to our messages was ‘almost done.’” The review shows at the top. The same week, two couples who had booked a meeting canceled their appointments. Tolga Erinç is thinking of applying to have the review removed. He's against outsourcing the editing: “If someone else touches my colors, it's no longer my photo.”

By the numbers

Weddings shot between May and August: 27; 13 delivered, 14 waiting
Delivery time in the contract 8 weeks, actual time in August 15 weeks
Editing one wedding takes 22 hours; time available per week is 25 hours
Meetings canceled after the one-star review: 2
What would you do?

Would you try to get the review removed, hand the editing to someone else, or take fewer weddings?

Suggested solution AI suggestion

1First 24 hours

  • Answer the review with a date, not a defense: an apology, the reason for the delay in one sentence, and what you'll do from now on. The couple who wrote the review won't be the ones reading that answer; the couples who will ask you for a quote will.
  • Write to the 14 waiting couples today and give each of them a real delivery date. “Almost done” isn't a date; the complaint in the review is as much about that answer as about the delay.

2This week

  • Break the 22 hours into parts. Culling bad frames and bringing light and color to a baseline isn't your signature; the final color and the selection are your signature. The first part can be handed off.
  • Prepare your own color settings as a file and give it to the person who'll do the editing. What preserves your style is that settings file as much as your eye.
  • Send each couple a small preview selection the week after the wedding. A couple with 30 photos in hand will wait patiently for the album.
  • Call the couple who wrote the review. Don't try to get the review deleted; listen, and offer something concrete to make up for the delay.

3Lasting fix

  • Write the contract's delivery time according to the season: long in season, short out of season. Eight weeks you can't keep is worse than ten weeks you can.
  • Put a cap on the number of weddings you'll take per week, or line up editing help at the start of the season, not after the queue has reached 14.
  • Write down the number of jobs waiting and the age of the oldest one every Monday. The first job past eight weeks is the alarm.
The lesson

Clients are angrier about uncertainty than about delay; a job you can't give a date for is uncertain for you, too.
