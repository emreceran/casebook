Of the 41 who left in winter, only 19 came back in spring

Mavi Koy Otel Tourism · Muğla · 55 employees · 64 rooms
The problem

The hotel is closed five months of the year. Trained seasonal staff find other work in winter and don't return in spring; every season starts almost from scratch.

How it was solved

Instead of keeping 12 people on half pay, found winter work for eight key employees: four maintained the hotel, four worked at a ski hotel. The return rate rose from 46 percent to 76 percent.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

Mavi Koy is closed from November to the end of March. 41 of its 55 employees are seasonal; they are signed off in October and called back in April. Of the 41 called this spring, only 19 returned. The rest had found work in the city or at facilities that stay open year-round over the winter, and stayed there.

By Pelin Arıkan's calculation, recruiting a new employee, training them and the time until they learn the job cost the hotel about 35,000 TL per person: 770,000 TL for 22 new people. The guests pay the real price: in the first six weeks of the season, complaints run double those of summer, and the April–May score stays at 8.2.

Nesrin Karaöz wants to keep the best 12 people through winter at half pay. The average salary is 36,000 TL; 12 people, five months, half pay comes to 1,080,000 TL. Pelin Arıkan says a closed hotel can't afford to pay 12 people sitting idle for five months. She is also the first to point out that every winter 620,000 TL is paid to outside contractors for painting, maintenance and renovation.

By the numbers

Seasonal employees who returned this spring: 19 of 41 (46 percent)
Cost to the hotel of a new employee: about 35,000 TL per person, 770,000 TL for 22 people
Cost of keeping 12 people at half pay for five months: 1,080,000 TL
Paid to outside contractors for winter maintenance: 620,000 TL

What did Mavi Koy Otel do?

Nesrin Karaöz identified eight key employees. Four stayed at the hotel through winter on full pay and did painting, furniture, garden and pool work: their pay cost 720,000 TL, but 500,000 TL of the 620,000 TL of work previously given to outside contractors was done in-house. The other four went on a temporary assignment to a ski hotel in Erzurum, and that hotel paid their salaries through the winter. The rest of the seasonal team were promised a 10,000 TL bonus for returning in April and staying until the end of June, and contracts were signed in October.

The following spring 31 of the 41 reported for work: all eight key employees and 23 of the other 33. Net winter pay came to 220,000 TL, return bonuses to 230,000 TL and the cost of ten new employees to 350,000 TL; 800,000 TL in total. In other words, the hotel opened the season with a trained team for roughly the 770,000 TL it had spent the previous year just on training new staff. The April–May score rose from 8.2 to 8.8.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Don't look at the 41 as a single list. Single out those who are hardest to replace: the front desk chief, the housekeeping chief, the maintenance foreman, two key names in the kitchen. The problem isn't everyone, it's these eight or ten people not coming back.
  • Talk one-on-one with these people before October: what are they doing in winter, how much do they earn, what stops them from returning in spring.

2This week

  • List the winter maintenance work and see which jobs your own team can do: painting, furniture repair, garden, pool, plumbing. Part of the 620,000 TL going outside can become winter pay for key staff.
  • Talk to a facility that is open in winter: a ski hotel, a thermal hotel, a city hotel. The months you are closed are their busiest; staff work there for five months and come back in April.
  • Design a return bonus for the rest of the seasonal team: a fixed amount for everyone who starts in April and stays until the end of June.
  • Sign next season's contracts in October, when people are signed off. A phone call in April is an invitation; a signature in October is a job.

3Lasting fix

  • Move the key staff onto annual contracts; let maintenance, training and preparation for the next season be their work in winter.
  • Don't cut ties with the seasonal team over the winter months: a get-together in January, the season calendar in February, a start date in March.
  • Measure the return rate every spring and make it a management target. A season that begins at 46 percent spends its first six weeks on training.
The lesson

When the season ends, the hotel closes, but the team doesn't; either you keep them or someone else does.
