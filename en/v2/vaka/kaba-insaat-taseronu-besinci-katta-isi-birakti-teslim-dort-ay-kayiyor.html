The rough construction subcontractor walked off on the fifth floor, delivery slips four months

Atabey Yapı Construction · Antalya · 60 employees · About 180 homes a year, 3 construction sites
The problem

On a 72-apartment project, the formwork and rebar crew didn't show up at the site one morning. The contracts for the 58 apartments already sold carry a 25,000 TL penalty for each month of delay.

How it was solved

It had the work assessed and gave the two blocks to two new subcontractors, and it told the buyers about the delay itself. The delay fell from four months to two.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

The subcontractor doing the rough construction on Atabey Yapı's 72-apartment Yeşilova Konutları project stopped work on the fifth floor of two nine-story blocks. The 42-person crew didn't turn up at the site on Monday morning. The subcontractor gives two reasons: after the labor wage increase, the unit price no longer makes the job viable, and it is owed a delayed progress payment of 3.2 million TL. According to Atabey's records, progress payments have been paid an average of 20 days late over the past six months.

Levent Karataş calculates that finding a new crew, taking over the work and making up the lost weeks will delay delivery by four months. 58 of the apartments are sold, and the contracts set the delivery date at June 30, 2027. There is a penalty of 25,000 TL per apartment for each month of delay: 5.8 million TL over four months.

Levent Karataş wants to cash in the subcontractor's 1.5 million TL guarantee right away and file a lawsuit. Rüstem Atabey is in favor of telling the buyers nothing for now: perhaps the gap can be closed, so let's not cause needless panic. Hande Özkök argues the opposite: buyers will see for themselves within a week that the site has stopped; a buyer who hears about the delay from a neighbor does not react the same way as one who hears it from the company.

By the numbers

Project: 72 apartments, 58 sold, contractual delivery June 30, 2027
Estimated delay: 4 months
Delay penalty: 25,000 TL per apartment per month, 5.8 million TL over four months
Subcontractor's overdue progress payment: 3.2 million TL; its guarantee: 1.5 million TL

What did Atabey Yapı do?

Levent Karataş had an independent expert measure the work done. No lawsuit was filed: the account was closed by deducting the 1.5 million TL guarantee from the old subcontractor's 3.2 million TL claim. The two blocks were given to two separate subcontractors, and the new crews started within three weeks. Their unit prices were 15% higher, which added 4.1 million TL to the project's cost.

Hande Özkök called the 58 buyers one by one, then sent written notice. Because the two blocks rose at the same time, the delay fell from four months to two; the penalty came to 2.9 million TL instead of 5.8 million TL. Of the 58 buyers, 44 agreed to have the penalty deducted from the last installment, 12 asked for cash, and two withdrew from their contracts. Atabey Yapı now pays progress payments every 15 days.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Have an independent expert measure and photograph the work done. Which floor, which axis, how much rebar and formwork are in place? If a new crew enters the site before this assessment, you will never settle accounts with the old subcontractor.
  • Don't hang up on the subcontractor. A lawsuit takes years, while the site waits every day. First find out what it would take for them to come back, or how the account could be settled.
  • The same day, request quotes from at least three formwork and rebar crews.

2This week

  • Split the work between the two blocks and give them to two separate subcontractors. You're not at the mercy of a single crew, both blocks rise at the same time, and part of the four-month delay is recovered.
  • Give the 58 buyers written information: what happened, what the new delivery estimate is, what their rights are under the contract. Don't give an optimistic date; a date postponed a second time costs far more than the first.
  • Offer buyers options for how the penalty will be paid: cash, or an offset against the last installment. An offset protects your cash, and for the buyer it is a certain credit.
  • Pay the new subcontractors their progress payments every 15 days, not once a month, and put it in the contract.

3Lasting fix

  • Measure the progress payment day inside the company. A progress payment that is 20 days late means a subcontractor's workers are paid 20 days late; the crew first slows down, then leaves.
  • On large jobs, split the rough construction among at least two subcontractors from the start.
  • Set the delivery date in the sales contract not at the last day of the work schedule but with a buffer added on top.
The lesson

A subcontractor rarely leaves in one morning; it leaves a little more with every month its progress payment is late.
