Month-end update: six hours of downtime, 1,840 faulty invoices

Kodmer Yazılım Software · Ankara · 34 employees · 5,200 subscriber businesses
The problem

The update released on the morning of the last business day of August took the system down for six hours and miscalculated VAT on the invoices of 312 customers. The technical team wants to fix it quietly.

How it was solved

Sent 312 customers the list of their faulty invoices, fixed all of them in three business days and refunded one month's fee. Banned updates at month-end.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

Monday, August 31, was the last business day of the month and the day small businesses issue the most invoices. Kodmer's technical team released an update that had been waiting for weeks at 8:00 a.m. At 10:10 a.m. the database locked up and the system couldn't be brought back until 4:10 p.m. The support line, which takes 140 calls on a normal day, received 1,150 calls that day.

The real problem became clear the next day. The update miscalculated the tax total on invoices containing more than one VAT rate. In the two hours and ten minutes the system stayed up, 312 customers issued 1,840 invoices. Of these, 1,310 had not yet been sent; 530 had reached their recipients and had to be canceled and reissued.

Technical Director Ozan Baysal says most of the differences don't exceed a few lira; in his view the invoices should be fixed quietly in the background, and there is no point in causing panic with an announcement. Sevgi Altıntaş objects: if a customer learns of the error from their accountant or their own customer, the problem stops being a software bug and becomes a question of trust. Kerem Odabaşı has to decide.

By the numbers

Outage: August 31, 6 hours between 10:10 a.m. and 4:10 p.m.
Faulty invoices: 1,840 across 312 customers; 530 reached their recipients
Calls to the support line: 1,150 (normal day: 140)
Monthly subscription revenue of the 312 affected customers: 296,400 TL

What did Kodmer Yazılım do?

Kerem Odabaşı rejected the quiet fix. On the evening of September 1, the 312 customers were sent the list of their own faulty invoices; their accountants were also informed separately. The 1,310 unsent invoices were fixed in the system with the customers' approval; a one-step cancel-and-reissue tool was written for the 530 invoices that had reached their recipients. All 1,840 invoices were corrected within three business days.

Affected customers were refunded one month's fee; 296,400 TL in total. Nine of the 312 customers canceled their subscriptions. Kodmer no longer releases updates on the last three and first three business days of the month, and opens each update to 5 percent of customers first. Through rehearsals, the time to roll back an update was cut from six hours to 25 minutes.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Write to the 312 customers the same day. Send each customer the list of their own faulty invoices: invoice number, wrong amount, correct amount. A generic announcement saying “a problem occurred” is useless in accounting software.
  • Fix the 1,310 unsent invoices in the system, but with the customer's approval, not without asking. Changing a document issued in their name without telling them is a second mistake.
  • Give the support team a one-page script so that everyone who calls hears the same explanation and the same timeline.

2This week

  • Reduce the cancel-and-reissue steps for the 530 invoices that reached their recipients to a single button; don't leave the customer alone with the regulations.
  • Send information separately to the customers' accountants. They do the month-end close; they will be the first to see the error.
  • Refund one month's fee to the 312 affected customers. The total is 296,400 TL; finding a replacement for a single lost customer costs 4,200 TL.
  • Produce a written account of the incident: what happened when, why it took six hours, why the rollback couldn't be done right away. Hour by hour, without looking for someone to blame.

3Lasting fix

  • Don't release updates on the last three and first three business days of the month. The customer's busiest day should be your quietest.
  • Open an update first to 5 percent of customers, watch for a day, then roll it out to everyone.
  • Rehearse the rollback in advance. Most of the six hours went to trying to roll back the update for the first time that day.
The lesson

In accounting software, an error fixed quietly stops being an error and becomes a secret; and secrets get out sooner or later.
