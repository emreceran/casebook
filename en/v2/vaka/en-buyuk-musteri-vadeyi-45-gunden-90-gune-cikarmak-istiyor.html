The biggest customer wants to stretch payment terms from 45 days to 90

Vargel Lojistik Logistics · Kocaeli · 85 employees · 42 tractor units, 3 warehouses
The problem

The customer that brings in 34% of revenue is doubling the payment period in the new contract. Accepting could eat the profit; refusing could cost the customer.

How it was solved

Instead of saying yes or no, it took a three-option counteroffer. They agreed on supplier financing through the customer's bank.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

In the contract renewal meeting, Vargel's biggest customer asked for just one change: the payment term will go from 45 days to 90. The purchasing manager says it is group policy and applies to all suppliers.

This customer is invoiced 9.2 million TL a month. Vargel, meanwhile, pays for fuel with 15-day terms and salaries at month-end. The extra 45 days of terms means about 13.8 million TL permanently sitting with the customer.

Finance Lead Derya Koç says they could only cover that money with a loan. Selim Vargel is afraid of losing a 12-year customer.

By the numbers

Customer's share of revenue: 34%
Monthly invoicing: 9.2 million TL
Extra working capital need: about 13.8 million TL
Loan cost 45% a year; the company's net profit margin is 6%

What did Vargel Lojistik do?

Derya Koç put the numbers on the table: a 90-day term meant about 6.2 million TL a year in financing cost, while the annual profit earned from this customer was 6.6 million TL. Selim Vargel invited the customer's operations director to the meeting and presented three options.

The customer accepted supplier financing: on paper the term is 90 days, but Vargel gets its money from the bank 10 days after the invoice. The cost is about a third of borrowing. A monthly fuel price difference clause was also added to the contract. Vargel wrote down a goal of cutting this customer's share of revenue to 25% within two years.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Don't answer at the table. First work out the number: financing 13.8 million TL at 45% interest comes to about 6.2 million TL a year. The net profit earned from this customer in a year is about 6.6 million TL.
  • In other words, if the term change is accepted as is, the customer stays on paper, but almost all of the profit goes to the bank. The decision should be debated with that sentence.

2This week

  • Take a counteroffer with options instead of a yes or a no. Option one: a 90-day term, with a 5.5% term surcharge on the price in return (the cost of 45 days of financing).
  • Option two: supplier financing through the customer's own bank. The customer pays in 90 days, Vargel gets its money early, and the interest is calculated at the customer's low borrowing cost.
  • Option three: meet at 60 days and pass through the fuel price difference monthly.
  • Go into the meeting not just with purchasing but, if possible, with the operations side. They are the ones who know the value of 12 years of trouble-free service.

3Lasting fix

  • Set a limit on any single customer's share of revenue (say, 25%) and steer your sales effort accordingly. Your bargaining power is only as great as your ability to stay on your feet without that customer.
  • In price quotes, calculate the payment term as part of the price; have a separate price table for each term.
  • Prepare a factoring or credit line now, not at the moment you need it.
The lesson

Payment terms are a price too; a term you don't negotiate is a discount you've given.
