The memory card failed: 460 of the wedding's 1,140 frames won't open

Tolga Erinç Fotoğraf Self-employed · Antalya · 1 employees · 45 weddings a year
The problem

The failed card holds the marriage ceremony and the family photos. Recovery costs 18,000 TL and the result is uncertain; the couple doesn't know anything yet.

How it was solved

He called the couple the next morning; the card went to recovery and 318 of the 460 frames came back. He reshot the family photos at his own expense and didn't take the remaining 20,000 TL.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

Tolga Erinç shot a wedding at a hotel in Kemer on August 29. When he got home that night and was transferring the cards to his computer, the second card wouldn't read. Of the 1,140 frames he shot that day, 680 are on the good card and 460 on the failed one. The ones on the failed card are the marriage ceremony and the group photos of the families.

His camera has two card slots, but he used the second slot not as a backup but to carry on when the first card filled up. The cards were four years old. A data recovery company wants 18,000 TL, gives ten days, and makes no promise about the result.

The couple paid 32,000 TL of the 52,000 TL package; the remaining 20,000 TL is due on delivery, and they leave for their honeymoon the next day. Tolga Erinç has two paths. Wait for the recovery result and talk based on that; maybe he'll never have to say anything. Or call right away and start the couple's honeymoon with this news. The second shooter he hired that day has 210 frames from the ceremony on his camera; but they are from a different angle and don't include the family photos.

By the numbers

Frames shot at the wedding: 1,140; 680 on the good card, 460 on the failed card
Data recovery fee: 18,000 TL, 10 days, result uncertain
Package price 52,000 TL; 32,000 TL paid, 20,000 TL due on delivery
Frames of the ceremony shot by the second shooter: 210

What did Tolga Erinç Fotoğraf do?

Tolga Erinç called the couple on the morning of August 30, before they left for the airport; he told them what had happened, the 680 frames he had, and that the card was going to recovery. The bride cried on the phone; the groom asked when they would hear. The card was delivered to the company the same day. Ten days later, 318 of the 460 frames were recovered; nearly the whole marriage ceremony was among them. Most of the 142 frames that didn't come back were the families' group photos.

The second shooter's 210 frames were added to the album, and nearly 60 photos taken by guests on their phones were collected. When the couple returned from their honeymoon, on September 12 the two families came together again in the garden of the same hotel and the family photos were reshot; 6,500 TL was spent on transport and refreshments. Tolga Erinç did not ask for the remaining 20,000 TL. Together with the recovery fee, the incident cost him 44,500 TL. He now writes every frame to both cards at once, has renewed his cards, and makes a copy to a laptop before the wedding is over.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Don't put the card in a camera or a computer again, don't format it, don't try recovery software. Every attempt reduces the frames that can be recovered. Take the card to the recovery company as it is.
  • Call the couple today. The difference between saying “we couldn't recover them” ten days from now and saying “there's a problem, here's what I'm doing” today is whether the couple will ever trust you again.
  • When you call, count what you have: 680 good frames, the second shooter's 210 frames, and the card going to recovery. Give the news and the plan in the same conversation.

2This week

  • Fill in what's missing from other sources: the second shooter, the video crew, the guests' phones. Ask the couple for a list and write to the guests yourself.
  • Recreate what won't come back. The ceremony can't be repeated, but the family photos can be reshot; gathering the family is your job and your expense.
  • Talk about the cost before the couple asks. Waiving the remaining payment is an apology if done before haggling starts; after the haggling, it is compensation.

3Lasting fix

  • On a two-slot camera, always use the second card as a backup; write every frame to both cards at once.
  • Give your cards a lifespan and write the date on them. A card that has gone through two seasons isn't used at a wedding.
  • Copy the cards to a laptop before leaving the venue. Don't travel home with a single copy.
  • Add a technical failure clause to the contract and get a quote for professional liability insurance.
The lesson

In work that can't be redone, every part without a backup will sooner or later decide for you.
