Two applications instead of six apprentices, and two of the grinding masters retire next year

Wendrath Antriebstechnik Manufacturing · Esslingen, Germany · 140 employees · 21 million euros in annual revenue
The problem

An apprenticeship programme that drew 31 young people five years ago got two applicants this spring. Four of the 11 people in tooth grinding are over 60.

How it was solved

Instead of raising the ad budget it brought students into the workshop: applications went from 2 to 14, and five apprentices started in September. Setup knowledge for 27 of the masters' 64 types has been written down.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

Wendrath takes on six apprentices every September; most of the young people it trains as machining technicians or industrial mechanics stay with the company afterwards. This March, the number of applications for the intake starting in September was two. In 2021 there were 31; the number has fallen every year since.

The problem is felt most in the tooth grinding department. Four of its 11 people are over 60, and two will retire by the end of 2027. According to Production Manager Jochen Brettschneider, it takes 18 months before a new operator can set up a grinding machine alone. Much of the setup knowledge for the 64 gear types the company makes is also not written down; it is in the masters' notebooks and in their heads.

Managing Director Henrik Wendrath proposes raising apprentice pay and doubling the advertising budget. Sabine Kollmer, head of finance, says the big factories in the area already pay more and that a pay race can't be won. Jochen Brettschneider's worry is different: the masters are busy getting orders out and have no time to spare for an apprentice.

By the numbers

Apprenticeship applications: 31 in 2021, 2 as of March 2026; places available: 6
Tooth grinding department: 11 people, 4 over 60, 2 retiring by the end of 2027
Time before a new operator can set up a machine alone: 18 months
Gear types with no written setup knowledge: 64

What did Wendrath Antriebstechnik do?

Between April and June Wendrath brought students from three schools into the workshop, running internships for a total of 23 students over two separate weeks. Each student took home a part they had turned themselves. After the internships nine students, plus three more from outside, applied; total applications rose to 14. Five apprentices started on 1 September; the sixth place stayed empty. A car mechanic and a warehouse worker were also taken into the grinding department with 18 months of in-house training. Apprentice pay was left untouched.

The grinding masters were given time every Wednesday afternoon to write setup sheets, and orders were planned around it. By early September the sheets for 27 of the 64 gear types, with photographs, were on the screen beside the machine. The two masters who will retire were offered two days a week of work after retirement; one accepted, the other hasn't decided yet.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • See that there are two separate problems: finding new people takes years, while the masters' knowledge will walk out the door within two. Deal first with the one that has a deadline.
  • Talk to the two masters who will retire. When are they thinking of leaving, would they want to come in a day or two a week afterwards, and what would they need in order to pass on what they know?

2This week

  • Rank the 64 gear types by order frequency and start having setup sheets written for the 20 most-produced types. Give the master a fixed half day every week for this; work taken off the schedule never gets done.
  • Before putting money into ads, look at where applicants heard about you. A fifteen-year-old doesn't read job ads; they've either seen the factory or they haven't.
  • Talk to the schools around you and bring students into the workshop: a one-day visit, a one-week internship. The student who makes a part at a machine and takes it home is the one who applies.
  • Don't open the door only to people coming from school. Design a shortened in-house training course for adults from other trades who are good with their hands.

3Lasting fix

  • Keep a five-year age table for each department: who retires when, and who is being trained to replace them. A retirement date is never a surprise.
  • Pair master and apprentice at the same machine and lower the master's output target accordingly. Teaching should be the job itself, not a favour added on top of the working day.
  • Offer retiring masters part-time work as a standing rule; that is how you buy the time to pass on the knowledge.
The lesson

The date a master retires is known years ahead; what was late was not the ad but the plan built around that date.
