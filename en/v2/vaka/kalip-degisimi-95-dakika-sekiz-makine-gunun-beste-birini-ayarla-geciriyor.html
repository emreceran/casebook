Mold changeover takes 95 minutes: eight machines spend a fifth of the day on setup

Kalıpsak Plastik Manufacturing · Bursa · 95 employees · 22 injection molding machines
The problem

Customers want smaller batches and mold changeovers have doubled. On eight small-tonnage machines, 41 hours a day are spent waiting on molds.

How it was solved

Instead of setting a minimum order, filmed the mold changeover and broke it into its parts. In three months the time fell from 95 minutes to 38; about 25 machine hours a day were recovered.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

Appliance makers no longer hold stock; large weekly orders have been replaced by small daily batches. On Kalıpsak's eight small-tonnage machines, daily mold changeovers rose from 12 to 26 in one year. One changeover takes 95 minutes on average.

The math is simple: 26 changeovers, 2,470 minutes, or about 41 machine hours a day. 21 percent of the eight machines' 192 daily hours pass without any production. A machine hour is worth 2,600 TL; the loss is about 107,000 TL a day.

İlker Dinç sees the solution in batch size: set a minimum order quantity, or run large batches and hold them in the warehouse. Müge Sarıkaya says customers won't accept a minimum quantity, that small batches are now the rule. Nurten Bozkurt is against stock too: the warehouse already holds 38 days of finished goods and cash is sitting there.

By the numbers

Mold changeovers: 26 a day on eight machines (12 a year ago)
Duration of one changeover: 95 minutes on average
Loss: about 41 machine hours a day, 107,000 TL
Finished goods stock in the warehouse: 38 days

What did Kalıpsak Plastik do?

İlker Dinç filmed one changeover and watched it with the team. Of the 95 minutes, 34 went to finding and moving the mold, hoses and fixtures, 22 to waiting for the mold to warm up on the machine, 21 to removing and mounting, and 18 to setup and first-piece approval. In other words, more than half of the time went to jobs that could also be done while the machine was running.

Kalıpsak spent 1.9 million TL on three preheating stations, mold carts and quick-connect fittings; each mold's settings were saved to the machine. In three months the changeover time fell to 38 minutes. 26 changeovers a day now take 16.5 hours; the roughly 25 machine hours recovered are worth 64,000 TL a day, and the investment paid for itself in a month. Because batches got smaller, the finished goods stock fell from 38 days to 24; no minimum order quantity was imposed on customers.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Shift the discussion from batch size to changeover time. Small batches are the customer's decision; the 95 minutes are yours.
  • Film one mold changeover from start to finish and watch it with the team. Not to blame anyone, but to see for the first time where the 95 minutes go.

2This week

  • Break the time into parts and ask one question about each: does this job have to be done while the machine is stopped? Finding, moving and preheating the mold can also be done while the machine is running.
  • Bring the next mold, its hoses and fixtures next to the machine before the previous job ends. A changeover cart and a checklist for each mold are often enough.
  • Preheat the mold at a separate station before mounting it on the machine. Waiting for it to warm up on the machine means using the most expensive heater there is.
  • Record the machine settings for every mold and give first-piece approval at the machine. Setting up from the foreman's memory every time increases both time and scrap.

3Lasting fix

  • Invest in quick-connect fittings and in standardizing mold dimensions; compare the investment with the machine hours gained.
  • Measure changeover time by machine and shift, and show it every week at the production meeting. Don't set single-digit minutes as the target; first cut it in half.
  • Rebuild the production plan around changeover time: as changeovers get shorter, make batches smaller and run down the stock.
The lesson

Small batches aren't the problem; what makes them expensive is the work done while the machine is stopped.
