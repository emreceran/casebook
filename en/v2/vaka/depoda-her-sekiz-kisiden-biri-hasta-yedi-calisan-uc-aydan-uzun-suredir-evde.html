One in eight people in the warehouse is off sick: seven employees have been at home for more than three months

Havenwijk Logistiek Logistics · Rotterdam, Netherlands · 96 employees · 42,000 square metres of warehouse, 28 tractor units
The problem

In January absence across the company was 9.2%. In the Netherlands the employer pays a sick employee's wages for two years; with the temporary workers brought in to replace them, the bill is 672,000 euros a year.

How it was solved

It made a return plan for every long-term sick employee, defined light duties and moved the heavy items to different shelf positions. Absence fell from 9.2% in January to 6.1% in August; five of the seven returned to work.

What happened?

In January 9.2% of Havenwijk's 96 employees were off work sick; among the 54 people in the warehouse the rate was 12.4%. Seven employees had been at home for more than three months. Five worked in the warehouse, and their complaints were of the back and shoulders. Operations Manager Marloes Hendriksen summed up the picture: “The ones left behind carry the load of those who've gone, and then it's their turn.”

In the Netherlands the employer must pay a sick employee at least 70% of wages for two years; the collective labour agreement Havenwijk falls under provides full pay for the first year. If it is found that not enough was done to get the employee back to work, this period can be extended by a further year. Finance Manager Ruben Smeets's figures: the annual wage cost of the seven employees is 308,000 euros, and the seven temporary workers from an agency to replace them cost 364,000 euros. A total of 672,000 euros.

Managing Director Pieter van Doorn kept his distance from the matter: “A sick person is a sick person, we're not doctors.” Shift supervisors also shied away from phoning sick employees; they didn't want to look as if they were checking up on them. So some employees had not heard a word from the company for weeks. What Marloes Hendriksen noticed was this: most of the 40 heaviest items in the warehouse were on the lowest and highest shelves, and were picked by hand.

By the numbers

Absence, January: 9.2% across the company, 12.4% in the warehouse
Sick for more than three months: 7 employees, 5 in the warehouse
Annual cost: 308,000 euros wages, 364,000 euros temporary workers; total 672,000 euros
Employer's obligation to pay wages: two years

What did Havenwijk Logistiek do?

In February, return plans for each of the seven employees were drawn up with the occupational physician, and nine light tasks were defined in the warehouse. Shift supervisors took a half-day training course; when an employee reports sick, the supervisor now phones them on the first day. Marloes Hendriksen had the 40 heaviest items moved to waist-height shelves and had two vacuum lifting aids bought for 31,000 euros. Picking of heavy items was put on rotation within the shift.

In August absence across the company was 6.1%. Of the seven long-term sick employees, three have returned to their own jobs full-time, two are working half days on light duties, and two are still at home. The number of temporary workers employed to replace them fell from seven to three; on an annual basis, that is 208,000 euros less on the agency bill. No new report of long-term illness with back or shoulder complaints has come in since February.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Find out when, and who, last spoke to each of the seven. For an employee nobody has called for weeks, returning to work gets harder every day.
  • Tell the shift supervisors what they may ask. You don't ask for a diagnosis; you ask how they are, what they need and what work they could do. The person calling is a colleague, not an inspector.

2This week

  • With the occupational physician, draw up a separate return plan for each long-term sick employee: which work, how many hours a day, starting when?
  • List the physically light jobs in the warehouse: labelling, returns checking, counting, planning support. If there is work that can be done without being fully fit, the employee doesn't have to wait at home.
  • Look at the cause of the complaints. If five people's backs and shoulders have given out in the same warehouse, the problem is not with the people but with the way the work is done. Move the heavy items onto shelves at waist height.
  • Compare the price of lifting aids with the cost of a single long-term illness.

3Lasting fix

  • Track absence by department every month and show short-term and long-term sickness separately; their causes and their remedies are different.
  • Make it a rule that a supervisor phones an employee who reports sick on the first day, and train supervisors for that conversation.
  • Rotate the heavy jobs within the shift; the same person should not pick the same heavy item for eight hours.
The lesson

Treating illness is not the employer's job; but fixing the work that makes people ill and keeping the way back open is.

    In long-term sickness, the first six weeks are the most decisive. An employee who loses touch with the workplace in that time is less likely to return with every month that passes. That's why the supervisor's call is valuable, but there is a limit: a supervisor can't ask for a diagnosis and can't speculate either. Asking “what work could you do” instead of “when are you coming back” is both lawful and more useful.

    That was the main subject of the supervisors' training. We made a four-question card for the call; none of the questions is about health, all are about the work and what the person needs. Once supervisors knew what to ask, they stopped shying away from phoning.
