Scrap doubles on the night shift

Demirtaş Döküm Manufacturing · Konya · 140 employees · 900 tons a month, two shifts
The problem

Same machine, same mold, same material. Daytime scrap is 3.8%, nighttime 7.9%. The difference is 885,000 TL a month.

How it was solved

Instead of penalizing, it looked at the data: defects clustered between 2:00 and 5:00 a.m. and were temperature-related. It gave the night shift a quality inspector and an experienced foreman; night scrap fell to 4.6%.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

At Demirtaş, production is split evenly between two shifts: about 450 tons per shift per month. When Quality Supervisor Elif Sarı broke scrap down by shift for the first time, an unexpected picture emerged: 3.8% during the day, 7.9% at night.

The 4.1-point gap means about 18.5 tons of defective castings a month. At a cost of 48,000 TL per ton, that comes to about 885,000 TL a month.

The night shift has no quality inspector, maintenance only comes on call, and the shift supervisor is eight months into the job. Production Manager Murat Kılınç thinks the night crew is 'slack' and wants to apply penalties.

By the numbers

Scrap: 3.8% during the day, 7.9% at night
Production per shift: about 450 tons a month
Extra scrap: about 18.5 tons a month
Cost: about 885,000 TL a month

What did Demirtaş Döküm do?

Elif Sarı broke down night scrap by hour and defect type. 60% of defects occurred between 2:00 and 5:00 a.m. and came from cold pours. The cause was found too: on the night shift, temperature was measured every two hours instead of hourly, so ladle waiting time grew longer.

Nobody was penalized. The night shift was given a quality inspector, shift handover was put in writing, and two experienced foremen were rotated onto nights. After six weeks, night scrap fell from 7.9% to 4.6%; that means about 710,000 TL a month.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Before looking for someone to blame, dig into the data: which defect type is the night scrap, at which hours, on which line? 'The night crew is slack' is not a diagnosis, it's a guess.
  • If defects cluster at certain hours (for example between 2:00 and 5:00 a.m.) and of a certain type (temperature-related), the problem is not people's intentions but what is missing at those hours.

2This week

  • List everything that exists during the day but not at night: quality inspector, maintenance technician, experienced foreman, engineer. The night shift often does the same job with half the support.
  • Give the night shift a quality inspector, or write the critical measurements into a specific operator's responsibility.
  • Put shift handover in writing: which setting changed, which machine has a problem, which batch is suspect. What the day shift knows doesn't pass to the night by word of mouth.
  • Rotate two experienced foremen onto the night shift; don't leave the eight-month supervisor alone.

3Lasting fix

  • Measure scrap daily by shift, line and defect type, and post it where everyone in the shop can see it.
  • Tie the bonus to plant-wide scrap. A bonus that pits shifts against each other encourages hiding information, not sharing it.
  • Give shift supervisors management training; a good foreman does not automatically make a good supervisor.
The lesson

The difference between shifts is more often a difference in support than a difference in people.
