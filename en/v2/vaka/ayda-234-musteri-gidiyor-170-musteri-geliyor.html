SoftwareSeptember 14, 2026

234 customers leave each month, 170 arrive

Kodmer Yazılım Software · Ankara · 34 employees · 5,200 subscriber businesses
The problem

Monthly churn rose from 2 percent to 4.5 percent in five months. For the first time the company is shrinking rather than growing, and marketing wants a discount.

How it was solved

Did not give a discount and called those who left: most had gone because they couldn't set up the software. Brought back phone setup support; the loss fell to 2.6 percent in three months.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

Kodmer has 5,200 subscribers and wins about 170 new customers each month. In April the share of subscribers canceling was 2 percent: 104 customers a month. In August the rate rose to 4.5 percent: 234 customers. In April the company grew by 66 customers a month; in August it shrank by 64.

Sales and Marketing Manager İpek Soyer sees the cause in price: two competitors have brought out cheaper packages. Her proposal is a 20 percent discount for all subscribers and a larger advertising budget. With monthly revenue at 4.94 million TL, that discount means 988,000 TL a month. Winning a new customer already costs 4,200 TL.

Customer Support Manager Sevgi Altıntaş points to something else: of the 234 customers who left in August, 148 were in the first three months of their subscription. In March, to cut costs, phone setup support was removed and replaced with training videos. According to Sevgi Altıntaş, those who leave are not leaving because they find it expensive, but because they never managed to start using the software. No one called the customers who left.

By the numbers

Monthly churn: from 2 percent (104 customers) to 4.5 percent (234 customers)
New customers per month: about 170; acquisition cost 4,200 TL per customer
Of the 234 customers who left in August, 148 were in their first three months (63 percent)
Cost of a 20 percent discount for everyone: 988,000 TL a month

What did Kodmer Yazılım do?

Sevgi Altıntaş's team called the 60 customers who left in August; 41 spoke with them. Twenty-three said they hadn't been able to set up or start using the software, nine cited price; six had businesses that had closed and three had switched to a competitor. The discount was shelved. The phone setup support removed in March was brought back for new subscribers with a team of three; the team's monthly cost is about 210,000 TL.

The share of new customers issuing their first invoice within 14 days rose from 38 percent to 71 percent. Monthly churn fell to 2.6 percent in November, or 135 customers; the company began growing again. Of the 23 customers reached during the calls, seven came back when offered setup help.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Hold off on the discount decision and put the numbers side by side. The monthly revenue of the 130 extra customers who left is 123,500 TL; the discount for everyone is 988,000 TL. The cure costs eight times the disease.
  • Call 60 of those who left in August. Ask just one question: what most made you quit? Sort the answers under four headings: price, couldn't use it, business closed and competitor.

2This week

  • Break down those who left by the age of their subscription. If 63 percent of the loss is in the first three months, the problem isn't with the old customer, it's in the new customer's first weeks.
  • Look at the first 14 days of new customers: how many have issued their first invoice? A customer who hasn't issued a first invoice has bought the software but not started using it.
  • Bring phone setup support back for new subscribers: a call in the first week, transferring customer and inventory records together, issuing the first invoice together.
  • Redo the math on the March savings: what did the removed support save, and what did the increased loss take away?

3Lasting fix

  • Track churn not as a single rate but by subscription age: first 90 days, first year, older than a year.
  • Ask for and record the reason at every cancellation. Put a one-question choice and a call-back option on the cancellation screen.
  • Check the effect of every cost-cutting decision on customer loss three months later; the price of the March decision was only noticed in August.
The lesson

Customers often leave not because of price, but because they never managed to start using the product.
