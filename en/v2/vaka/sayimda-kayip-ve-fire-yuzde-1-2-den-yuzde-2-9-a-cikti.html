Shrinkage and waste in the stock count rose from 1.2 percent to 2.9 percent

Günizli Market Retail · Bursa · 320 employees · 14 stores
The problem

The six-month count showed 16.5 million TL worth of goods lost, 9.7 million TL more than the previous period. The operations manager wants cameras and security guards.

How it was solved

Broke the loss down section by section: 58 percent was fresh product thrown away, and theft accounted for 14 percent. Changed the ordering system; three months later the rate fell to 1.7 percent.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

Günizli Market counts inventory at all its stores twice a year. The result of the September count surprised management: on six-month revenue of 570 million TL, shrinkage and waste came to 2.9 percent, up from 1.2 percent in the previous period. In money terms, that is from 6.84 million TL to 16.53 million TL, a difference of 9.69 million TL.

Operations and Store Manager Salih Karabulut blames theft. He proposes renewing the camera system at 14 stores, posting security guards at the exits and checking employees' bags: an investment of 1.8 million TL and 420,000 TL a month in running costs.

Orhan Günizli wants to see where the loss is before spending the money. Meral Dinçer points out two things. In March, fresh product ordering was taken away from store managers and centralized; head office places orders based on last year's sales. Also, of the four stores where the rate exceeds 4.5 percent, three are stores with a discount market opened next door: their revenue fell, but the goods coming in did not decrease.

By the numbers

Shrinkage and waste rate: from 1.2 percent to 2.9 percent
Six-month revenue: 570 million TL; amount lost: 16.53 million TL
Increase over the previous period: 9.69 million TL
Proposed security investment: 1.8 million TL plus 420,000 TL a month

What did Günizli Market do?

Meral Dinçer and Salih Karabulut broke down the loss together. Of the 16.53 million TL, about 9.6 million TL, or 58 percent, was fresh product that had been destroyed. 3.3 million TL came from register voids and record errors, 2.3 million TL from customer and employee theft, and 1.3 million TL from short deliveries by suppliers. At two stores the void rate was four times that of the others; after review, the company parted ways with one cashier.

No security investment was made. Store managers were given authority to change fresh product orders by up to 20 percent, evening discounts began on products nearing their expiry date, and voided receipts were made subject to store manager approval. At the interim count three months later the rate came in at 1.7 percent; destruction of fresh products fell 45 percent. On three-month revenue of 285 million TL, that means about 3.4 million TL less in losses.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Don't decide from a single percentage. Break the loss down by store and by section: of the 16.53 million TL, how much is fresh product thrown away, how much is record errors, how much is goods actually stolen?
  • List everything that changed in the last six months: ordering system, suppliers, checkout software, staff. If a rate more than doubles in one period, thieves haven't suddenly multiplied; something has changed.

2This week

  • Compare fresh product orders with store sales. If a store whose revenue fell 18 percent is still getting last year's quantity of produce and meat, the excess is going in the trash.
  • Give store managers the authority to raise or lower the fresh product orders sent from head office by up to 20 percent. The store knows it will rain tomorrow and which day the weekly market is set up.
  • Review voided receipts by store and cashier. A register whose void rate is several times the others is where to look before the cameras.
  • At goods receiving, check incoming products against the delivery note and weigh them. A case the supplier shorted shows up in the count as your loss.

3Lasting fix

  • Sell products two days from their expiry date at a discount in the evening hours. A product sold at half price is better than one thrown away.
  • Record the daily write-off of fresh products and tie it to the store manager's bonus.
  • Count not twice a year but monthly in high-risk sections. A loss you see after six months is a loss that has been running for six months.
The lesson

A measure taken without knowing where the loss is amounts to looking for your keys under the streetlight.
