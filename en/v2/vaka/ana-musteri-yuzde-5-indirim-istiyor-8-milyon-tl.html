The main customer wants a 5 percent discount: 8 million TL

Kalıpsak Plastik Manufacturing · Bursa · 95 employees · 22 injection molding machines
The problem

The appliance maker that brings in half of revenue has made a 5 percent discount a condition for next year's contract. Annual profit earned from this customer is 11.2 million TL.

How it was solved

Offered a 2 percent flat discount plus joint savings projects instead of 5 percent; got a two-year contract. The customer gained 6 million TL, and Kalıpsak's profit fell from 11.2 million TL to 10.8 million TL.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

The appliance maker, Kalıpsak's biggest customer, came to the new-year meeting with a single item: a 5 percent price cut on all parts. The purchasing manager said it was being asked of all suppliers, and that the parts of those who don't accept would be reopened for bidding.

Sales to this customer are 160 million TL a year across 140 separate parts. A 5 percent discount comes to 8 million TL. By Nurten Bozkurt's calculation, the annual profit left from this customer is 11.2 million TL, or 7 percent of sales. If the discount is accepted as is, profit falls to 3.2 million TL. 58 percent of costs is raw material tied to foreign currency.

Cengiz Ergün is in favor of accepting: “If 160 million TL of business goes, half of the 22 machines will stop.” Müge Sarıkaya, on the other hand, says a discount given once without question will be asked for every year, and that not all 140 parts leave the same profit.

By the numbers

Annual sales to the customer: 160 million TL, 140 parts
Discount requested: 5 percent, or 8 million TL
Annual profit from this customer: 11.2 million TL (7 percent)
Raw material's share of cost: 58 percent

What did Kalıpsak Plastik do?

Nurten Bozkurt worked out the profit on the 140 parts one by one; 18 parts turned out to be run at a loss. İlker Dinç's team prepared three savings projects: switching to equivalent raw material in two part groups, returnable crates in place of single-use boxes and shorter cycle times on six molds. Müge Sarıkaya took a two-part offer to the customer: a 2 percent flat discount, with the rest from joint projects.

The customer accepted. The flat discount cost 3.2 million TL. The three projects saved 5.6 million TL a year; half of it, 2.8 million TL, was passed through in price. The customer's total gain was 6 million TL, or 3.75 percent. Kalıpsak's profit from this customer fell from 11.2 million TL to 10.8 million TL. The contract was made for two years and a clause for adjustment tied to raw material prices was added; the prices of seven of the 18 parts run at a loss were renegotiated.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Don't say yes or no at the table; ask for time. First write the figure in a form everyone can see: when 8 million TL comes out of 11.2 million TL in profit, 3.2 million TL remains; 71 percent of the profit is gone.
  • Work out the profit on each of the 140 parts one by one. You will see that some earn well and some are already being run at a loss. Negotiation is done part by part, not on the average.

2This week

  • What the customer wants is really lower cost; you can deliver that without cutting prices. Prepare a savings list with the production and quality teams: approval of equivalent raw materials, returnable crates, cycle times that can be shortened, overly tight dimensional tolerances.
  • Build the counteroffer in two parts: a small flat discount immediately, and the rest from joint savings projects and by sharing the savings.
  • Ask for something in return: a two-year contract, a volume commitment and a price adjustment clause tied to raw material prices.
  • Open the parts being run at a loss separately. Either the price gets corrected or the customer gives that part to another supplier; both are better than today.

3Lasting fix

  • Update the profitability table by part every quarter. A firm that doesn't know what it earns on each part can't know where to give a discount.
  • Don't wait each year for the discount request to come; bring your own savings proposals during the year. The party that brings the proposal also sets the sharing ratio.
  • Write reducing any single customer's share of revenue down as a target. Every negotiation held when half of revenue sits with one customer is partly a plea.
The lesson

The customer asks for a discount but is really looking for lower cost; there are ways to deliver the second other than cutting prices.
