40 percent of revenue is leaving within six months

Hızır Kurye Logistics · Istanbul · 152 employees · 130 couriers, 5,200 deliveries a day
The problem

The biggest customer, an online store, is building its own delivery team. Work worth 2,080 deliveries a day and 4.6 million TL a month will be pulled within six months.

How it was solved

Did not cut prices; proposed an overflow-capacity contract to the departing customer and won 38 new mid-size customers in six months. Daily deliveries fell from 5,200 to 4,690, and the biggest customer's share dropped to 9 percent.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

Hızır Kurye's biggest customer, the online store Tekçarşı, has decided to build its own delivery team. The store's logistics manager gave the news to Bahar Tunç: the transition will take six months, and in the end all deliveries will be done in-house.

Tekçarşı accounts for 2,080 deliveries a day, 40 percent of Hızır's 5,200. That is about 4.6 million TL in monthly revenue and the work of 52 of the 130 couriers is tied to this customer. If nothing else is done, daily deliveries fall to 3,120 after six months.

Kerem Hızır wants to cut prices by 15 percent and talk the customer out of its decision. Bahar Tunç says the decision has nothing to do with price, that the store wants to make delivery part of its own brand; in her view the six months should be spent on new customers, not on negotiation. Gamze Yücel sees a third thing: every store that builds its own team still has to rely on outside help on campaign days and in distant districts.

By the numbers

Tekçarşı's share: 2,080 deliveries a day, 40 percent of revenue
Monthly revenue: 11.5 million TL in total, 4.6 million TL from Tekçarşı
Couriers tied to this customer: 52 of 130
Transition period: 6 months

What did Hızır Kurye do?

Bahar Tunç met the store's logistics manager and confirmed that the decision had nothing to do with price. No discount was offered. Hızır Kurye proposed planning the transition together and afterwards providing overflow capacity for campaign days and distant districts; Tekçarşı accepted. Kerem Hızır explained the situation to the couriers himself in the first week.

At the end of six months Tekçarşı's daily deliveries fell from 2,080 to 420. The sales team won 38 new customers from pharmacies, florists, spare parts and small online sellers; they brought 1,150 deliveries a day, and the average fee per delivery was 96 TL instead of 85 TL. Daily deliveries fell to 4,690, and monthly revenue from 11.5 million TL to 10.7 million TL. The number of couriers dropped from 130 to 118. The biggest customer's share of deliveries fell from 40 percent to 9 percent.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Find out the reason for the decision. Is it price, service, or the wish to keep delivery in their own hands? If the reason isn't price, a discount will eat into your profit for six months and the customer will still leave in the end.
  • Tell the couriers the news yourself. If 52 people hear from the store's couriers that their jobs will go, they will leave without waiting six months and you won't be able to fulfill the rest of the contract either.
  • Start a six-month countdown: how many deliveries will drop from Tekçarşı each month, and how many need to be put in their place.

2This week

  • Don't burn bridges with the departing customer; be the party that eases the transition and propose a new role: overflow capacity on campaign days, at peak hours and in districts their own team can't reach.
  • Aim your sales effort not at finding one big customer but at many mid-size ones: pharmacies and medical firms, flowers and cakes, spare parts, small online sellers.
  • Set up a separate pricing and pickup scheme for small customers. A seller sending 20 parcels a day doesn't get the price of a store sending 2,000.
  • Review fixed costs against the volume six months from now: warehouse, vehicles, office staff.

3Lasting fix

  • Put a ceiling on any single customer's share of revenue (for example 20 percent) and make winning new customers a mandatory target when it is exceeded.
  • Write a clause into large customer contracts requiring at least six months' notice of exit and a gradual volume reduction.
  • Talk through future plans with your three biggest customers every quarter. A decision to build their own team isn't made in a day; it gives signs months in advance.
The lesson

As your biggest customer grows, one day it becomes able to do your job itself; start filling its place without waiting for that day.
