The plant is producing 15 percent below what was promised, and the customer wants 781,000 TL

Solmira Enerji Energy · Konya · 36 employees · 30 megawatts installed per year
The problem

The 1.2-megawatt plant installed eight months ago generated 186,000 kilowatt-hours less than expected. The contract has no production guarantee, but the quote file states a figure.

How it was solved

It broke the shortfall down by cause; it paid 428,000 TL arising from the inverter failure and the shade error, and showed the customer the soiling share by measurement. Production rose to 97% of the projection.

What happened?

The 1.2-megawatt plant Solmira Enerji installed in January on the roof of a foundry fell short of expectations. The quote file had projected 1,240,000 kilowatt-hours of production for the first eight months; the meter shows 1,054,000. The shortfall is 186,000 kilowatt-hours, or 15%. The factory has calculated it at 4.20 TL per kilowatt-hour and is asking for 781,000 TL in compensation and a written production guarantee for the remaining years.

Sales Manager Gizem Tuna looks at the contract: no production guarantee was given, and the figure in the quote is a projection. Moreover, the foundry's roof is covered in dust, and the customer has not had the panels washed even once in eight months. General Manager Tolga Erdin agrees: “If we pay a single kuruş, from now on we'll get the bill for every cloudy month.”

Project Manager Kaan Özbey, however, found two problems in his own records. One of the inverters was out of service for 23 days in spring; the remote monitoring system raised an alert but nobody looked at it, even though the maintenance contract is with Solmira. Also, the quote calculation left out the shadow of the ventilation stacks on the roof; 36 panels sit in shade in the afternoon.

By the numbers

Projected production 1,240,000 kilowatt-hours, actual 1,054,000
Shortfall: 186,000 kilowatt-hours (15%)
Customer's claim: 781,000 TL (4.20 TL per kilowatt-hour)
One inverter was out of service for 23 days; 36 panels in shade

What did Solmira Enerji do?

Kaan Özbey's team reviewed the monitoring records and a one-week washing trial together with the customer's technical manager. Of the 186,000 kilowatt-hour shortfall, 84,000 came from panel soiling, 62,000 from the inverter that was down for 23 days, and 40,000 from the unaccounted stack shadow. Tolga Erdin backed away from his first reaction; Solmira paid 428,000 TL, the equivalent of the 102,000 kilowatt-hours within its own responsibility.

The 36 panels in shade were moved to the south edge of the roof, the panels were washed, and four washes a year were added to the maintenance contract. Over the following two months, the plant reached 97% of projected production. The customer dropped its 781,000 TL claim and asked for a quote for the roof of its second factory.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Don't answer the customer with contract clauses. First make a promise: we will set out the cause of the shortfall with measurements within ten days, and we will pay our share.
  • Download the remote monitoring records today and store them in a form that can't be altered. This is the only source both you and the customer will trust.

2This week

  • Break the 186,000 kilowatt-hours down by cause: inverter failure, shade, panel soiling, weather conditions. Each can be measured separately; bargaining without measurement turns into stubbornness.
  • Have one row of panels washed and compare it with the dirty row next to it for a week. Show the share of soiling not by estimate but by meter.
  • Take your own share openly: the 23 days of the inverter covered by your maintenance contract, and the shade calculation omitted from the quote, are your responsibility.
  • Move the panels sitting in shade. Compensation closes the past; moving the panels fixes the future.

3Lasting fix

  • In quotes, give a range instead of a single production figure and state the conditions under which it holds: how many washes a year, which shade assumptions.
  • Assign an owner and a deadline to monitoring system alerts: a call within 24 hours of an alert, intervention within 72 hours.
  • On dusty industrial rooftops, build washing into the maintenance contract; don't leave it to the customer to remember.
The lesson

The figure you put in a quote is a promise in the customer's mind, even if it isn't written in the contract.
