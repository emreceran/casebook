The tour company that fills 45 percent of the rooms stopped paying

Sığla Koy Otel Tourism · Muğla · 55 employees · 64 rooms
The problem

The foreign tour company's invoices for two months went unpaid; the receivable is 7.3 million TL. Guests are still at the hotel, with six weeks left in the season.

How it was solved

Kept hosting the guests inside, tied new arrivals to prepayment and sold 14 of the 29 rooms through its own channels. Collected 5.7 million TL of the 7.3 million TL receivable before the season ended.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

Of Sığla Koy's 64 rooms, 29 have been allocated to the same foreign tour company, Helvia Reisen, for nine years. The company has not paid its July and August invoices: 3.6 million TL and 3.7 million TL, 7.3 million TL in total. The payment term in the contract is 15 days. An email in mid-September said “payments have been temporarily suspended” and gave no date.

The tour company's guests are staying at the hotel at the moment, with new ones arriving each week. There are another 5.1 million TL of reservations through this channel for September and October. Pelin Arıkan's table is clear: there is 2.9 million TL in the bank, and the hotel's fixed monthly expenses are 4.4 million TL.

Oktay Bilgin wants to refuse new groups and immediately sell the 29 rooms through the hotel's own channels. Nesrin Karaöz thinks that turning away a guest arriving from the airport would ruin the hotel's name, and that a nine-year partner shouldn't be written off over one crisis.

By the numbers

Rooms allocated to the tour company: 29 of 64 (45 percent)
Unpaid July and August invoices: 7.3 million TL
Reservations pending from the same channel for September–October: 5.1 million TL
Cash on hand 2.9 million TL; fixed monthly expenses 4.4 million TL

What did Sığla Koy Otel do?

Nesrin Karaöz met with the tour company's finance director; none of the guests inside were told about the matter. After the written notice, Helvia Reisen paid 2.5 million TL within ten days and put the remaining 4.8 million TL into three installments of 1.6 million TL. For new arrivals, payment began to be required seven days before check-in.

Oktay Bilgin took back 14 of the 29 rooms and put them on sale on the hotel's own site and online channels; October closed 78 percent full. By the end of the season two of the three installments had been paid, bringing the receivable down to 1.6 million TL. In next year's contract the tour company's room share was cut from 45 percent to 30 percent and a prepayment condition was written in.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Don't touch the guests already inside. Making a family that has paid for its vacation suffer over a receivable between you and the tour company won't collect the money, but it will permanently damage the hotel's name.
  • Write down the whole risk: a 7.3 million TL receivable, plus 5.1 million TL in reservations. If you do nothing, the amount that could be left exposed at the end of the season is 12.4 million TL.
  • The same day, speak directly with the tour company's finance director and send a written notice: you want a dated payment plan for the overdue amount, with five business days to respond.

2This week

  • Tie future arrivals to prepayment: if payment doesn't arrive seven days before check-in, those rooms are released. Announce this not as a penalty but as a new working condition.
  • Don't take back all 29 rooms overnight; put about half on sale on your own site and online sales channels. There is still domestic demand for late September and October.
  • Talk to the bank and prepare a short-term credit line using the receivable's documents. The cash on hand doesn't cover a month of expenses.
  • Find out from colleagues in the region whether the tour company has stopped paying other hotels too. If it isn't paying anyone, there is a risk of bankruptcy, and you negotiate accordingly.

3Lasting fix

  • Put a ceiling on the share of rooms allocated to any single tour company (for example 30 percent) and spread the remaining rooms across at least two other channels.
  • Convert contracts from invoices with payment terms to prepayment: the price of the stay should be in your account before the check-in date.
  • For foreign receivables, make credit insurance or a bank letter of guarantee a condition of the contract.
The lesson

The partner who fills your rooms and the partner who pays are not the same thing; occupancy is not cash.
