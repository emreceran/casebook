Receivables from patients reached 1.4 million TL

Mirva Diş Klinikleri Healthcare · Ankara · 38 employees · 3 clinics, 11 dentists
The problem

The clinic offers informal installment plans and nobody takes responsibility for follow-up. 520,000 TL is more than three months overdue.

How it was solved

Made collections one person's main job, rescheduled the old receivables and shifted installments to credit cards and a financing institution.

What would you do? 2 comments ↓

What happened?

Mirva offers patients informal installment plans with no promissory notes on expensive treatments. When Clinic Manager Sibel Aydın tallied the open accounts, the figure emerged: 1.4 million TL owed by 310 patients. Of this, 520,000 TL is more than 90 days overdue.

Follow-up is something the secretaries do when they have spare time. The dentists don't want to discuss money with patients. And once treatment is finished, a patient's willingness to pay drops noticeably.

Monthly revenue is 4.8 million TL; the receivables are nearly equal to ten days of revenue. Dr. Emine Soylu worries that if they eliminate installments they will lose patients.

By the numbers

Total receivables: 1.4 million TL, 310 patients
Receivables over 90 days: 520,000 TL
Monthly revenue: 4.8 million TL
Person responsible for collections: none

What did Mirva Diş Klinikleri do?

Tolga Eren set aside two days a week solely for collections. Of the 520,000 TL more than 90 days overdue, 310,000 TL was collected in two months, 140,000 TL was put on a new payment plan, and 70,000 TL was set aside as doubtful receivables.

For new treatments, installments began to be handled through credit cards or a partner financing institution. For patients who want informal installments, 80 percent of the amount is collected before the prosthesis is fitted. Total receivables fell from 1.4 million TL to 780,000 TL.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Sort the receivables by age: up to 30 days, 30–90 days, over 90 days. Also mark which patients' treatment is ongoing and which has ended.
  • Make collections one person's main job. A job everyone does in their spare time is nobody's job.

2This week

  • Call the patients who are more than 90 days overdue personally. The aim is not to press them, but to agree on a realistic new payment plan and put it in writing.
  • For patients with ongoing treatment, give the next appointment together with an update of the payment plan.
  • Take the dentists out of money conversations entirely: have the patient coordinator explain the payment plan before treatment begins and have the patient sign it.

3Lasting fix

  • Don't be a bank. Don't eliminate installments, but don't carry them yourself: arrange credit card installments or an agreement with an institution that offers health financing. The patient still pays in installments, and the clinic gets its money upfront.
  • If informal installments are to remain, tie payment to treatment stages: 80 percent of the amount should have been collected before the most expensive stage (for example, fitting the prosthesis).
  • Set an open balance limit per patient and make the receivables aging table the first item at every partners' meeting.
The lesson

An invoice issued is not income but a promise; the job isn't done until the money is in the till.
