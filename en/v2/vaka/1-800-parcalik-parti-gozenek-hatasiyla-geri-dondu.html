A batch of 1,800 parts came back with porosity defects

Ergitaş Döküm Manufacturing · Konya · 140 employees · 900 tons a month, two shifts
The problem

An automotive customer found a 6% defect rate at incoming inspection and rejected the batch. It wants a report within 48 hours, and its stock lasts three days.

How it was solved

It first stopped the three suspect batches and fed the customer's line with on-site sorting. It found the root cause in a resin that had been changed two weeks earlier.

What would you do? 2 comments ↓

What happened?

A batch of 1,800 brake brackets that Ergitaş shipped to an automotive customer was rejected in full when incoming inspection found a 6% porosity defect rate. Because the part is used in the brake system, the customer is treating the matter as a safety issue.

The customer wants a root cause report within 48 hours. Its own stock lasts three days; if its line stops, it will pass the cost on to Ergitaş. It has also said it will invoice the sorting costs.

Quality Supervisor Elif Sarı noticed that two more batches cast the same week are in transit and in the warehouse. Production Manager Murat Kılınç, on the other hand, thinks the problem was a one-off.

By the numbers

Rejected batch: 1,800 units, defect rate 6%
Batch value: about 738,000 TL
Report deadline: 48 hours
Customer's stock: 3 days

What did Ergitaş Döküm do?

Elif Sarı quarantined the three batches cast the same week. A four-person team sorted the batch at the customer's plant, while an urgent shipment was made with 100% inspected parts. The customer's line did not stop.

The question “what changed in that batch?” revealed the cause: a new resin introduced two weeks earlier had increased gas formation in the casting. The old resin was restored and the report was delivered within 48 hours. The customer passed on 96,000 TL in sorting costs but did not cut the business. At Ergitaş, when a raw material or supplier changes, the first batch is no longer shipped without separate approval.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • First stop the spread: quarantine every batch cast the same week, in the warehouse and in transit. Until you can prove it was a one-off, assume it wasn't.
  • Keep the customer's line running. Send a team to the customer's plant to sort the batch on site, and meanwhile make an urgent shipment with parts that have been 100% inspected.
  • Give the customer a short status update on day one: what we've stopped, how we'll feed your line, when the report will arrive.

2This week

  • Look for the root cause in the data: pouring temperature records, sand moisture, degassing time, raw material lots. The question to ask is: what changed in that batch?
  • List every change made in recent weeks (new supplier, new resin, new operator, setting after maintenance). Most defects come right after a change.
  • Write the report honestly. If the cause isn't yet certain, say so and state the interim measure; a made-up root cause will finish off trust completely at the second defect.

3Lasting fix

  • Set a change management rule: when a raw material, supplier or process changes, the first batch is not shipped without a separate approval.
  • For safety parts, monitor critical process values by shift and take a cross-section sample on every shift.
  • Record defects caught before reaching the customer as well. A defect caught inside is a cheap warning; one caught outside is an expensive lesson.
The lesson

What loses a customer is often not the defect but how the defect is handled.
