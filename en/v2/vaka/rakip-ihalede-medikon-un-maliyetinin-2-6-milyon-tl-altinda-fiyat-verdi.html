A competitor bid 2.6 million TL below Medikon's cost on a tender

Medikon Tıbbi Cihaz Healthcare · Ankara · 28 employees · 9 service engineers, 640 installed devices
The problem

On a tender for 18 anesthesia machines, the competitor's bid is 44.2 million TL; Medikon's cost is 46.8 million TL. There are three days until final bids.

How it was solved

Set a floor at 3 percent above cost, submitted a final bid of 48.2 million TL and did not go lower. Lost the tender, but made 2.1 million TL in profit from two small tenders the same month.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

A university hospital opened a tender for 18 anesthesia machines; the contracting authority's estimated value is 54 million TL. Medikon's cost, including devices, installation and two years of warranty service, is 46.8 million TL. Hülya Demirci bid 51.5 million TL, with a 10 percent profit. When the envelopes were opened, the competitor's bid was 44.2 million TL: 2.6 million TL below Medikon's cost.

The authority wants final bids in three days. Tülin Erkan is in favor of going in: 18 machines means at least eight years of service and consumables revenue after the warranty ends. By her calculation, that is 310,000 TL per device per year, 5.58 million TL in revenue in total and, at a 35 percent margin, about 1.95 million TL a year in profit. “We lose 2.6 million TL today and make it back in two years.”

Hülya Demirci doesn't trust the same calculation. Post-warranty service will be awarded in a separate tender, and there is no guarantee it will go to Medikon. Selling at a loss would strain the cash position and also send the other hospitals in the region the message that “Medikon gives this price too.” Ferhat Günay is undecided: leaving the market's biggest tender to a competitor weighs heavily on him as well.

By the numbers

Authority's estimated value: 54 million TL (18 anesthesia machines)
Medikon's cost 46.8 million TL, first bid 51.5 million TL
Competitor's bid: 44.2 million TL
Expected post-warranty service revenue: 5.58 million TL a year, with no guarantee

What did Medikon Tıbbi Cihaz do?

Ferhat Günay set the floor at 3 percent above cost, or 48.2 million TL. Medikon submitted its final bid at this figure and asked the authority in writing to investigate the competitor's bid as abnormally low. The competitor gave its explanation, the authority accepted it and the tender stayed with the competitor at 44.2 million TL.

Medikon lost the tender, but it also didn't book a 2.6 million TL loss. Tülin Erkan's team entered two small tenders in the same month and won a total of 19.4 million TL of work at an 11 percent profit: about 2.1 million TL. Eight months later the same university hospital opened a maintenance tender for 22 old devices whose warranties had expired; Medikon won the contract, worth 3.1 million TL a year.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • First write down your floor and have everyone sign it: below which price will you not go? This figure is set not by tender excitement but by the cost table, and it doesn't change on final bid day.
  • Don't count future service revenue as compensation for today's loss. Revenue without a contract is not revenue, it is hope; it enters the calculation only at its probability.
  • Try to understand how the competitor's price is possible: a different device model, a missing line item, or a loss deliberately accepted to enter the market?

2This week

  • Examine whether the device the competitor offered meets the technical specification point by point; if there are shortfalls, notify the authority in writing with documentation.
  • Properly request that the authority investigate whether the bid is abnormally low. This is not smearing a competitor, it is a right recognized by the regulations.
  • Submit your final bid at your floor and stop there. If you lose, you will have lost by your own decision, not by price.
  • Look at the smaller tenders in the same period. A team locked onto the big tender misses three small jobs that leave a profit.

3Lasting fix

  • Before bidding on any tender, write down three numbers: target price, floor and walk-away point. The decision should be made before the envelopes are opened.
  • Follow the tenders you lose: how did the winning company handle delivery and service? That information is your strongest file in the next tender.
  • Present hospitals not just the device price but a ten-year total cost calculation: service, consumables, days the device is out of service.
The lesson

A lost tender is a lost sale; a tender won below cost is a debt paid for years.
