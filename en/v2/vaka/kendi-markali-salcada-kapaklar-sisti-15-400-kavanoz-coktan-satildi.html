Lids swelled on own-brand tomato paste: 15,400 jars already sold

Günizli Market Retail · Bursa · 320 employees · 14 stores
The problem

Nine complaints came in over six days about Günizli Sofra tomato paste. The lab result is five days away; the decision has to be made today.

How it was solved

Pulled the product from the shelves the same day without waiting for the lab, texted card-holding customers and opened refunds without receipts. The result confirmed spoilage; the manufacturer covered most of the cost.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

Nine customer complaints came in over six days about the 700-gram Günizli Sofra tomato paste that Günizli Market sells under its own label: lids swollen, gas escaping when opened, a sour smell. All from the same batch. The paste is filled under contract by a canning manufacturer. The batch is 24,000 jars; 15,400 have been sold, and 8,600 are on the shelves of 14 stores and in the warehouse.

Meral Dinçer sent samples to the laboratory; the result will take five business days. In her view waiting is not an option: a swollen lid is a sign of spoilage, the product should be pulled from the shelves today, and buyers should be notified. Of the 15,400 jars sold, 9,800 were bought with a loyalty card; these customers can be reached directly.

İsmet Günizli is cautious. Nine complaints out of 15,400 jars is six in ten thousand. The product should be quietly pulled from the shelves, but the announcement should wait for the lab result: announcing “it turned out to be spoiled” for a product with his own surname on the label would stain a 28-year-old name over a tomato paste. If the result comes back clean, panic will have been created for nothing.

By the numbers

Batch: 24,000 jars; 15,400 sold, 8,600 on shelves and in the warehouse
Complaints: 9 in 6 days, all from the same batch
Sold with a loyalty card: 9,800 jars
Shelf price 85 TL, cost price 58 TL; lab result in 5 business days

What did Günizli Market do?

Orhan Günizli made the decision the same day. The 8,600 jars were pulled from shelves and the warehouse by evening, and the barcode was blocked at the registers. A text message went out to customers who shop with a loyalty card, notices were posted in stores, and the provincial directorate was informed. The lab result, which arrived five days later, confirmed spoilage: heat treatment had fallen short in part of the batch, and 6 percent of the jars examined were spoiled. No customer who fell ill was reported.

In three weeks 6,200 jars were returned. The refunds cost 527,000 TL, and the purchase cost of the 8,600 destroyed jars came to about 499,000 TL; a total of 1.03 million TL. Thanks to a clause in the contract, the manufacturer covered 858,000 TL, the purchase cost of the 14,800 jars that were left on the shelves or returned. Sales of own-brand products fell 6 percent in the first month and returned to their previous level in the third.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Pull the product from shelves and warehouses at all stores today, and block its barcode at the register. Taking it off the shelf isn't enough; a case forgotten in the back warehouse will be back on the shelf next week.
  • Don't tie the announcement to the lab result. Within five days those jars will be opened in kitchens. If the result comes back clean, you were overly cautious; if it comes back bad and you stayed silent, there is no explanation for that.
  • Send a short text message to customers who bought with a loyalty card: the batch number, a “do not consume” warning, and a refund with no receipt required.

2This week

  • Post a notice showing the batch number at store entrances and at the registers; you have no other way to reach the owners of the 5,600 jars sold without a card.
  • Report the situation yourself to the provincial directorate of agriculture and forestry. There is a big difference between the official learning about it from a complaint and learning about it from you.
  • Ask the manufacturer for the production and heat treatment records of that batch. Find out whether any other product or batch was filled the same week.
  • Record the refund and disposal costs item by item; the settlement with the manufacturer will be based on this breakdown.

3Lasting fix

  • Prepare a written recall plan for every own-brand product: who decides, who announces, how the registers are blocked.
  • Audit the contract manufacturer unannounced at least twice a year and ask for a retained sample from every batch.
  • State clearly in contract manufacturing agreements who bears the cost of a recall.
The lesson

The responsibility for a product with your name on it is yours, no matter who made it.
