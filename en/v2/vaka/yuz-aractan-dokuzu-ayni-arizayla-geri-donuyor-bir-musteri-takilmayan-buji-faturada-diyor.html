Nine out of every hundred vehicles come back with the same fault, and a customer says “spark plugs on the invoice that were never installed”

Rodaj Oto Servis Automotive · Adana · 42 employees · 3 branches, 1,200 vehicles a month
The problem

Each month 108 vehicles return to the garage with the same complaint. One customer also claims that work he paid for was never done; the garage has no record to show otherwise.

How it was solved

It opened the car in front of the customer, and on seeing that the spark plugs had not been installed, refunded him in full. With photo-documented work orders and a bonus tied to returns, the rate fell from 9% to 4%.

What happened?

Of the 1,200 work orders opened at Rodaj Oto Servis each month, 108, or 9%, are for vehicles that come back within 30 days with the same fault. This work is done free of charge; each one costs an average of 2,400 TL in labor and parts, about 259,000 TL a month. There is a big difference among the branches: the rate is 6% at Merkez, 7.1% at Çukurova and 16.7% at Yüreğir.

The same week, a customer from the Yüreğir branch complained. His invoice lists 7,800 TL for replacing four spark plugs and cleaning the injectors. When the car kept misfiring, he had another mechanic open it up; according to that mechanic, the spark plugs are old. The customer wrote an online review with photos and says he will apply to the consumer arbitration board.

Founder Cemal Gündoğdu is furious: “My mechanics don't write down work they didn't do, this is slander.” General Manager Ozan Gündoğdu knows they can't prove it; removed parts aren't kept and no photos are taken. Shop Foreman Şükrü Bayram points out one more thing: at Yüreğir, mechanics get a 4% bonus on the invoices they write.

By the numbers

1,200 work orders a month, 108 returning with the same fault (9%)
Merkez 6%, Çukurova 7.1%, Yüreğir 16.7%
Average cost of a returning vehicle 2,400 TL; about 259,000 TL a month
Disputed invoice: 7,800 TL

What did Rodaj Oto Servis do?

Ozan Gündoğdu called the customer; the car was opened the next day with the customer present. The injector cleaning had been done, but the spark plugs really were old. According to the warehouse record, four spark plugs had been issued that day under the mechanic's name, handed off to the apprentice during the rush, and forgotten on the bench. The full 7,800 TL was refunded and the spark plugs were installed free of charge. The customer updated his review. Cemal Gündoğdu called the customer himself to apologize.

At all three branches, bagged delivery of removed parts and photo-documented work orders began. The bonus tied to invoice amounts was abolished; the bonus is now paid on jobs that don't come back within 30 days. Three months later the number of returning vehicles fell from 108 to 48, the rate from 9% to 4%, and the monthly cost from 259,000 TL to 115,000 TL. Yüreğir's rate dropped from 16.7% to 6%.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Have the General Manager call the customer and make a single offer without getting defensive: we'll come pick up the car and open the hood in front of you. If you're right, a full refund and the job is free.
  • Compare the work order with the parts warehouse record: did four spark plugs leave the warehouse that day, and under which mechanic's name?
  • Write a short, calm reply to the online review: we are looking into it, we have called you. Don't get into an argument; the people reading that reply won't be the customer but the next hundred customers.

2This week

  • Review Yüreğir's 50 returns one by one: which mechanic, which fault, is it a faulty part or a faulty diagnosis? A rate of 16.7% is a sign bigger than a single complaint.
  • Bag every removed part and hand it to the customer, and photograph the work before and after and attach it to the work order. This protects the honest mechanic as much as the customer.
  • Separate the bonus from the invoice amount. A system that pays more the more jobs are written up pushes even the most honest mechanic toward unnecessary work.
  • Before a vehicle is handed over, have someone other than the mechanic who did the work take it for a test drive.

3Lasting fix

  • Tie the bonus to jobs that don't come back within 30 days: a mechanic should earn from jobs finished the first time, not from the invoice.
  • Post the return rate by branch and by mechanic on the wall every month.
  • Apply the same diagnosis and handover steps at all three branches; carry the system that works at Merkez over to Yüreğir.
The lesson

If you can't show the work you did, you have no answer for a customer who says you didn't do it.
