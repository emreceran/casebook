Batıkent branch average dropped 7 net points, parents' group up in arms

Bilgi Çınarı Kursları Education · Eskişehir · 48 employees · 3 branches, 1,150 students
The problem

The practice exam average for 12th graders fell from 62.4 to 55.1 net points in three months. In the 140-member parents' group, 23 parents are demanding tuition refunds.

How it was solved

It wrote a single signed message to the group, invited parents to a meeting three days later with the numbers, and launched 24 hours of math make-up lessons. Of the 23 parents asking for refunds, 19 stayed.

What happened?

The 120-question practice exam average of the 86 university prep students at the Batıkent branch was a net score of 62.4 in June. In July it was 61.8, in August 58.9, and in the first practice exam of September 55.1. On the same exam, the average of the other two branches was 63.5.

On the evening the results were released, the 140-member parents' messaging group came alive. Within two days, 23 parents wrote that they wanted a tuition refund; a few parents shared the results tables in other course groups too. Branch Manager Filiz Erdem knows the two causes of the drop: in July the math teacher left, and because the replacement taught at two branches at once, math lessons were replaced by study sessions for three weeks. The math average fell from a net 21.3 to 15.6 out of 40 questions. In addition, since August the practice exams had been deliberately made harder.

Filiz Erdem wants to answer parents one by one in the group. Halil Çınar is against it: he says every reply written to the group will inflate the argument and that calling parents individually is enough. Zerrin Aktaş thinks silence is also an answer and will be read in the worst way.

By the numbers

Practice exam average: 62.4 net in June, 55.1 net in September (other branches 63.5)
Math average: from 21.3 net to 15.6 net
Time without a teacher: 3 weeks
Parents' group of 140 members; 23 parents asking for a tuition refund

What did Bilgi Çınarı Kursları do?

That same evening, Filiz Erdem wrote a single message to the group, co-signed with Halil Çınar; it stated openly the three weeks without a teacher and called a meeting for Thursday evening. The 23 parents asking for refunds were called one by one before the meeting. 71 parents came to the meeting. Zerrin Aktaş showed that 5.7 net points of the drop came from math and the rest from the harder exams; the other two branches had also fallen by about 2 net points over the same period.

A permanent math teacher was assigned to Batıkent, and free make-up lessons were held on weekends for four weeks, six hours each, 24 hours in total. Of the 23 parents asking for refunds, four left and got back the fees for their remaining months, and 19 stayed. In the practice exam at the end of October, the branch average rose to 60.7 net and the math average to 19.8 net.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Write one short, signed message to the group: we see the drop, we know the reason, and on Thursday evening we will be at the branch with the numbers and a make-up plan. Don't get drawn into the argument, but don't stay silent either.
  • Don't be defensive in the message. Even if it's true, “the exams got harder” reads like an excuse in a first message; you be the one to mention the three weeks without a teacher.
  • Call the 23 parents asking for refunds one by one before the meeting. A parent who writes in the group is speaking to the crowd; a parent on the phone is speaking to you.

2This week

  • Go to the meeting with a lesson-by-lesson breakdown: how much of the drop comes from math and how much from the harder exams. Show the other branches' results on the same exam too.
  • Present a concrete make-up program: hours, days, the teacher's name. “We'll do what's needed” is not a plan.
  • Give Batıkent a permanent math teacher who teaches only at this branch, and announce the name at the meeting.
  • Apply the contract's refund terms without making it difficult for a parent who wants to leave. A parent kept against their will stays in the group and keeps writing.

3Lasting fix

  • Have the course, not the student, send exam results to parents: within 48 hours, together with the student's own trend line and the branch average. A parent who hears bad news from the course doesn't react the same way as one who hears it in the group.
  • Make a backup teacher plan for every subject. When a teacher leaves, it should be set in advance that a class will go unstaffed for no more than a week.
  • If the difficulty of the practice exams is going to change, announce it to parents beforehand; say that the average will fall before the results come out.
The lesson

If you don't announce the bad number first, someone else will announce it for you.
