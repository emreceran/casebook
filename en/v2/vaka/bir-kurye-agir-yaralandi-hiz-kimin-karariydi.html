A courier was seriously injured: whose decision was the speed?

Hızır Kurye Logistics · Istanbul · 152 employees · 130 couriers, 5,200 deliveries a day
The problem

A 27-year-old courier had an accident on a rainy evening; he has had surgery and is recovering. The accident put the late-delivery deduction and the self-employed model on the table.

How it was solved

Covered the courier's treatment costs and four months of lost income, removed the late-delivery deduction and put all couriers on accident insurance. Accidents fell from 12 to 5 over the next six months.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

In the last week of September, on a rainy evening, Sinan K., 27, who has worked at Hızır Kurye for three years, collided with a car on his way to his last delivery. He has two fractures in his leg; he has had surgery, is doing well and is recovering. Doctors say he won't be able to work for about four months.

Sinan K. is not a company employee; like 112 of the 130 couriers, he is self-employed. He earns 52 TL per delivery, and 26 TL of that is deducted for a late delivery. On days he doesn't work he has no income. When Ufuk Sezer pulled the records, the picture got worse: in the last 12 months there were 23 accidents, six with injuries. Fifteen of the accidents happened with less than 30 minutes left before the delivery deadline.

Ufuk Sezer wants all couriers moved onto the payroll. Kerem Hızır says this would raise the cost per courier by 35 percent, about 2.1 million TL a month, and that the price won't hold unless competitors do the same. Gamze Yücel's objection is different: the debate over the model will drag on for months, but the late-delivery deduction that pushes couriers to speed can be removed tomorrow.

By the numbers

23 accidents in the last 12 months; 6 with injuries
15 of the accidents in the last 30 minutes of the delivery window (65 percent)
Earnings per delivery 52 TL; deduction for lateness 26 TL
Extra cost of putting 112 couriers on the payroll: about 2.1 million TL a month

What did Hızır Kurye do?

Kerem Hızır and Ufuk Sezer visited the courier in the hospital. The company took on paying the part of the treatment that insurance doesn't cover, plus 45,000 TL a month for four months, the courier's average monthly earnings. Sinan K. is recovering; his doctors say he can return to work in the spring. The accident was explained openly to all couriers the next morning.

The late-delivery deduction was removed that week. All 112 self-employed couriers were put on a personal accident policy with premiums paid by the company: 650 TL per courier per month, 72,800 TL in total. A payroll position was offered to the 31 couriers who had completed two years; 19 accepted, at an additional cost of about 360,000 TL a month. The on-time delivery rate slipped from 96 percent to 95 percent. Accidents, which numbered 12 in the previous six months, fell to 5 in the following six; only one involved an injury.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • First, be there for the person. Visit the courier and his family; cover the part of the treatment that insurance doesn't, and the income lost during the time he can't work, without arguing over whether you are legally required to.
  • Tell all couriers openly what happened: what occurred, how their colleague is, what the company is doing. If couriers hear it on the street, they will also hear that the company stayed silent.
  • Freeze the record of that evening: delivery times, route, weather, messages sent to the courier. Not to look for someone to blame, but to see what needs to change.

2This week

  • Remove the late-delivery deduction. A rule that takes money out of a late courier's pocket makes a red light negotiable on a rainy evening.
  • Analyze the 23 accidents by time of day, weather, district and time left before the deadline. If accidents cluster under certain conditions, the measures should be built around those conditions.
  • Put all couriers, including the self-employed, on a personal accident insurance policy paid for by the company.
  • Start a monthly check of helmets, rain gear, tires and brakes; don't withhold deliveries from a courier with missing equipment, help them fix it.

3Lasting fix

  • Change the model gradually, not overnight: offer the payroll option to couriers who reach a certain length of service, and reflect its cost in pricing.
  • Tie bonuses to safe riding rather than speed: accident-free months, complete equipment, riding by the rules.
  • Make your delivery-time promise depend on the weather. A company that promises the dry-weather time in the rain loads the difference onto the courier's back.
The lesson

If you make the courier pay for lateness, he also pays for the speed.
