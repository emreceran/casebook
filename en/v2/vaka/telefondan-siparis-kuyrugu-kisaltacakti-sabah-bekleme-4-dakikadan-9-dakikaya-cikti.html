Phone ordering was supposed to shorten the line: the morning wait went from 4 minutes to 9

Copperwren Coffee Food & Drink · Portland, USA · 41 employees · 4 locations, $2.6 million in annual revenue
The problem

Between 7 and 9 a.m., 38% of orders come in by phone and join the same queue as the people at the counter. The people waiting at the counter walk away, and the coffee of the person who ordered by phone goes cold.

How it was solved

He measured for a week; the problem wasn't the machine but the sequencing. He added pickup times and a cap to phone orders and put a third person on during the morning hours. The wait dropped from 9 minutes to 5.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

In April, Copperwren Coffee launched phone pre-ordering at all four locations. The goal was to shorten the morning line. By July, 38% of orders between 7 and 9 a.m. were coming in by phone, and the average wait at the counter had gone from 4 minutes to 9.

Phone orders and counter orders fall into the same queue. When ten orders arrive at once within five minutes, the customer waiting at the counter can't understand why the baristas aren't looking at him when no one is in front of them. The people who ordered by phone aren't happy either: those who arrive early wait for their coffee, and those who arrive late pick up a coffee that has been sitting cold on the shelf for six or seven minutes. Counter sales in the morning hours are down 12%.

There are three proposals. Director of Locations Eli Navarro wants to shut off phone ordering during the morning hours. Founder Hannah Whitlock suggests buying a second espresso machine for each location; at $24,000 each, that's $96,000 for four locations. Head Roaster Marcus Delaney says the menu is too long. No one has measured whether the problem is the machine, the people or the sequencing.

By the numbers

Share of orders by phone between 7 and 9 a.m.: 38%
Average wait at the counter went from 4 minutes to 9
Counter sales in the morning hours fell 12%
Second espresso machine: $24,000 per location, $96,000 total

What did Copperwren Coffee do?

In mid-August, Eli Navarro timed the first location with a stopwatch every morning for a week. The espresso machine sat idle for a third of the time even at rush hour; the delay was piling up in milk prep and handoff. The second machine wasn't bought. Starting August 24, customers who order by phone choose a pickup time, no more than 12 phone orders are accepted in any fifteen-minute window, and those orders are handed off from a separate shelf. Between 7 and 9 a.m., a third person, handling only milk and handoff, was added at each location. The four drinks that take the longest to make were removed from the phone menu in the morning hours.

The third person comes to eight hours a day across the four locations, at a cost of about $5,600 a month. In the first three weeks of September, the average wait at the counter dropped to 5 minutes. The drop in morning counter sales eased from 12% to 3%. The morning share of phone orders fell from 38% to 33%; some of the customers who hit the cap moved their order fifteen minutes later, and some came to the counter.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Before you decide, measure for a week at one location. Write down the time each order came in, when it was started and when it was handed over. You buy the $96,000 machine once it's clear the problem is the machine.
  • Do something visible for the customer waiting at the counter: a small screen showing how many orders are in line, or one sentence from the barista. A customer who knows why will wait two minutes longer.

2This week

  • Have phone orders choose a time. Let the customer pick how many minutes from now they'll pick up their coffee, and let the order enter the queue by that time. An order placed now doesn't have to be made now.
  • Set a cap on the number of phone orders accepted for every fifteen minutes. When the cap is full, the app suggests the next slot.
  • Separate the pickup spot for phone orders from the counter. A separate shelf pulls half the crowd away from the front of the counter.
  • Put a third person on for two hours in the morning, with the job of handling only milk and handoff. The bottleneck may not be the machine but the two people at the machine.

3Lasting fix

  • When you open a new sales channel, calculate the capacity with it. Phone ordering doesn't bring in new customers, it bunches existing customers into the same minute.
  • Measure the morning wait at every location one day a week and make it one of the numbers the location manager watches.
  • Shorten the phone menu in the morning hours. The drinks that take longest to make stretch everyone's wait at rush hour.
The lesson

Letting the customer order without standing in line doesn't remove the line, it just makes it invisible.
