Re-enrollment fell from 71% to 54%

Bilgi Çınarı Kursları Education · Eskişehir · 48 employees · 3 branches, 1,150 students
The problem

Of the 620 students expected to continue, 285 did not re-enroll. Management thinks the cause is the fee increase, but nobody asked the parents who left.

How it was solved

Before offering a discount, it called the parents who left; in 48 of 164 conversations the cause was indifference. 46 students came back, and the re-enrollment rate rose to 61%.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

Bilgi Çınarı had 620 students who attended last year and could continue this year. The course is used to about 71% of them, or 440 students, re-enrolling every year. This September, the number who re-enrolled stayed at 335: 54%. The 105 students in between come to about 10.1 million TL at the annual fee of 96,000 TL.

Halil Çınar is sure of the cause: the fee rose from 62,000 TL to 96,000 TL in one year, an increase of 55%. He wants a win-back campaign with a 15% discount for those who left.

Zerrin Aktaş looks at the list differently. The 285 students who left are not evenly distributed; they cluster in certain grades and at the Batıkent branch. She reminds everyone that, apart from a single message sent to parents in June about re-enrollment, nobody was spoken to, and that half of the year's parent meetings were never held.

By the numbers

Re-enrollment rate: from 71% to 54% (335 of 620 students)
Shortfall against expectation: 105 students, about 10.1 million TL
Annual fee: from 62,000 TL to 96,000 TL (55% increase)
Conversations held with the 285 parents who didn't re-enroll: 0

What did Bilgi Çınarı Kursları do?

Onur Baysal's team reached 164 of the 285 parents in two weeks. 61 parents said price, 48 said nobody paid attention to their child, 33 said they had switched to online or private lessons, and 22 cited a move or a change of school. 31 of the 48 parents who complained of indifference were from the same six grades, and half of last year's parent meetings in those grades had not been held.

No discount campaign was run. Parents who said price had the number of installments raised from nine to twelve; those who said indifference were offered a meeting with a guidance counselor and a written follow-up plan. 46 students came back, worth 4.4 million TL in fees. Enrolled students rose to 381 and the re-enrollment rate to 61%. This year every student was assigned a guidance counselor, and re-enrollment conversations were moved up to March.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Hold off on the discount campaign. If the cause isn't price, the discount won't work, and it will also look unfair to the 335 parents who re-enrolled at the full fee.
  • List the 285 students who left by branch, grade and teacher. See where they cluster first.

2This week

  • Call the parents. Don't make a sales pitch, ask one question: what was the biggest factor in your not re-enrolling? Count the answers under three or four headings.
  • Treat the parent who says price separately from the parent who says indifference. Offer the first more installments; offer the second a meeting with a guidance counselor and a concrete follow-up plan.
  • In the grades where they cluster, look at last year's attendance, practice exam and parent meeting records. If the problem is concentrated in one teacher or one branch, the fix is not a campaign but that class.

3Lasting fix

  • Assign every student a guidance counselor and make a monthly parent meeting mandatory. If a parent hasn't talked to anyone all year, the only thing they'll talk about in September is the price.
  • Move re-enrollment conversations beyond the single message in June; start in March and hold each one face to face with the parent.
  • Track the re-enrollment rate by branch and grade; consider a student whose attendance rises mid-year a candidate for leaving, and call them then.
The lesson

Parents see the fee increase on the price list, but they decide whether it's worth it by the attention they received all year.
