640 disputes in one season: members don't trust the sample taken from their olives

Oleícola Montesira Food · Jaén, Spain · 38 employees · 412 farmer members, 2,400–3,100 tonnes of olive oil a year
The problem

Payment for olives is based on the oil content of a sample taken at delivery. The result arrives on paper three weeks later, and when a member disputes it, there is no sample left to show them.

How it was solved

It began taking the sample in front of the member, keeping a second sealed sample, and sending the result to their phone the same day. Disputes fell from 640 to 71 in a season.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

At Montesira, what a member is paid is decided not by the weight on the scale but by the oil that comes out of the olives. A sample is taken at every delivery, oil content and acidity are measured in the lab, and payment is calculated from that. In the 2024–25 season there were 9,800 deliveries, and members disputed 640 of them. Almost every dispute began with the same sentence: “We picked from the same slope as my neighbour, so why did his come out oilier?”

The team of Inmaculada Serrano, Head of Finance and Member Relations, spent an average of 45 minutes on each dispute, 480 hours over the season. The result never changed, because the sample was thrown away after analysis and there was nothing left to measure again. The member didn't see how the sample was taken and learned the result about three weeks later from a printed statement. At the end of the season, 14 members who left gave as their reason that they did not trust the measurement.

Andrés Cobo, Head of Mill and Quality, was sure the measurements were correct and put the disputes down to harvest fatigue. Managing Director Rafael Molinero had been putting off a 58,000 euro automatic sampling and rapid analysis system for three years; in his view, the money should go into the mill's capacity. Inmaculada Serrano's question was different: even if the measurement is right, how would the member know?

By the numbers

2024–25 season: 9,800 deliveries, 640 disputes (6.5%)
Time spent on one dispute: 45 minutes on average; 480 hours over the season
Time for the result to reach the member: about three weeks
Members who left saying they did not trust the measurement: 14

What did Oleícola Montesira do?

Rafael Molinero approved the investment in summer 2025. In the 2025–26 season, which began in November, a probe the member could watch took the sample from the trailer automatically, and every sample was split in two: one went to the rapid analysis instrument, the other was sealed and kept in the cold room with the member's signature. The same evening the result was sent to the member's phone as a message, together with that week's village average. For a member who disputed, the kept sample was sent to an independent laboratory; if no difference showed up, the member paid the 35 euro analysis fee.

Over the season there were 7,600 deliveries and 71 disputes; the rate fell from 6.5% to 0.9%. In eight of the 71 samples the independent laboratory found a significant difference from the mill's measurement; those eight members were paid the difference, and the instrument was recalibrated twice. Andrés Cobo presented the results at the general assembly himself. By the end of the season, no member had left citing the measurement.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Separate the disputes from the question of whether the measurement is accurate. The member is not disputing the result but a process they can't see; explaining how good the lab is won't fix that.
  • Pull together a breakdown of all 640 disputes: which village, which day, which shift. If the disputes cluster on particular days, look first at what happened on those days.

2This week

  • Take the sample in front of the member. Let them stand by the scale and see where on the trailer and at how many points it is taken.
  • Split every sample in two: one goes to analysis, the other is sealed and kept with the member's signature. If there is a dispute, there will be a sample to open.
  • Send the result to the member's phone the same day, not on paper three weeks later. The longer they wait, the more suspicion grows, and people forget which day's olives those were.
  • Define a clear route for disputes: the kept sample goes to an independent laboratory. If it shows a difference, the mill pays for the test and makes up the difference; if not, the member pays the analysis fee.

3Lasting fix

  • Set aside a morning for members at the start of the season and show them the sample's path from start to finish: scale, sampling, laboratory, payment calculation.
  • Share oil content results together with the village and weekly averages. A member who compares their result with that week's average rather than with their neighbour's is less suspicious.
  • Present the number of disputes and the share of members who turned out to be right to the general assembly every season. Showing that the mill can be wrong too doesn't reduce trust.
The lesson

Measuring correctly isn't enough; the person whose pay depends on the measurement has to be able to see it and test it.
