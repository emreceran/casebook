The customer said “it can be done without grounding”: haggling a 96,000 TL quote down to 61,000 TL

Rıfat Dönmezer Elektrik Self-employed · Bursa · 2 employees · About 30 jobs a month
The problem

The owner of a laundromat opening in an old building showed him a cheap quote from another electrician and wants the same price. The difference comes from the safety items, and Rıfat Dönmezer's calendar is empty for two weeks.

How it was solved

He split the quote into safety items and everything else and brought the price down to 79,000 TL, but wouldn't agree to work without grounding. He lost the job; three weeks later the same shop called him because the breaker kept tripping.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

At the end of August, Rıfat Dönmezer quoted 96,000 TL for a laundromat opening on the ground floor of a forty-year-old building. The shop will run six washers and four dryers. The quote includes a new panel, a residual-current relay, a grounding line, and a new main line from the meter to the shop; these four items come to 58,000 TL.

The shop owner had gotten a 61,000 TL quote from another electrician. That quote has no grounding, the work is added onto the building's old line, and the panel stays where it is. The shop owner spoke plainly: “The building has stood like this for forty years and nothing has happened. You do it for 61,000 too, the job is yours.”

Rıfat Dönmezer's calendar is empty for the next two weeks, and the pay of his apprentice Emirhan Koçak is due at the end of the month. Emirhan Koçak is in favor of taking the job: “If we don't do it, the other guy will, and do it worse.” Rıfat Dönmezer, though, doesn't want to put his name to ten machines running on wet floor without grounding. In a fire or an electric shock, the first question will be who did the wiring.

By the numbers

Rıfat Dönmezer's quote: 96,000 TL; safety items 58,000 TL
The other electrician's quote: 61,000 TL, no grounding and no new main line
Machines that will run in the shop: 6 washers, 4 dryers
Time empty on the calendar: 2 weeks

What did Rıfat Dönmezer Elektrik do?

Rıfat Dönmezer rewrote the quote. He left the safety items at 58,000 TL under a separate heading. From the remaining 38,000 TL, he took out the fixtures, moved the cable to surface-mounted conduit, and switched the outlets and switches to the standard series; this part dropped to 21,000 TL. The new quote came to 79,000 TL, with a note underneath: “No work taken without grounding and a residual-current relay.” The shop owner thought it over for two days and gave the job to the other electrician for 61,000 TL. In the two empty weeks, Rıfat Dönmezer did nine small jobs, worth 47,000 TL in all.

The laundromat opened in mid-September. On September 17 the shop owner called: when two dryers ran at the same time the breaker tripped, and a customer said they had gotten an electric shock from the body of a machine. Rıfat Dönmezer took measurements; the old line couldn't carry the load. Between September 22 and 26 he did the new main line, the panel, the residual-current relay and the grounding for 58,000 TL; the shop stayed closed four days. The shop owner's total spending on the wiring reached 119,000 TL. Rıfat Dönmezer now shows the safety items under a separate heading in all his quotes.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Split the quote in two: the items that aren't negotiable and the ones that are. Grounding, the residual-current relay, the panel and the main line are in the first group; light fixtures, outlet covers, and whether the cable runs under or over the plaster are in the second.
  • Don't just tell the customer “no,” show them why. Write the current ten machines will draw next to the cross-section of the old line. A customer who sees the numbers argues with the math, not with the electrician.

2This week

  • Make the discount from the second group. Let the customer buy the fixtures themselves, run the cable in surface-mounted conduit, and fit standard outlets instead of decorative ones. The price drops and the safety stays the same.
  • Don't take the job without the safety items, and say so in writing. Under the quote, write in one sentence which items can't be removed and why, send the quote by message and keep it.
  • If the customer goes to the other electrician, don't close the door. Saying “call me if there's a problem and I'll come and take measurements” costs nothing.
  • Fill the two empty weeks with small fixed-price jobs. Taking a bad job because of an empty calendar puts the next years' work at risk.

3Lasting fix

  • In every quote, show the safety items under a separate heading. Let the customer know from the start what is up for negotiation.
  • Keep the quotes and correspondence for jobs you turn down. If a question about that shop ever comes up, you'll have the documents.
  • Always keep a short list of small jobs good for a few days on the calendar. An electrician with an empty calendar is weak in negotiation.
The lesson

You can come down on price; the electrician decides which item to come down on, not the customer.
