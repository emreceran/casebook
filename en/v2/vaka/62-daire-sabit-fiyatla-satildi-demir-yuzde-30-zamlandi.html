62 apartments sold at a fixed price, rebar up 30%

Atabey Yapı Construction · Antalya · 60 employees · About 180 homes a year, 3 construction sites
The problem

Just as rough construction began on a 96-apartment project, rebar and concrete prices shot up. The extra cost is 18.5 million TL, while the price of the apartments already sold is fixed by contract.

How it was solved

It left the sold apartments alone. It locked in 900 tons of rebar at a fixed price and raised the price of the 34 remaining apartments to 7.1 million TL; 21 of them sold in three months.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

Atabey Yapı broke ground on the 96-apartment Narlıbahçe Evleri project and sold 62 apartments at an average of 6.5 million TL, at a fixed price. Just as rough construction was beginning, two pieces of news arrived: the price of rebar rose from 27,000 TL to 35,100 TL per ton in three months, and ready-mix concrete went from 3,200 TL to 4,000 TL per cubic meter.

Construction Sites Coordinator Levent Karataş calculated that the remaining work requires 1,400 tons of rebar and 9,000 cubic meters of concrete. The difference on rebar is 11.34 million TL and on concrete 7.2 million TL, for a total of 18.54 million TL. The project's expected profit was 75 million TL. In other words, a quarter of the profit was gone before a single floor had gone up.

Rüstem Atabey wants to send a price adjustment letter to the owners of the 62 sold apartments: about 300,000 TL per apartment. Tuba Atabey Ersoy is against it. The contracts have no price adjustment clause; the letter would have no legal basis and would also kill sales of the 34 remaining apartments. In her view, the difference should come from the unsold apartments and from purchasing.

By the numbers

Apartments sold: 62 of 96, average 6.5 million TL, fixed price
Rebar: from 27,000 TL to 35,100 TL per ton (30%), 1,400 tons needed
Concrete: from 3,200 TL to 4,000 TL per cubic meter (25%), 9,000 cubic meters needed
Extra cost: 18.54 million TL; expected project profit: 75 million TL

What did Atabey Yapı do?

No price adjustment letter was sent. Tuba Atabey Ersoy paused sales of the 34 remaining apartments for a week and raised the price from 6.5 million TL to 7.1 million TL. Levent Karataş signed a fixed-price deal with a rebar supplier for 900 tons at 33,500 TL per ton; that saved 1.44 million TL compared with the current price and brought the extra cost down to 17.1 million TL.

At the new price, 21 apartments sold in three months; these sales brought in 12.6 million TL more than the old price would have. If the remaining 13 apartments sell at the same price, the gap will be closed entirely. On its next project, Atabey Yapı put only 40% of the apartments on sale at the foundation stage and locked in the rebar for the sold apartments in the same month.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Don't touch the sold apartments. Demanding a price adjustment that isn't in the contract creates a receivable you can't collect and 62 resentful customers; both cost more than 18.5 million TL.
  • Stop sales of the 34 remaining apartments today. Every apartment sold at the old price shrinks the source that could cover the difference.

2This week

  • Talk to rebar suppliers about a fixed-price bulk purchase. Lock in not your entire need but the portion that will be used over the next six months; don't bury all your cash in rebar.
  • Recalculate the price of the remaining 34 apartments. Closing the gap takes about 545,000 TL per apartment, which means an 8.4% increase. Compare with nearby projects to see whether the market can bear it.
  • Get quotes from two more batching plants for concrete and fix a monthly price according to the pouring schedule.
  • Bring the owners of sold apartments an optional offer: a small discount for those who pay off their remaining installments early. Cash that arrives early can be used to lock in materials at today's price.

3Lasting fix

  • Tie the pace of sales to the pace of construction. Sell no more than 40% of the apartments at the foundation stage; price the rest once most of the costs are known.
  • The day you make a fixed-price sale, also lock in the rebar and concrete for those apartments. Fixing revenue while leaving costs open is a bet against price increases.
  • Build an explicit price-increase allowance for key materials into the project budget, and don't spend that allowance as if it were profit.
The lesson

The day you fix the sale price, you must fix your cost too.
