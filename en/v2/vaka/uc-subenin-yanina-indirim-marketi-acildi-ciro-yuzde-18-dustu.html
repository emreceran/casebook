A discount market opened next to three stores, and revenue fell 18 percent

Günizli Market Retail · Bursa · 320 employees · 14 stores
The problem

The discount stores that opened in July are taking 3.78 million TL a month in revenue from three stores. The general manager wants to match prices on 200 products.

How it was solved

Matched prices on 30 products instead of 200, and expanded the fresh sections at the three stores and moved them to the entrance. The revenue loss narrowed from 18 percent to 7 percent in three months.

What happened?

In early July a national discount chain opened stores right next to three Günizli locations: one on the same street, two a hundred meters away. Combined monthly revenue at these three stores was 21 million TL in June and fell to 17.22 million TL in August. An 18 percent drop, at a 24 percent gross margin, means about 907,000 TL a month in lost gross profit.

General Manager Orhan Günizli wants to act fast: match the discount chain's prices on the 200 products customers buy most. Those products bring in 4.5 million TL a month at the three stores; an average 12 percent price cut would cost another 540,000 TL a month in profit.

Purchasing and Category Manager Meral Dinçer went through the receipts and saw a different picture. In packaged goods (dry food, cleaning products, beverages), which make up 52 percent of revenue, the drop is 31 percent. In fresh products, which make up 48 percent, meaning meat, produce, deli and bakery, the drop is only 4 percent. In her view the customers haven't left; they've split their basket in two. Competing on price would mean playing on the other side's strongest ground.

By the numbers

Monthly revenue of the three stores: from 21 million TL to 17.22 million TL (an 18 percent drop)
Drop in packaged goods: 31 percent; in fresh products: 4 percent
Gross profit loss: about 907,000 TL a month
Cost of matching prices on 200 products: 540,000 TL a month

What did Günizli Market do?

Meral Dinçer identified the 30 products whose prices customers know best, and prices on them were matched to the discount market. These products brought in 1.5 million TL a month at the three stores, and an average 10 percent price cut cost 150,000 TL a month, less than a third of the 200-product plan. At the three stores the butcher and produce sections were moved to the entrance, the deli counter was extended, and hot-bread hours were introduced at the bakery.

Combined revenue of the three stores rose from 17.22 million TL in August to 19.53 million TL in November, 7 percent below the June level. Fresh product revenue climbed 9 percent above June. Most of the loss in packaged goods did not come back; Günizli reduced the space given to those sections in the three stores.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Before cutting prices, find out where the loss is. If the drop is 31 percent in packaged goods and 4 percent in fresh, customers haven't abandoned you; they've moved part of their shopping next door.
  • Send someone to the discount market and have them write down its shelves: which products it carries, which it doesn't, where the price gap is big. Know your rival from a list, not from a guess.

2This week

  • Match prices not on 200 products but on the 30 whose prices customers know by heart: bread, milk, eggs, sugar, sunflower oil. The perception of expensive or cheap is formed on these items.
  • Expand the fresh sections at the three stores and move them to the entrance: butcher, produce, deli, bakery. The discount market has no counter; your strongest card is where it has nothing.
  • From loyalty card data, pull the customers who came in June but not in August and send them an offer valid on fresh products.
  • Put your own-label products at eye level in these three stores. They are the closest to the discount market's prices.

3Lasting fix

  • Position each store against the competitor around it. A store with a discount market next door and one without don't need the same shelf layout or product range.
  • Track revenue by section, not as a store total; see which basket you're losing in the first month.
  • Watch new store permits and shops for rent. Prepare two months before a rival opens, not after.
The lesson

Compete where the rival is absent, not where it is strongest.
