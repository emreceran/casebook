31 farmers gave their milk to a competitor, and daily milk fell from 40 tons to 32

Koruçak Süt Ürünleri Food · Balıkesir · 75 employees · 40 tons of milk a day, 186 farmers
The problem

A large competitor pays 1.20 TL more per liter and pays within 15 days. Matching the price would cost Koruçak 1.44 million TL a month.

How it was solved

It didn't match the price; it cut the payment term from 45 days to 30, introduced a quality premium and opened up the analysis results to farmers. In two months 17 farmers came back, and daily milk rose to 38,500 liters.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

Koruçak collects its milk from 186 farmers. In the last six weeks, 31 of them, mostly owners of large herds, started giving their milk to a national dairy company that has newly entered the region. Daily milk collected fell from 40,000 liters to 32,000 liters. The plant is running 20% short of capacity; for two weeks, small customers' orders have been filled only in part.

The competitor pays 24 TL a liter, Koruçak 22.80 TL. The competitor pays within 15 days and gives feed advances to those who want them; Koruçak pays in 45 days. Cevdet Koruçak wants to match the price right away. Gülşen Koruçak shows the math: you'd have to raise the price not just for those who left but for everyone, and a difference of 1.20 TL per liter on 40,000 liters a day comes to 1.44 million TL a month. After the milk price increase, there is no profit that can bear this money.

Raw Milk Procurement Lead Recep Dursun toured the villages and spoke with 22 of the 31 farmers who left. 14 complained about late payment, nine named the price, and six the feed advance. A few mentioned something else: they don't know how the deductions based on the milk's fat and protein analysis are calculated, and they don't see the results. According to Recep Dursun, matching the price is the most expensive answer and the easiest one to copy.

By the numbers

Farmers who left: 31 of 186; daily milk from 40,000 liters to 32,000 liters
Price: competitor 24 TL, Koruçak 22.80 TL; payment: competitor 15 days, Koruçak 45 days
Cost of a 1.20 TL increase for everyone: 1.44 million TL a month
Reason for leaving (22 interviews): 14 late payment, 9 price, 6 feed advance

What did Koruçak Süt Ürünleri do?

The price was not matched. The payment term was cut from 45 days to 30 for all farmers; the financing cost is about 513,000 TL a month. A quality premium of 0.60 TL per liter was introduced for milk meeting fat, protein and cleanliness criteria; about 21,000 liters of milk a day earned the premium, which came to about 380,000 TL a month. The two together total 893,000 TL a month, well below the 1.44 million TL cost of matching the price. Every farmer began receiving their analysis result and the calculation by text message.

Within two months, 17 of the 31 farmers who left came back, and nine new farmers were added. Daily milk collected rose from 32,000 liters to 38,500 liters. Most of the 14 farmers who did not return said they could not leave because they owed the competitor over a feed advance. Koruçak signed annual purchase contracts with its 40 largest farmers.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Listen to those who left before matching the price; that is exactly what Recep Dursun did. If 14 of 22 farmers named late payment and nine named price, the weight of the problem is in the term, not the price.
  • Visit the twenty largest of the remaining 155 farmers this week. The competitor's representative has knocked on their doors too; talking to them before they go is cheaper than winning them back after.

2This week

  • Cut the payment term from 45 days to 30 and work out the cost: 15 days of milk payments requires about 13.7 million TL of working capital; at 45% interest the cost is about 513,000 TL a month. One third of matching the price.
  • Introduce a quality premium: an extra payment per liter for milk whose fat, protein and cleanliness exceed a set level. The money goes to the farmer who supplies the best milk; it isn't spread across everyone.
  • Send every farmer the analysis result for their milk and how deductions or premiums are calculated, in writing. No farmer trusts an analysis whose result they can't see.
  • Keep the door open to farmers who want to come back, and don't offend them; the competitor won't pay the early months' price forever.

3Lasting fix

  • Make annual purchase contracts with the large herd owners: purchase guarantee, floor price and premium terms in writing.
  • Buy feed collectively on the farmers' behalf and deduct the cost from the milk payment. Against the competitor's advance, what you can offer is cheap feed.
  • Track each farmer's daily milk volume weekly. A farmer whose milk is declining may have started splitting their milk before leaving.
The lesson

A farmer takes his milk not to whoever pays the most, but to whoever pays on time and shows how the account is calculated.
