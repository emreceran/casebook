Body balm melted in transit during the heatwave: 364 complaints in two weeks

Savonnerie Clairvent E-commerce · Lyon, France · 14 employees · 62,000 orders a year
The problem

In the first two weeks of August, more than one in four parcels containing balm or solid deodorant arrived with melted product. The shop's rating fell from 4.7 to 4.3.

How it was solved

In hot weeks it handed balm and deodorant to the carrier only Monday to Wednesday, closed locker delivery and moved the product into a metal tin. In the second heatwave the complaint rate fell to 3.2%.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

In the first two weeks of August, temperatures in most of France rose above 36 degrees. In those two weeks Clairvent shipped 4,150 parcels; 1,280 of them contained solid body balm or solid deodorant. Into the inbox of Solène Guichard, Head of Customer Service, came 364 complaints, 28% of those parcels. The photos looked alike: inside the cardboard box, a melted oil stain that had spread onto the other products.

Solène Guichard sent a replacement to everyone who complained; at an average of 19 euros a parcel, that came to 6,916 euros. The reviews hurt more: the shop's rating on the review site fell from 4.7 to 4.3 in two weeks. A breakdown of the complaints showed something. Of the 610 parcels that went to a pickup point or parcel locker, 241 had melted, against 123 of the 670 parcels delivered to the home. 187 of the complaints came from parcels handed to the carrier on Friday and Saturday.

Nadia Belkacem, Head of E-commerce, suggested taking these two products off sale for the summer, but balm and deodorant made up 22% of summer revenue. Thibault Renaudin, Head of Formulation, was against hardening the product: the balm started to soften at 34 degrees because it is meant to melt immediately on the skin. Founder Camille Vasseur was thinking of moving to insulated cooling packaging, which cost 2.80 euros per parcel.

By the numbers

Parcels containing balm or deodorant in two weeks: 1,280; complaints: 364 (28%)
Pickup point and locker: 241 of 610 parcels (39.5%); home delivery: 123 of 670 (18.4%)
Cost of resending: 6,916 euros; shop rating from 4.7 to 4.3
Share of balm and deodorant in summer revenue: 22%

What did Savonnerie Clairvent do?

Solène Guichard sent new product to all 364 customers without asking for returns. In mid-August three changes were made at once. When the forecast was above 30 degrees, orders containing balm and deodorant were handed to the carrier only on Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday, and customers were told the reason when they ordered. The parcel locker option was closed for these products. A metal tin with a lid replaced the cardboard box; the cost per tin rose by 0.42 euros. Insulated cooling packaging was not judged necessary.

Thibault Renaudin raised the share of plant wax in the formula from 12% to 16%; the balm now starts to soften at 38 degrees. In the second heatwave in the last week of August, 590 parcels containing these products were sent and 19 complaints came in: 3.2%. The shop's rating rose to 4.6 in mid-September. Balm and deodorant stayed on sale all summer.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Send a replacement to everyone who complained without asking, but not by the same route. If you send a second parcel in the same box in the same heat to replace the melted product, you get two complaints.
  • When the forecast is above 30 degrees, stop shipping these two products today and tell the customers with orders. A balm that arrives three days late is better than a balm that arrives melted.

2This week

  • Break the complaints down by delivery type and day of dispatch. The problem may be not the time the product spends in transit but the time it spends in a hot locker or in a depot over the weekend.
  • In hot weeks, hand these products to the carrier only in the first days of the week; no parcel should spend the weekend in the carrier's depot.
  • Look at what happens when the product melts. When balm melts in a cardboard box, the product is finished; in a metal tin with a lid, it sets again as it cools. If you can't prevent melting, make it harmless.
  • Try to raise the formula by a few degrees without spoiling the feel on the skin. The difference between 34 and 38 degrees is decisive in a delivery van on a summer day.

3Lasting fix

  • Put every new product through a heat-resistance test before launch: in its packaged form, at 40 degrees, for 48 hours.
  • Tie your shipping rules to the weather forecast and state this clearly on the site. If the customer knows why they are waiting, they will wait.
  • Record complaints not just by number but with delivery type and day. This time the breakdown was done by hand; next time have it ready.
The lesson

It isn't enough for the product to be sound in the workshop; the worst place it passes through on the way to the customer's door is part of the design too.
