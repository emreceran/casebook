The biggest customer arrived with 11 custom requests: if the answer is yes, the product roadmap stops for six months

Kodmer Yazılım Software · Ankara · 34 employees · 5,200 subscriber businesses
The problem

The customer that brings in 8 percent of revenue wants developments made only for itself and offers 2.4 million TL. The real cost of the work is 7.56 million TL and seven of nine developers.

How it was solved

Sorted the 11 requests one by one: took six into the product as features everyone will use, left three to the customer's developers through a connection interface and rejected two. The customer signed the three-year contract.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

Kodmer's biggest customer is a network of accounting firms that lets its 420 member businesses use the software under its own umbrella. It pays 399,000 TL a month, about 8 percent of the company's revenue. Its contract ends at the end of the year. The network's management brought an 11-item list for the renewal: consolidated reports across branches, a direct connection to its own enterprise software, approval steps built just for them. In return they offer 2.4 million TL for development and a three-year contract.

Technical Director Ozan Baysal worked out the job: seven of the nine developers will work only on this for six months. At a monthly cost of 180,000 TL per developer, that comes to 7.56 million TL. The product roadmap will also stop for six months. At the top of that roadmap is automatic import of bank account transactions, which 1,300 customers want.

İpek Soyer is in favor of saying yes: 8 percent of revenue and a three-year contract can't be turned down, and if the customer leaves, finding 420 small subscribers to replace it would take more than a year. According to Ozan Baysal, code written for a single customer has to be maintained forever, and the remaining 4,780 customers will see no improvements for six months. Kerem Odabaşı agrees with both of them.

By the numbers

Customer's monthly payment: 399,000 TL (about 8 percent of revenue), 420 member businesses
Number of requests: 11; offered fee: 2.4 million TL and a three-year contract
Real cost: 7 developers × 6 months × 180,000 TL = 7.56 million TL
Roadmap item waiting: bank import wanted by 1,300 customers

What did Kodmer Yazılım do?

Kerem Odabaşı put the list on the table item by item. Six requests about consolidated reports across branches would also serve 610 customers with multiple branches; these were added to the roadmap and written in two months by three developers. For three requests concerning the customer's own enterprise software, a data connection interface was opened; a software firm the customer contracted wrote the connection. Two requests specific to that customer's workflow alone were rejected.

The customer signed the three-year contract and paid a priority fee of 1.1 million TL to use the new features three months ahead of everyone else. The product roadmap slipped by six weeks, not six months; bank import was released in February. The consolidated reports were offered as a separate package: 140 of the 610 multi-branch customers moved up to this package for a 400 TL monthly difference. That means 56,000 TL a month in additional revenue.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Don't say yes or no to the list as a whole. Open up the 11 requests one by one and ask about each: which of my other customers would use this?
  • Give the customer a date right away: tell them an itemized answer will come within two weeks. Silence reads worse than a no.

2This week

  • Divide the requests into three: those that would also help other customers, those specific to this customer alone, and those that run against the product's direction.
  • Put the first group on the product roadmap and give the customer the right to use them before everyone else. Code written as a product feature, not as custom work, is maintained with everyone's money.
  • For the second group, open a data connection interface in the software; let the customer's own developers or a solution partner write the customer-specific connection. You build the door, they furnish the room.
  • Talk about the price based on the real cost. A customer that puts 2.4 million TL on a 7.56 million TL job doesn't know the size of the work; show them the figure.

3Lasting fix

  • Set a written rule for custom development: only work that at least a certain number of customers will use goes into the product, and the rest is solved through the interface.
  • Track any single customer's share of revenue. Eight percent is close to the threshold at which one customer can hold the product roadmap hostage.
  • Hold a product roadmap meeting with large customers twice a year. Requests shouldn't arrive as a list at contract renewal, they should be discussed during the year.
The lesson

A big customer's request is not a yes-or-no question; it is a question of which parts are product and which are custom work.
