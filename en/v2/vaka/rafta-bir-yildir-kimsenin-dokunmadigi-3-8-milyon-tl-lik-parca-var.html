There are 3.8 million TL worth of parts on the shelves that nobody has touched in a year

Usta Oto Servis Automotive · Adana · 42 employees · 3 branches, 1,200 vehicles a month
The problem

Forty percent of the 9.5 million TL of parts in the three branches' warehouses has not moved in a year. While money runs through loans, it is gathering dust on the shelves.

How it was solved

It split the idle stock into three and found a separate remedy for each: returns to the wholesaler, use across branches, and sales to other garages. In three months, 2.3 million TL of the 3.8 million TL melted away.

What happened?

At Usta Oto Servis the year-end count was done early, and General Manager Ozan Gündoğdu ran into a number he hadn't expected. The three branches' warehouses hold 9.5 million TL of spare parts; 3.8 million TL of that has not gone into a single work order in the last 12 months.

The breakdown falls into three items. 1.5 million TL of parts belong to old models rarely seen on the road anymore. 1.1 million TL of parts were special-ordered for a customer who then backed out of the job; no deposit was taken. And 1.2 million TL of parts are actually double stock: because the branches can't see each other's shelves, they bought the same parts separately.

Spare Parts Lead Levent Aydemir defends himself: the wholesaler's “buy 10, get 1 free” deals bring costs down, and if a part isn't on the shelf, the car sits on the lift and the customer leaves. Ozan Gündoğdu's math is different: the company is borrowing at 45% interest; 3.8 million TL sitting on the shelf means 1.7 million TL a year in interest.

By the numbers

Total parts inventory: 9.5 million TL
Idle for a year: 3.8 million TL (40%)
Old models 1.5 million TL, canceled special orders 1.1 million TL, double stock 1.2 million TL
Annual interest burden of the idle stock: about 1.7 million TL

What did Usta Oto Servis do?

Levent Aydemir merged the three branches' stock into a single list. 800,000 TL of the double stock was used within three months by moving parts between branches; no new purchases were made for those parts. The wholesaler took back 900,000 TL of unopened parts at a 15% deduction and credited the account with 765,000 TL.

600,000 TL of old-model parts were sold to the garages and parts dealers who received the list at 60% of cost, for 360,000 TL. That way 2.3 million TL of the 3.8 million TL of idle stock melted away, leaving 1.5 million TL on the shelves. The 50% deposit rule on special orders began; over the next three months, canceled special orders fell from 14 to 3.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Don't treat the 3.8 million TL as a single problem; the remedy for each of the three items is different. Double stock isn't goods to sell but goods to use, whereas old-model parts lose a bit more value every month.
  • Set a rule for the three branches starting today: a part found at another branch on the shared list is not reordered, it is requested from that branch.

2This week

  • Talk to the wholesaler about returns. Give back parts bought in the last 12 months with unopened packaging, even at a deduction; a 15% deduction is cheaper than 45% interest.
  • Send the list of old-model parts to other garages and parts dealers in town. A part lying dead on your shelf may be one another mechanic has been looking for for three days.
  • Start taking a 50% deposit on special orders right away. A part for a customer who backs out shouldn't sit on the shelf tying up your capital.
  • Work out the real math of the “buy 10, get 1 free” deals: the eleventh part is free, but the interest on the five parts that don't sell is not.

3Lasting fix

  • Merge the three branches' stock into a single list and set up a system that moves parts between branches every day.
  • Set a maximum stock quantity for every part: as many units as were used in the last six months. Purchases above that go to the General Manager for approval.
  • List the parts that haven't moved for six months every month; make the decision to dispose of them without waiting a year.
The lesson

A part sitting on the shelf looks like security; in fact it is a receivable on which interest accrues every month.
