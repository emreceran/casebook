Self-employedSeptember 24, 2026

Only 7 of 26 free site visits turn into jobs: five working days a month go to the road

Rıfat Dönmezer Elektrik Self-employed · Bursa · 2 employees · About 30 jobs a month
The problem

Rıfat Dönmezer goes to the home of everyone who calls and gives a free price. In August he spent 43 hours on site visits; 19 of the visits led to no job.

How it was solved

He made a fixed price list for small jobs, started giving preliminary prices from photos, and set the on-site visit at 750 TL, deducted from the job. In the first three weeks of September, time spent on visits dropped from 43 hours to 15, and the number of jobs won went up.

What happened?

Rıfat Dönmezer likes to see the job on site before giving a price. In August he went on 26 site visits. In Bursa traffic, each visit, travel included, took an average of 1 hour 40 minutes; 43 hours in total, or five working days. Seven of those visits turned into jobs, and he never heard back from 19.

Together with his apprentice he produces 9,000 TL of labor a day; during the hours he's out on site visits, work stops or Emirhan Koçak is left on his own. Five days means 45,000 TL of labor a month. On top of that, at the visit he doesn't only give a price; he also draws up the materials list and leaves it with the customer. He later learned that two customers took that list to another electrician.

Charging for site visits has crossed his mind, but he hesitates. Most electricians in Bursa do the visit for free; he thinks, “No one calls an electrician who asks for money.” Emirhan Koçak suggests giving prices over the phone. Rıfat Dönmezer is against that too: on jobs where a price he gave sight unseen didn't hold, he has always taken the loss himself.

By the numbers

Site visits in August: 26; turned into jobs: 7
Time spent on site visits: 43 hours (about 5 working days)
One day of labor income: 9,000 TL; five days is 45,000 TL
Customers who took the materials list to another electrician: 2

What did Rıfat Dönmezer Elektrik do?

At the start of September, Rıfat Dönmezer prepared a twelve-item fixed price list and began asking everyone who called five questions and for a photo. The on-site visit became 750 TL; if the job is taken, it's deducted from the price. In the first three weeks of September, 24 people called. Eleven were given a price over the phone from a photo, nine got a site visit, and 4 backed out when they heard the visit fee.

Of the 11 jobs priced over the phone, 5 were won, and of the 9 jobs with a site visit, 6 were won; 11 jobs in all. In August, 7 jobs were won in a whole month. Time spent on visits dropped from 43 hours to 15. From the nine visits, 6,750 TL in fees was charged; six were deducted from the job price, and 2,250 TL remained for three. On one of the jobs priced from a photo, an unexpected old line turned up inside the wall, and Rıfat Dönmezer covered the 1,800 TL difference himself. The materials list is now given only after the job is approved.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Sort August's 26 site visits by type. How many were jobs like outlets, chandeliers and breakers that could be priced without seeing, and how many were renovations that really needed a look on site?
  • Write a fixed price list for the small jobs. The price of an outlet, a chandelier, a breaker doesn't change from house to house; you don't set out on the road for that.

2This week

  • Ask everyone who calls the same five questions and ask for a photo: what needs doing, how old the building is, where the panel is, whether the breaker trips, when it should be done. Half of jobs can be priced from a photo.
  • Make the on-site visit paid, but deduct the fee from the job. The visit is still free for the customer who gives you the job; only the one who will collect prices and leave pays the fee.
  • Don't give the materials list at the visit. At the visit you give the total price and the work items; the detailed list comes out once the job is approved.
  • Bundle the visits into two set afternoons a week and order them by neighborhood. Don't drop a job in the middle of the day to cross to the other end of the city.

3Lasting fix

  • Keep two numbers every month: how many inquiries came in and how many turned into jobs. If the ratio falls, look first at which jobs you go on visits for, not at price.
  • Update the fixed price list twice a year and send it to callers by message.
  • Don't state the visit fee as if apologizing. You have an hour and forty minutes and travel costs; an electrician who says so takes his work seriously.
The lesson

If you don't know the value of the time you give away free, the customer never will.
