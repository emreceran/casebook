“Buy 5, get 1 free” gives away 11,500 cups a month, most of them to people who come every day anyway

Demli Kahve Food & Drink · Ankara · 80 employees · 9 branches
The problem

72% of the free coffees go to 1,900 customers who come at least five days a week. The program raised sales by only 4% in a year.

How it was solved

It did not drop the program; it raised the threshold to 1 free per 8 coffees and protected accumulated rewards. Free cups fell from 11,500 to 6,900, and monthly revenue rose by 504,000 TL.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

For a year, the four branches Demli Kahve runs itself have had a “Buy 5, get 1 free” program. These branches sell 96,000 cups of coffee a month and give away 11,500 cups free. The average cup price is 140 TL; the list value of the free coffees is 1.61 million TL. The four branches' monthly net profit is 1.3 million TL.

Finance Manager Cihan Bulut went through the card records. 8,300 of the free cups, or 72%, go to 1,900 customers who come at least five days a week. These customers came every day before the program, too. 8,300 cups at 140 TL comes to 1.16 million TL a month. Since the program began, the number of cups sold has grown by only 4%. According to Cihan Bulut, the company is giving away an amount close to its profit every month to customers who were coming anyway.

Marketing Lead Nazlı Erkan is against dropping the program: “Those 1,900 people are our best customers. The rival two streets over does buy 4, get 1; if we take the gift back, they'll be the first place they go.” Founder Alper Sönmez is caught between the two; he doesn't want to lose either the profit or the customers whose names he knows every morning.

By the numbers

96,000 cups sold a month, 11,500 cups given away free
List value of the free coffees: 1.61 million TL (140 TL per cup)
8,300 of the free cups go to 1,900 customers who come at least five days a week
Monthly net profit of the four branches 1.3 million TL; sales increase 4%

What did Demli Kahve do?

Demli Kahve announced the change a month ahead and moved to the 1-in-8 scheme; accumulated rewards could be redeemed under the old rule for 60 days. For customers who come once or twice a week, a free dessert on the third visit of the week began. At Nazlı Erkan's suggestion, the 1,900 customers who come every day were given first invitation to tastings of new beans.

Three months later, free cups fell from 11,500 to 6,900; calculated at an ingredient cost of 38 TL per cup, that dropped from 437,000 TL to 262,000 TL. Paid cups rose from 96,000 to 99,600, meaning an extra 504,000 TL a month in revenue. About 140 customers wrote complaint messages. The mass exodus Nazlı Erkan feared did not happen; the number of customers coming every day fell from 1,900 to 1,840.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • First ask the right question: whose behavior is this program changing? A free coffee given to a customer who already comes every day is a reward, but it brings no sales.
  • Split customers into three by visit frequency: those who come every day, those who come once or twice a week, those who stop by once a month. Look at how much extra sales the free coffee brings in each group.

2This week

  • Don't drop the program, raise the threshold: 1 free for every 8 instead of every 5. The customer who comes every day still wins, but gets two or three free coffees a month instead of four.
  • Steer the free coffee toward the branch's quiet hours. A free coffee handed out in the line at 8:00 a.m. blocks a sale; one given between 2:00 and 5:00 p.m. fills an empty table.
  • Make a separate offer to the customer who comes once or twice a week: this week, on your third visit, the dessert is on us. This is the group whose frequency you can raise.
  • Announce the change a month ahead and don't burn accumulated rewards. You can change the rule, but you can't take back a promise already given.

3Lasting fix

  • Measure the program not by cups given away but by the extra sales it brings, and redo this calculation every three months.
  • Give your most loyal customers things other than money: let them taste the new beans first, have their name written on the cup without asking. It isn't a discount that keeps someone who comes every day.
  • Don't take the rival's promotion as your yardstick. You don't know their profit; perhaps they're making the same mistake.
The lesson

A gift given to a customer who would come anyway doesn't create loyalty, it only reduces profit.
