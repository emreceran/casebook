A 14-year distributorship is ending: the manufacturer is opening its own office in Turkey

Medikon Tıbbi Cihaz Healthcare · Ankara · 28 employees · 9 service engineers, 640 installed devices
The problem

The foreign manufacturer that brings in 62 percent of revenue will handle sales itself in nine months. It is offering Medikon a service partnership.

How it was solved

Did not sue; negotiated a five-year authorized service contract, a Central Anatolia dealership and a stock buyback, and took on a second brand. Revenue fell from 180 million TL to 131 million TL and the manufacturer's share dropped to 38 percent.

What would you do? 3 comments ↓

What happened?

Medikon has been the Turkish representative of Helmgard Medizintechnik, a German imaging device manufacturer, for 14 years. The manufacturer's regional manager came to Ankara and announced the decision: in nine months their own office will open in Istanbul and sales will be made directly. The role offered to Medikon is authorized service partner, with a 6 percent commission on the sales it brings in.

Revenue from this brand is 111.6 million TL a year; 62 percent of the 180 million TL total. Of that, 78 million TL is device sales and 33.6 million TL is service and consumables. There is 12 million TL of spare parts for this brand sitting in the warehouse.

Ferhat Günay is furious: he says they built the market from scratch, that they have a right to compensation on termination of the contract, and he wants to sue. Tülin Erkan looks elsewhere: the service records of the 410 devices installed in hospitals, the nine engineers who know those devices and a 14-year relationship with purchasing departments are all at Medikon. In her view the manufacturer cannot get through its first year in Turkey without these, and the negotiation should start from there.

By the numbers

Manufacturer's revenue share: 62 percent, 111.6 million TL a year
Breakdown: 78 million TL device sales, 33.6 million TL service and consumables
Devices installed in hospitals: 410; spare parts in warehouse: 12 million TL
Time until the office opens: 9 months

What did Medikon Tıbbi Cihaz do?

Ferhat Günay had a lawyer review the contract but did not sue. Tülin Erkan took a written counteroffer to the manufacturer. At the end of three months of talks, Medikon secured a five-year authorized service contract, a regional dealership for Central Anatolia and a 12 percent commission on sales in that region; the manufacturer bought back the portion of the warehouse stock belonging to new models. In the same period Medikon took on the distributorship of another company that makes sterilization equipment.

In the first year after the transition revenue fell from 180 million TL to 131 million TL. Income from the manufacturer came to 49.8 million TL: 36 million TL in service and 13.8 million TL in commission; its share dropped from 62 percent to 38 percent. Other business, with 12.8 million TL brought in by the new brand, rose from 68.4 million TL to 81.2 million TL. Because service and commission income is more profitable, gross profit declined from 41 million TL to 36 million TL; revenue fell 27 percent while profit fell 12 percent. Headcount went from 28 to 25, and all nine engineers stayed.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Don't answer in anger, but don't accept the offer as it stands either. Have a lawyer review the contract and learn your termination and compensation rights; do this not to file a suit, but to know what you stand on at the table.
  • Write down the cards you hold: the service history of 410 installed devices, nine trained engineers, work-experience certificates from public tenders, a 12 million TL parts stock, relationships with hospitals. The manufacturer can't build any of these in nine months.

2This week

  • Prepare your counteroffer. Turn the service partnership from a one-sentence proposal into something concrete: term, territory, price, parts supply and exclusivity in writing. Ask for a contract of at least five years.
  • Don't exit sales entirely. Ask for a regional dealership for the area where you are strongest and a commission above 6 percent; also make buyback of your stock a condition.
  • Talk to your key employees. While setting up its own office, the first people the manufacturer will call are your sales and service team.
  • Start looking for a second brand that same week. A distributorship in a product group that doesn't compete with the manufacturer increases both revenue and bargaining power.

3Lasting fix

  • Put a ceiling on any single manufacturer's share of revenue (for example 40 percent). A distributorship is like doing business in a rented shop; one day the landlord may want to run the shop himself.
  • Grow the lasting part of your income: service contracts, consumables, training. Device sales belong to the manufacturer; the relationship with the hospital belongs to you.
  • Have distributorship contracts include at least 18 months' notice of termination, stock buyback and post-termination service rights.
The lesson

A distributorship is a brand held in trust; what lasts is the service network you built with that brand and the trust at the hospital.
