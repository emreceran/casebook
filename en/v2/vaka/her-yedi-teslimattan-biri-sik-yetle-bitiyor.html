One in every seven deliveries ends in a complaint

Ardıç Mobilya Furniture · Kayseri · 160 employees · 64 dealers, 1,500 deliveries a month
The problem

Of 1,500 deliveries a month, 210 draw complaints about missing parts, damage or unfinished assembly. The factory blames the dealer, and the dealer blames the factory.

How it was solved

It sorted the complaints one by one and found that missing parts came from the factory and unfinished assemblies from the crews. With scale-checked packing and photo-documented delivery, the rate fell from 14% to 5%.

What happened?

According to Ardıç Mobilya's call records, 210 of the 1,500 deliveries made in September ended in a complaint, a rate of 14%. Of the complaints, 92 were about missing parts, 67 about damaged products and 51 about assembly left unfinished. Each complaint costs an average of 3,200 TL once the second visit, new parts and shipping are counted; that comes to 672,000 TL a month.

The product goes from the factory to the dealer, and the delivery to the home and the assembly are done by crews the dealer has contracted. Production and Shipping Manager Halil Bozkurt says the problem lies there: the crews are paid per piece, they rush, and when something turns out to be missing they say, “the factory didn't send it.”

Sales and Dealer Manager Fatma Görgülü doesn't share that view. In photos from dealers, the hardware bag turns up missing even from boxes that were never opened. There is no record showing who is right, because nobody takes a photo of the box when it leaves the factory or when it enters the home.

By the numbers

1,500 deliveries a month, 210 complaints (14%)
92 missing parts, 67 damaged products, 51 unfinished assemblies
Average cost per complaint: 3,200 TL
Total monthly cost: 672,000 TL

What did Ardıç Mobilya do?

Halil Bozkurt and Fatma Görgülü sorted the 210 complaints together. Each turned out to be partly right: 71 of the 92 missing-part complaints came from hardware bags counted by hand on the night shift; 48 of the 67 damage cases involved shipments that passed through two transfer warehouses. Most of the unfinished assemblies involved crews sent to five addresses a day.

A scale was installed on the packing line, direct shipping with corner protectors was introduced on two distant routes, and assembly vehicles were given spare-part kits. 20% of the assembly fee was tied to the photo-documented delivery receipt. Three months later, complaints fell to 75 out of 1,500 deliveries, a rate of 5%; the monthly cost dropped from 672,000 TL to 240,000 TL.

What to do in a similar situation, step by step

1First 24 hours

  • Drop the argument and break down the 210 complaints one by one: which product, which shift packed it, which warehouse it passed through, which crew assembled it. Look for a repeating pattern, not a culprit.
  • Starting today, require two photos: when the box leaves the factory and when it is opened in the customer's home. After a week, there will be nothing left to argue about.

2This week

  • For missing-part complaints, go down to the packing line. Instead of counting the hardware bag by eye, check it with a scale; a bag missing a single screw shows in the grams.
  • Look at which routes the damaged products traveled. If damage is concentrated in transfer warehouses, try corner protectors and direct shipping on those routes.
  • Put a spare hardware kit in the assembly crews' vehicles. A second visit for a single screw costs 3,200 TL, while the kit costs a few hundred lira.
  • Tie part of the assembly fee to the delivery receipt signed by the customer, so the payment isn't finished before the job is.

3Lasting fix

  • Publish the complaint rate by dealer, product and shift every month. A team that sees its own number corrects itself.
  • Train the assembly crews at the factory twice a year, and release the assembly video for each new model on the same day as the product.
  • Call the customer the day after delivery. Hear about the complaint before the customer has to write it.
The lesson

Customers remember the furniture as if they bought it not from the factory but from the crew that walked into their home.
